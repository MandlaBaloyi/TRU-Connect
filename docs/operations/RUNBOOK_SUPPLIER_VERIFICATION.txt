===========================================================================
RUNBOOK — SUPPLIER VERIFICATION (THE CURTAIN)
===========================================================================

Written 2026-09-11. An OPERATIONS document -- it tells you what to type.
The reasoning is V2_12; the architecture is V2_03 Part 4.

WHAT THIS CONTROLS. Whether a supplier can be SEEN by businesses.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  A supplier builds EVERYTHING and sells NOTHING until verified.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

An unverified supplier can sign up, complete their profile, import a
three-thousand-line catalogue, set their terms and invite connections.
No business can find them, open their storefront, or order from them.
The day you approve them, the whole shopfront turns on at once.

WHAT IT DOES NOT TOUCH. Their catalogue, prices, inventory, connections
and past orders all survive a revocation untouched. Revoking stops
FUTURE trade; it does not erase trade that already happened, and a
business's order history keeps showing orders from a supplier who was
live at the time.


===========================================================================
1. SETUP
===========================================================================

From `backend/`, with the virtualenv active. PowerShell:

    .venv\Scripts\Activate.ps1
    $env:FLASK_APP = "app.py"

bash / the production server:

    source .venv/bin/activate
    export FLASK_APP=app.py

Database and secrets are read from backend/.env automatically.

YOU NEED AN ADMIN ACCOUNT. The write commands ask you to sign in and
refuse anyone whose role is not ADMIN. If you have not made one yet,
see RUNBOOK_CATALOGUE_REVIEW.txt section 1 -- the same admin works for
both queues.


===========================================================================
2. LOOKING
===========================================================================

Who is waiting, newest first:

    flask verification pending
    flask verification pending --limit 100

One supplier, by EMAIL or by user id:

    flask verification status owner@example.com
    flask verification status 0e27415a-ea26-45fe-8a6b-6815ec636752

The line that matters in that output is `buyers see them: YES / NO`.
Everything else is context.

BOTH COMMANDS ARE READ-ONLY and ask for no password. Safe to run
against production while you think.


===========================================================================
3. DECIDING
===========================================================================

    flask verification approve <email|id> -r "what you checked"
    flask verification revoke  <email|id> -r "why"

Each one confirms first, then asks for YOUR ADMIN EMAIL and password --
not the supplier's. The screen just above the prompt names the supplier
being acted on; the prompt is asking who is DECIDING.

THE REASON IS NOT OPTIONAL. Leave `-r` off and it will ask. A record
saying a supplier was verified by somebody for reasons nobody wrote
down is worthless in three months, and three months is exactly when it
gets asked about. Write what you actually checked:

    -r "CIPC 2019/123456/07 confirmed, director ID matched, 2026-09-11"
    -r "Partnership agreement signed; verified during contracting"
    -r "Documents expired, asked for re-submission 2026-09-11"

Every decision is written to the audit trail with your user id and the
time.


===========================================================================
4. WHAT TO CHECK BEFORE APPROVING
===========================================================================

PHASE 1 HOLDS NO DOCUMENTS. There is no upload, no queue of scanned
certificates, nothing to click through. You verify a supplier
OUT-OF-BAND -- a certificate photographed and sent over WhatsApp, a
phone call, a company you have actually visited -- and then record the
outcome here. The document pipeline is V2_12 Phase 2.

THE QUESTION TO ASK IS NOT "do these documents look real". It is:

    IS A BUSINESS ABOUT TO HAND MONEY TO SOMEBODY WE CANNOT IDENTIFY?

Minimum for approval today:

  · a real, contactable person behind the account
  · a business name that matches what they trade as
  · EITHER a CIPC registration number you have checked, OR an ID plus
    a declared trading name for an unregistered trader

AN UNREGISTERED TRADER IS NOT A REJECTION. Most of the informal supply
chain is not CIPC-registered, and even Takealot does not require it. An
unregistered trader who gives you a real, checkable identity is more
accountable than an anonymous one. See V2_12 Part 3 (Tier 1, path b).

WHEN IN DOUBT, DO NOT APPROVE. An unapproved supplier is invisible,
which is a safe state -- exactly how every supplier behaved before any
of this existed. A wrongly approved one is a stranger taking a shop
owner's money with TruConnect's name on the introduction.

VERIFICATION SAYS NOTHING ABOUT WHAT THEY SELL. That is the other gate
entirely -- the catalogue review queue, RUNBOOK_CATALOGUE_REVIEW.txt.
A supplier can be completely genuine and still list something that must
not be sold through this platform.


===========================================================================
5. USING IT TO TEST THE APP
===========================================================================

The supplier app shows a banner across the top of EVERY supplier screen
when that supplier is hidden:

    "Your shop is ready — buyers cannot see it yet."

To see it against a real backend, hide a supplier, look, then put them
back:

    flask verification revoke you@example.com -r "testing the banner"
    ... open the app, pull to refresh ...
    flask verification approve you@example.com -r "testing done"

THE APP CACHES THE STATUS FOR FIVE MINUTES, so it will not flip the
instant you run the command. Pull to refresh, or restart the app.

REMEMBER THE SUPPLIER IS GENUINELY HIDDEN while revoked -- they will not
appear in any business's search and their storefront returns
not-found. On a dev database that is harmless. Do not do it on
production to test something.

IN MOCK MODE (EXPO_PUBLIC_USE_MOCK_API is not "false") the app does not
call the backend at all and always shows the banner, because the mock
returns the hidden state deliberately -- a banner that never appears in
mock mode is one nobody notices is broken.


===========================================================================
6. WHAT THIS IS NOT
===========================================================================

NOT A DOCUMENT REVIEW SYSTEM. Phase 2 adds intake, storage and an
audited queue, with documents deleted after the decision. Until then
every approval rests on something you checked yourself, elsewhere.

NOT A CONTENT CHECK. See section 4.

DO NOT EDIT verification_status WITH SQL. Not because it would fail --
it would work -- but because the decision, the reason, the actor and
the timestamp have to move together, and "who approved this supplier,
and on what grounds" is precisely the question asked after something
goes wrong.

===========================================================================

    flask verification pending                    who is waiting
    flask verification pending --limit 100        more of them
    flask verification status <email|id>          one supplier

    flask verification revoke  <email|id> -r "…"  close it again

    flask admin list                              who may decide
    flask catalogue reviews                       the OTHER gate
