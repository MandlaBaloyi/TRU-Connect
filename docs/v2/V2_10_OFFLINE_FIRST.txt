===========================================================================
V2_10 — OFFLINE-FIRST FOR THE BUSINESS TOOLS
===========================================================================

Written 2026-09-11. Depends on V2_03 (especially Part 1, tools stand
alone, and Part 3.2, the three ledgers).

The requirement, stated plainly: an informal business cannot be stopped
from recording a credit sale because they have no data. The Loan Book
and the Order Book have to work with the phone in aeroplane mode, and
catch up later.


===========================================================================
PART 1 — WE HAVE ALREADY PROMISED THIS, AND WE DO NOT DO IT
===========================================================================

This is not a new feature request. The app currently SAYS it works
offline. From OfflineModeScreen, shipped, on a real settings screen a
real user can read today:

    "Your business doesn't stop for a bad signal. TruConnect keeps
     working when your connection drops. Record a sale, log a payment,
     or update an order the same way you always do -- nothing about how
     the app works changes because you're offline."

    "Everything catches up automatically. Once your device reconnects,
     everything you recorded while offline syncs on its own."

    "Why this isn't a switch: working offline is built into how
     TruConnect is designed, not a feature you turn on... it's simply
     always on, so your business records are never at the mercy of your
     signal."

WHAT IS ACTUALLY INSTALLED: @react-native-async-storage/async-storage
and @tanstack/react-query. No SQLite. No persisted query cache. No
mutation queue. No connectivity listener. No sync.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  A shop owner who reads that screen, trusts it, and records four
  credit sales on a bad signal will lose them. The screen does not say
  "coming soon". It says it is always on.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

That makes this the most serious instance of the problem V2_06 Part 5.1
already names -- screens that promise something unbuilt -- and it is
worse than a dead toggle, because the thing being promised is the
survival of someone's records of who owes them money.

TWO HONEST OPTIONS, and only two:

  A. Build it. This document.
  B. Change the words until it is built.

Not doing either is the only unacceptable outcome. And because B costs
ten minutes, B should happen TODAY regardless of when A lands.


===========================================================================
PART 2 — WHAT CAN BE OFFLINE, AND WHAT CANNOT
===========================================================================

The line is not "everything should work offline". Some things genuinely
cannot, and pretending otherwise produces a worse failure than being
online-only.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  THE SAME LINE THAT DECIDES WHAT IS PROVABLE DECIDES WHAT CAN BE
  OFFLINE.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

V2_03 Part 3.2 split the three ledgers by whether the platform observed
them. That split turns out to answer this question too, exactly:

  SELF-REPORTED -> OFFLINE-FIRST
    Loan Book      who owes me money
    Order Book     what my own customers ordered
    Cash Up        my three numbers at closing

    These are the merchant's own records about their own customers. The
    server is a BACKUP and a second device's copy, not an authority. We
    do not verify a line of any of them (V2_03 3.3), so there is
    nothing the network adds at the moment of writing. The device can
    be the source of truth and the server can catch up.

  PLATFORM-OBSERVED -> ONLINE-ONLY
    Stock Orders   buying from a supplier

    You cannot place an order against a price you cannot see and stock
    you cannot check. Queueing the INTENT to order is worse than
    refusing: the price may have changed, the item may be gone, and the
    supplier finds an order they never had a chance to decline. A
    business that believes stock is coming and it is not has been
    harmed more than one told "you need signal for this".

    WHAT WE DO INSTEAD: the cart persists locally, and submitting is
    what needs a connection. Nothing typed is lost; only the commitment
    waits.

  READ SURFACES -> CACHED, AND HONESTLY LABELLED
    Community feed, Discovery, a supplier's catalogue

    Show the last copy, with the date it was fetched. A price from
    Tuesday shown as though it were today's is the same lie as an
    aspirational number on a card.


===========================================================================
PART 3 — THE DATA MODEL ALREADY SUPPORTS THIS, MOSTLY BY ACCIDENT
===========================================================================

Three properties already true, and each one is a thing that would
otherwise have to be retrofitted painfully:

  UUID PRIMARY KEYS, GENERATED IN PYTHON
    BaseModel: `id = db.Column(UUID, primary_key=True, default=uuid.uuid4)`.
    Not a database sequence, not an autoincrement.

    THIS IS THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT ONE. It means the DEVICE can mint
    the id at the moment the shop owner taps save, offline. Sync then
    becomes INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING and is idempotent by
    construction -- replaying the same queued entry ten times produces
    one row. With server-generated ids, offline creation needs a
    temporary-id remapping layer, which is where most offline systems
    acquire their worst bugs.

  AN APPEND-ONLY EVENT LOG ALREADY EXISTS
    LoanHistoryEvent -- event_type, amount_cents, occurred_at,
    metadata JSONB. It was built as an audit trail. It is also exactly
    the shape sync wants, because immutable facts merge without
    conflict resolution: two devices that each recorded three events
    produce six events, and no rule is needed to decide who wins.

  MONEY IS INTEGER CENTS EVERYWHERE
    No float drift when a total is recomputed on a device rather than
    the server.

WHAT DOES NOT FIT, AND NEEDS A DECISION:

  LoanEntry.is_settled and settled_at are STORED STATE on a mutable
  row. Settled is derivable -- sum(payments) >= original_amount -- and
  a derived value that is also stored is a value that can disagree with
  itself. Offline makes that likely rather than theoretical: a device
  records a payment, another device records a different one, both
  recompute, and the stored flag now depends on merge order.

  RECOMMENDATION: treat settled as DERIVED at read time. Keep the
  column if something depends on it, but recompute rather than trust.


===========================================================================
PART 4 — THE DESIGN
===========================================================================

4.1 LOCAL STORE: SQLITE, NOT ASYNCSTORAGE

  AsyncStorage is a key-value store. A loan book in AsyncStorage is one
  JSON blob, rewritten in full on every entry, unqueryable, and
  corruptible in a way that loses everything rather than one row. It is
  the right tool for "which tab was open", which is what it is used for
  today.

  expo-sqlite gives real tables, real queries and atomic writes. A
  merchant with 400 customers and two years of entries is a database,
  and it should be one.

4.2 THE OUTBOX

  Every local write does two things atomically: apply it to the local
  tables, and append it to an OUTBOX table.

  The outbox is a queue of intents with their client-generated ids. A
  connectivity listener drains it. Each item is retried until the
  server acknowledges, and because ids are client-generated, retrying
  is free.

  IT IS NOT "SAVE LOCALLY THEN REFETCH". The local write IS the write.
  The server copy is a replica that catches up.

4.3 SYNC IS PER-DOMAIN, NOT GLOBAL

  Loan Book syncs independently of Order Book. A failure in one must
  not stall the other, and a schema change to one must not require a
  version bump of the whole protocol.

4.4 WHAT THE USER SEES

  Almost nothing, and that is the design. No "offline mode" banner
  shouting at someone who is simply in a shop with thick walls. A small
  unobtrusive indicator that N entries are waiting to sync, and a
  timestamp on cached read surfaces. That is all.

  The screen text quoted in Part 1 is right about this: it should not
  be a switch.


===========================================================================
PART 5 — THE HARD PARTS, NAMED BEFORE THEY BITE
===========================================================================

  5.1 AUTHENTICATION IS THE TRAP THAT BREAKS OFFLINE MODE

    An access token expires in 15 minutes. A device offline for two
    days cannot refresh it. If recording a loan entry checks "is my
    session valid" first, the feature is dead exactly when it is needed.

    THE RULE: LOCAL WRITES MUST NEVER BE GATED ON SESSION VALIDITY.
    The tools are local-first; authentication is a SYNC concern. A shop
    owner records sales for a week with an expired token, signs in when
    they next have signal, and everything uploads.

    This also makes the tools-stand-alone principle real (V2_03 Part 1)
    rather than aspirational: a Loan Book that demands a live session
    is not standing alone.

  5.2 THE DEVICE CLOCK IS NOT TRUSTWORTHY

    Phones have wrong dates. A sale recorded "yesterday" by a device
    set to 2019 sorts into the wrong place forever.

    Record BOTH: the device's time as the business fact ("when the sale
    happened", which the merchant can correct), and the server's
    received-at as the ordering fact. Never sort across devices by
    device time.

  5.3 TWO DEVICES, ONE SHOP

    An owner and an employee both recording. Rare, real, and the reason
    append-only matters: immutable entries merge by union. Mutable rows
    need last-write-wins, and last-write-wins on money silently deletes
    somebody's record of a payment.

    Deletes need TOMBSTONES -- a delete is itself an event, not the
    absence of one, or a row deleted on one device reappears from the
    other on the next sync. The existing Trash feature makes this
    concrete rather than hypothetical.

  5.4 UNSYNCED DATA ON A LOST PHONE IS GONE

    Honest and unavoidable. It argues for syncing eagerly whenever
    there is any signal, and for the pending-count indicator in 4.4
    being visible enough that a week of unsynced entries is noticed.

  5.5 MIGRATIONS ON THE DEVICE

    The local schema will change. A phone that skipped four app
    versions needs its local database migrated on launch, and a failed
    migration must not discard data. This is a real cost of local-first
    and it is paid forever.

===========================================================================
PART 7 — WHY THIS IS WORTH THE COST
===========================================================================

Local-first is genuinely more work than it looks: two schemas, a sync
protocol, device migrations, and a class of bug that only appears on
other people's phones.

It is still right here, for one reason that is specific to this market
rather than general good practice. The research behind V2_03 found that
successful apps in this market are "deliberately light and work on weak
connections, holding actions on the device until signal returns" -- and
that a third of South Africa's food spend moves through shops that are
largely cash and largely unrecorded.

The whole retention argument (V2_03 Part 3) is that the merchant's own
records live here. A record-keeping tool that stops recording when the
signal drops is not a record-keeping tool. It is a website.
