===========================================================================
V2_08 — THE THIRD-PARTY LAYER
       VAS float · delivery brokerage · WhatsApp · group buying
===========================================================================

Written 2026-09-09, out of the V2_03 review. Depends on V2_03, especially
Principle 1 (asset-light, but brokering is allowed) and Part 3.3 (only
digital transactions are provable).

THE IDEA THIS DOCUMENT EXISTS TO SERVE, in the reviewer's words: we
cannot limit ourselves to wiring CRUD business tools. A supplier with no
vehicle should be able to reach a delivery partner through us. A business
that resells airtime should be able to buy that airtime here.

THE RULE THAT KEEPS IT SAFE: we ARRANGE, a licensed partner PERFORMS. We
never own the truck, never hold the float, never carry the credit risk.
That is precisely how the survivors of the 2022–24 B2B collapse made
margin without the balance sheet that killed the rest.


===========================================================================
PART 1 — VAS FLOAT: THE BEST-FITTING IDEA IN THIS DOCUMENT
===========================================================================

1.1 THE REFRAME, WHICH IS WHAT MAKES IT WORK

  The obvious version of "TruConnect does airtime" is that we become a
  vending provider competing with Kazang and Flash. That version is bad:
  it is licensed, float-managed, hardware-adjacent, and we would be
  entering against incumbents running ~90,000 devices and ~3 million
  transactions a day.

  The review's version is different and much better:

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  The merchant who RESELLS airtime has to BUY it somewhere.
  A VAS distributor is simply a supplier whose products are digital.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  We are not selling airtime to consumers. We are selling the merchant
  their stock — the same thing we already do with maize meal. The
  product happens to have no weight.

1.2 WHY IT FITS THE ARCHITECTURE ALMOST EXACTLY

  A VAS distributor onboards as a SUPPLIER. They have a catalogue
  (R5/R10/R29 airtime, data bundles, electricity, DStv, gaming
  vouchers), they have prices, they have terms. Every mechanism the
  platform already has applies:

    supplier_offers          the bundles
    catalogue products       matched and comparable across distributors
    stock_orders             a purchase
    payment path             DIGITAL ONLY, necessarily — and per V2_03
                             Part 3.3 that means every VAS purchase is
                             a PROVEN transaction, which makes this the
                             highest-quality data in the entire product
    connections              the distributor's own approval rules

  What differs is FULFILMENT. A crate of cooldrink is delivered. A
  bundle is issued — a voucher code, a PIN, or a balance credited to the
  merchant's existing device.

  So the only genuinely new concept is a DIGITAL FULFILMENT PATH on
  stock orders: instead of "expected delivery date", the order resolves
  to an issued credential, immediately, and the app shows it.

1.3 THE ECONOMICS, FROM RESEARCH

    Airtime and data        ~3–5% commission to the reseller
    Electricity             ~1.5–3%
    Bill payments (DStv,
      municipal)            ~1–2.5%
                       — Flash merchant documentation, 2025/26

  Thin, but on enormous, daily, repeating volume. And it is the income
  line that brings a merchant into the app every single day rather than
  weekly — airtime sells out constantly.

  FLOAT IS THE REAL CONSTRAINT. Resellers must "ensure sufficient
  working capital to maintain float". That is exactly the working-capital
  problem in V2_03 Part 3.3, and it is where a financing partner would
  eventually attach. Not now.

1.4 THE HARD PARTS, STATED HONESTLY

  · WE MUST NOT HOLD THE FLOAT. The moment TruConnect holds merchant
    balances we are running a deposit-taking business, which is a
    licence and a regulator. The design must be: merchant pays the
    distributor, distributor credits the merchant's device or issues the
    voucher. We orchestrate and record.

  · "AUTOMATICALLY LOADED ONTO YOUR PoS" IS A PARTNER DECISION, NOT A
    BUILD. Whether a purchase can credit a Kazang or Flash device
    directly depends entirely on whether that provider exposes an API
    for it. This needs a conversation before it needs a sprint. The
    fallback — issue a voucher/PIN the merchant loads themselves — works
    with no integration at all and should be the v1.

  · AGGREGATOR VERSUS DIRECT. Reaching MTN, Vodacom, Cell C, Telkom and
    Eskom individually is many integrations. Aggregators exist for
    exactly this. Choosing one is a real evaluation and it is not this
    document's job.

1.5 WHAT TO DO ABOUT IT NOW

  Nothing to build. One thing to decide: whether to approach a VAS
  distributor or aggregator as a SUPPLIER on the platform. That is a
  business-development conversation, and if it lands, most of the
  product already exists.


===========================================================================
PART 2 — DELIVERY BROKERAGE
===========================================================================

2.1 THE PROBLEM IT SOLVES

  SupplierProfile.offers_delivery is a boolean today. A supplier with no
  vehicle sets it false and silently loses every business that cannot
  collect — which, for a shop owner who cannot leave their counter, is
  most of them. The supplier is not short of demand; they are short of a
  bakkie.

2.2 THE MARKET, RESEARCHED

    Bob Go        aggregator over 10+ couriers (The Courier Guy, Pargo,
                  RAM, SkyNet, Internet Express). ~R299–R699/month plus
                  ~R3–R8 per shipment. Built for exactly this problem.
    Droppa        a documented developer portal; SWIFT (pickup within
                  60 min, ≤7kg, ≤15km) and FLASHROAD same-day
                  nationwide.
    Pargo         pickup-point network (Pep, Postnet and partners),
                  R0 base plus per-shipment. Collect-point rather than
                  door delivery.

  PARGO IS WORTH A SECOND LOOK for this market specifically. Pep stores
  are everywhere a spaza is, collection is a habit already, and a
  pickup-point model removes the "nobody was at the shop" failure that
  kills door delivery to a trader who is out buying stock.

2.3 THE DESIGN

  A third fulfilment option beside delivery and collection:

    COLLECTION           the business collects. Exists.
    SUPPLIER DELIVERY    the supplier's own vehicle. Exists.
    PARTNER DELIVERY     new. A courier quoted at checkout, booked on
                         approval, tracked on the order.

  Behind a PROVIDER ABSTRACTION, exactly as media (Cloudinary), payment
  (PayFast), geocoding (Mapbox) and AI (Claude) already are. A
  DeliveryProvider interface with quote() and book(); Bob Go or Droppa
  is one implementation. No service imports a courier by name. This is
  the fifth time this pattern pays for itself.

2.4 THE HONEST WARNINGS

  · A QUOTE IS A COMMITMENT. Showing a delivery price at checkout means
    standing behind it. Quote failures, address failures and size
    failures all need an answer before this ships.

  · IT IS NOT ASSET-LIGHT IF WE UNDERWRITE IT. If TruConnect covers the
    difference when a courier overcharges, we have quietly taken on the
    logistics risk that bankrupted the cohort in V2_03 Part 2. The
    supplier's price is the courier's price.

  · WHO PAYS FOR A FAILED DELIVERY is the question that decides whether
    this is a feature or a support department. Answer it in the design,
    not in production.

PART 3 — WHATSAPP AS THE COMMUNICATION LAYER
===========================================================================

3.1 THE REFRAME, AGAIN A GOOD ONE

  V2_07 Part 5 treats WhatsApp as a possible ORDERING channel and a
  strategic threat to our form factor. That remains true and unresolved.

  But the review pointed at something available immediately and far
  cheaper: use WhatsApp as the NOTIFICATION layer for orders placed in
  the app. Order confirmation, reference, supplier, expected delivery
  date — pushed to the channel this market actually reads, without
  asking anyone to change how they work.

3.2 THE ECONOMICS, RESEARCHED — AND THEY ARE GOOD

    Utility templates (order confirmations, delivery updates)
      ~$0.0076 per message + 15% VAT ≈ R0.14–R0.16
    Marketing templates
      ~$0.0379 ≈ R0.70–R1.15 — five times the price
    Volume discounts up to ~25% on utility and authentication.
                       — WhatsApp Business Platform pricing, SA, 2026

  At R0.15, ten thousand order confirmations cost about R1,500. This is
  affordable at our scale today, which is not something that can be said
  about most things in this document.

  ONE DATE TO KNOW: service messages and utility messages inside the
  24-hour customer service window are free today, and BOTH BECOME
  CHARGEABLE FROM 1 OCTOBER 2026. That is three weeks away. It does not
  change the plan — utility templates outside the window are already
  priced as above — but any cost modelling done before that date will be
  wrong after it.

3.3 WHAT TO SEND, AND WHAT NEVER TO SEND

  SEND — utility, transactional, expected:
    order placed, with reference and supplier name
    order approved, with the expected delivery date
    out for delivery / ready for collection
    payment received
    verification approved (supplier side)

  NEVER SEND — marketing, at five times the price and a fraction of the
  welcome:
    promotions, "we miss you", supplier advertising, feed digests.
    A merchant who gets marketing on WhatsApp blocks the number, and
    then never receives their delivery confirmations either. The
    channel's value is precisely that everything on it matters.

3.4 THE ARCHITECTURE

  It is a NotificationProvider, and one already exists —
  src/shared/notifications with a queue and templates behind it. This is
  a new channel next to email and in-app, not a new system.

    src/shared/notifications/providers/whatsapp_provider.py

  Template approval by Meta is the long pole, and templates are
  per-language. isiZulu and isiXhosa versions of an order confirmation
  are a genuine kindness here and cost the same to send.

  CONSENT IS NOT OPTIONAL. Opt-in at sign-up, and a real off switch —
  the settings screens (notifications-orders, notifications-community)
  already exist and this is another row in them, not a new concept.

PART 4 — GROUP BUYING / STOKVEL ORDERING
===========================================================================

4.1 THE SCALE

  ~800,000 stokvels, ~11 million members, >R50 billion in assets — the
  largest formalised non-bank savings movement on the continent. Groups
  are typically 5–30 people, contributing monthly. Grocery stokvels
  already buy in bulk at cash-and-carry wholesalers at 20–30% below
  retail, and Shoprite and Massmart both run dedicated stokvel
  programmes.
                       — Ipsos 2024; WSCC; Bizcommunity

  This is not a behaviour we would be introducing. It is one we would be
  joining, late, against retailers who already court it.

4.2 THE FINDING THAT MAKES IT BUILDABLE

  The first draft rejected group buying as "operationally heavy — needs
  multi-party payment, allocation and dispute handling". The research
  found the model that removes the worst of it:

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  "Some stokvels save directly with the wholesaler, who then records
   the contributions and makes stock available for collection."
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  THE SUPPLIER HOLDS THE MONEY, NOT US. That single fact eliminates the
  reason group buying looked dangerous: TruConnect never becomes an
  escrow, never allocates pooled funds, and never has to adjudicate who
  gets what when a member drops out. We orchestrate a group order
  against a supplier who already runs this arrangement offline.

  It also fits Principle 1 exactly. We arrange; the supplier performs.

4.3 THE SHAPE OF A DESIGN (SKETCH ONLY — NEEDS ITS OWN DOCUMENT)

    A GROUP is a named set of businesses (or individuals) with one
    ORGANISER. Members are invited by the existing invitation flow.

    A GROUP ORDER is a basket with a target, a deadline and a supplier.
    Members add lines or contributions. The organiser submits.

    CONTRIBUTION TRACKING IS A LEDGER, NOT AN ACCOUNT. We record what
    each member has paid the supplier and what remains. The money moves
    between member and supplier. Ours is a record, exactly as Loan Book
    is a record.

    ALLOCATION is the organiser's, recorded for everyone to see. The
    single most valuable thing software adds to a stokvel is that
    everybody can see the same numbers — most disputes in these groups
    are bookkeeping disputes.

4.4 WHY IT IS STILL NOT MVP

  It needs its own document, a real conversation with two or three
  actual stokvels, and a supplier willing to run the arrangement. It is
  the largest opportunity in this file and the one most likely to be got
  wrong by guessing. A group-buy feature that leaves a member out of
  pocket destroys more trust than the feature could ever earn.

  WHAT TO DO NEXT: talk to three stokvels before writing another line
  about them.


===========================================================================
PART 5 — HOW THESE RANK
===========================================================================

  Judged on (value delivered) ÷ (cost and risk to build).

  1  WHATSAPP NOTIFICATIONS      Cheapest, fastest, uses infrastructure
                                 that exists, reaches the channel this
                                 market already reads. Do this first of
                                 the four — plausibly right after MVP.

  2  VAS FLOAT                   Best architectural fit, daily
                                 engagement, proven transactions. Gated
                                 on a partner conversation, not on
                                 engineering.

  3  DELIVERY BROKERAGE          Real problem, real APIs, moderate
                                 build. Gated on answering "who pays for
                                 a failed delivery".

  4  GROUP BUYING                Biggest prize, most research still
                                 owed, needs its own document.

  NONE OF THESE ARE MVP (V2_07). All four are the answer to "what is
  TruConnect in three years", and none of them require us to own a
  warehouse, a truck, or a lending book.
