===========================================================================
V2_14 — ORDER FULFILMENT, AND WHO CARRIES THE RISK
===========================================================================

Written 2026-09-14. A PLAN, not a decision. Part 7 lists what only
Mandla can answer; nothing here gets built before those are settled.

It exists because building D (My Connections as a relationship ledger,
V2_04 2.3) needed to show "an outstanding order, if one is open" and
there turned out to be no honest way to compute it.


===========================================================================
PART 0 — THE PROBLEM, IN ONE PAGE
===========================================================================

An order today walks one of two paths and both stop in the same place.

    DIRECT (off-platform — notes, EFT, a deposit, a transfer)

        cart ──▶ PENDING_SUPPLIER_APPROVAL ──▶ CONFIRMED ──▶ ·
                    supplier decides                          nothing

    PAYFAST (the observed rail)

        cart ──▶ AWAITING_PAYMENT ─────────▶ CONFIRMED ──▶ ·
                    money reaches the                        nothing
                    provider; verified,
                    no approval needed

NOTHING EVER MOVES AN ORDER OUT OF CONFIRMED. There is no DELIVERED
status, no delivered_at, no received_at. The model carries delivery
DETAILS — delivery_type, delivery_address_text — and no delivery
OUTCOME.

So CONFIRMED is doing two jobs at once and they are not the same job:

    what it is used as     "the successful order" — sales totals,
                           trust stats, repeat-business counts
    what it actually means "paid or approved; the goods have not been
                           accounted for"

The stock arrives in real life. TruConnect never finds out.

0.1  THE THREE THINGS THIS BREAKS

  A BADGE THAT CAN NEVER CLEAR. "Is there an open order with this
  supplier" has no correct answer. Count CONFIRMED and every business
  who has ever paid shows an outstanding order forever. Exclude it and
  somebody who paid this morning and is waiting for bread sees nothing.
  D currently excludes it, which is the quieter wrong answer, not a
  right one.

  RECOURSE HAS NOTHING TO STAND ON. The whole argument for the digital
  rail is that a business who pays and receives nothing has proof and
  the platform can act. But there is no field in this system that could
  ever record that the goods did not arrive. TruConnect cannot today
  tell a completed trade from an abandoned one — on either rail.

  THE REORDER RHYTHM IS MEASURED FROM THE WRONG DATE. reorder_gap reads
  submitted_at: when they ORDERED. What actually sets the next order is
  when the stock ARRIVED and started selling. Two suppliers, one
  same-day and one three days out, produce rhythms wrong by exactly that
  difference — and the whole point of the ledger is telling a supplier
  who is due.


===========================================================================
PART 1 — THE RULE THAT DECIDES THE DESIGN: LIABILITY FOLLOWS THE MONEY
===========================================================================

This is the constraint everything else hangs off, and it is Mandla's,
stated 2026-09-14.

  ON THE DIRECT RAIL WE HOLD NOTHING. The money never touched us. We
  did not see it move, we cannot prove it moved, and we cannot prove
  what was promised. The model already says this in
  StockOrderPaymentMethod's own docstring: "TruConnect observes none of
  them, and that, not the instrument, is what makes them one thing."
  Both parties carry that risk and must know they carry it.

  ON THE PAYFAST RAIL WE HOLD THE EVIDENCE. There is a provider record:
  an amount, a timestamp, two identified parties. If a business pays
  and the supplier does not deliver, that is a thing we can establish
  and therefore a thing we can act on — suspend, revoke verification,
  remove from the curtain.

  THEREFORE: ONE STATE MACHINE, TWO DIFFERENT CONSEQUENCES. The states
  below are identical on both rails, because the rhythm and the badge
  need them on both. What differs is what a FAILURE TO COMPLETE
  entitles anyone to. Non-delivery on PayFast opens a case. Non-delivery
  on DIRECT opens a record and nothing more.

  This is not timidity. A platform that promises protection it cannot
  deliver is worse than one that is clear about where its protection
  ends — the promise is discovered to be empty at the exact moment
  somebody is already out of pocket.

1.1  A CORRECTION THAT CHANGES THE MITIGATION STORY

  It is tempting to say the connection requirement is what protects a
  cash buyer — connect first, so you only trade with somebody you have
  a relationship with.

  THAT IS NO LONGER TRUE BY DEFAULT. V2_00 2.3 changed the ordering
  gate from mandatory to OPT-IN: SupplierProfile.requires_connection
  defaults to FALSE, and the reasoning was sound — order accept/decline
  is a strictly better control, per transaction, with the supplier
  seeing what is actually being ordered.

  So on the DIRECT rail today the real mitigations are: the supplier's
  own decline, the per-transaction cap on direct payments (invisible
  money is what the caps exist to bound), and the verification curtain
  keeping unverified suppliers unreachable. Not the connection.

  WORTH DECIDING SEPARATELY (Part 7): whether requires_connection should
  default TRUE for suppliers who accept DIRECT payment. It is the one
  place where the old instinct and the new gate could be reconciled.


===========================================================================
PART 2 — THE STATES
===========================================================================

  ADD

    DISPATCHED     the supplier says it has left them.
                   dispatched_at.
                   Optional — see 3.1. Not every informal supplier
                   will ever tap this, and the design must not break
                   when they do not.

    COMPLETED      the trade is finished and nothing further is
                   expected. completed_at, plus completed_by
                   (BUSINESS | SUPPLIER | SYSTEM) — because "who said
                   so" is the entire value of the field.

    DISPUTED       the business says it did not arrive, or did not
                   arrive as ordered. disputed_at, reason.
                   REACHABLE ON BOTH RAILS, actionable on one.

  KEEP AS IS

    CART, AWAITING_PAYMENT, PENDING_SUPPLIER_APPROVAL, CONFIRMED,
    DECLINED, CANCELLED.

  WHAT "OPEN" THEN MEANS, and it becomes computable rather than argued
  about:

    open      = submitted, and not in a terminal state
    terminal  = DECLINED, CANCELLED, COMPLETED
                (DISPUTED is NOT terminal — it is the loudest possible
                 kind of open)

  NOTE WHAT THIS DOES TO EVERY EXISTING QUERY. Sales totals, trust
  stats, repeat-business counts and performance_stats_for_supplier all
  currently read status == CONFIRMED and mean "a real sale". Once
  CONFIRMED can move to COMPLETED, every one of those is silently wrong
  unless it is changed to read "CONFIRMED or beyond". That migration of
  MEANING is the largest and least visible part of this work, and it is
  the part most likely to be missed.


===========================================================================
PART 3 — WHO MARKS IT COMPLETE
===========================================================================

  THE SUPPLIER ALONE          They know when it left. But they are
                              already paid on the PayFast rail, it
                              flatters their own stats, and nobody is
                              checking — so the incentive points the
                              wrong way at exactly the moment the
                              record matters.

  THE BUSINESS ALONE          The honest signal, and the only one a
                              dispute can rest on. But a shopkeeper
                              unpacking stock at 6am is not going to
                              open an app, so orders sit open forever
                              and we are back where we started.

  BOTH, IN SEQUENCE           Supplier marks DISPATCHED; business
  + A TIMEOUT                 confirms received (COMPLETED); if neither
                              happens within N days, a sweep completes
  ← RECOMMENDED               it as SYSTEM.

  WHY THE TIMEOUT IS THE PART THAT MAKES IT WORK. It is what stops
  "open forever" without nagging anybody, and TruConnect already has
  this exact machinery: auto_decline_sweep_job closes orders a supplier
  never answered, on the same JobSpec contract. A completion sweep is
  the same shape.

  AND completed_by IS WHY IT IS SAFE. A SYSTEM completion is not
  evidence of anything and must never be treated as the business
  confirming receipt — it only means nobody objected in time. A
  BUSINESS completion is the strong signal. Collapsing the two into one
  boolean would destroy the only distinction that matters in a dispute.

  3.1  THE SUPPLIER WHO NEVER TAPS ANYTHING

  Most informal suppliers will not mark dispatch. The design must treat
  DISPATCHED as decoration, never as a precondition: a business can
  confirm receipt of an order that was never marked dispatched, and the
  sweep completes orders that were never touched at all. If DISPATCHED
  becomes required, suppliers who ignore it silently break their
  customers' rhythm data.


===========================================================================
PART 4 — WHAT IT UNBLOCKS
===========================================================================

  THE LEDGER BADGE BECOMES HONEST. D can then define open as
  "not terminal" and include CONFIRMED, which is what Mandla asked for:
  a business who paid this morning sees their order, and it clears when
  the trade finishes.

  THE RHYTHM GETS MEASURED FROM ARRIVAL. reorder_gap takes a list of
  dates; feeding it completed_at rather than submitted_at is a one-line
  change at the call site and makes "due to reorder" mean what it says.
  KEEP submitted_at AS THE FALLBACK for historical orders that have no
  completion — never silently drop them from the history.

  RECOURSE BECOMES POSSIBLE ON THE RAIL WHERE WE CAN ACT. A DISPUTED
  order with a PayFast payment record is a case with evidence attached.
  It is also the first thing the audit trail has been waiting for — the
  supplier-side consequence (suspend, revoke verification) already
  exists in the verification service.

  AND IT FEEDS THE ONE ASSET THAT COMPOUNDS. A completed order with a
  provider record is third-party-attested: two identified parties, an
  amount, a date, an outcome. That is the only class of data in this
  platform that could ever support a lending decision, and today it
  stops one step short of being a completed fact.


===========================================================================
PART 5 — WHAT MUST NOT HAPPEN
===========================================================================

  NEVER IMPLY PROTECTION ON A DIRECT ORDER. If the dispute flow looks
  identical on both rails, a business will reasonably believe paying
  cash through TruConnect is covered. The wording, and probably the
  affordance itself, has to differ — a DIRECT dispute records, it does
  not promise.

  NEVER LET A SUPPLIER SELF-CERTIFY DELIVERY AS PROOF. DISPATCHED is
  the supplier's claim about their own conduct. It is useful context
  and it is not evidence.

  NEVER TREAT A SYSTEM COMPLETION AS CONFIRMATION. See Part 3.

  DO NOT BUILD A REFUND MECHANISM HERE. TruConnect holds no funds — the
  money is at the provider, and anything resembling us returning it
  makes us a payment intermediary with the licensing that implies. The
  consequence we control is the supplier's standing on the platform,
  not the money.

PART 6 — THE WORK, IN ORDER
===========================================================================

  1  Migration: three enum values, dispatched_at, completed_at,
     completed_by, disputed_at, dispute_reason.
     ALTER TYPE ... ADD VALUE, no downgrade (Postgres cannot remove an
     enum value — same note as 4b7e1a05c9d2).

  2  Audit the meaning of CONFIRMED across every existing query
     (Part 2). This is the risky step and it comes before any new
     behaviour, not after.

  3  Service: mark_dispatched, confirm_received, raise_dispute — with
     the actor recorded on stock_order_events, which already exists and
     is append-only.

  4  The completion sweep, on the auto_decline_sweep_job pattern.

  5  Wire "open" in connection_list_service to terminal-state logic and
     delete the interim OPEN_STATUSES comment.

  6  Feed completed_at into reorder_gap, with submitted_at as fallback.

  7  UI: the two actions, and the rail-dependent dispute wording.

  Steps 1-2 are the ones that can go wrong quietly. 3-7 are ordinary.


===========================================================================
PART 7 — WHAT ONLY MANDLA CAN ANSWER
===========================================================================

  1  HOW LONG IS THE TIMEOUT? Days from CONFIRMED to a SYSTEM
     completion. Too short and it completes things that never arrived;
     too long and the ledger is stale. Suggest 7 days, no evidence.

  2  CAN A DIRECT ORDER BE DISPUTED AT ALL? Recording it costs nothing
     and builds a pattern — a supplier with many DIRECT disputes is
     worth looking at even if no single one is actionable. But it must
     not read as a claims process. Record, or leave the rail silent?

  3  SHOULD requires_connection DEFAULT TRUE FOR SUPPLIERS WHO ACCEPT
     DIRECT? See 1.1. It is the one place the old connection instinct
     still has force.

  4  WHO CAN CANCEL AFTER CONFIRMED? Today CANCELLED exists and is
     reachable by both sides. Once money has moved through PayFast,
     "cancel" and "dispute" are different things and the UI must not
     offer the wrong one.

  5  IS DISPUTED VISIBLE TO THE SUPPLIER IMMEDIATELY? A business
     raising one is an accusation. Immediate visibility invites a phone
     call that resolves it; it also invites pressure.
