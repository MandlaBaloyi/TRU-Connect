V2_15 — THE FEED: COMPOSITION, ENGINE, AND THE DEMAND MODEL
===========================================================================

Written 2026-09-14. ONE document, deliberately: this began as three
(V2_15 composition, V2_16 the engine, V2_17 the demand model) and they
were three views of a single system, which is exactly how a plan gets
read in pieces and built in pieces.

Nothing here is built. Each book ends with questions only Mandla can
answer, and those are the gate.

  BOOK ONE    WHAT THE FEED CONTAINS, and why the first real-data
              version was wrong. Read this first: it is the shortest
              and it is the mistake the other two exist to avoid
              repeating.

  BOOK TWO    THE PIPELINE. Sources, eligibility, scoring, and the
              injection engine that turns candidates into a screen.

  BOOK THREE  THE DEMAND ENGINE. The part that makes it TruConnect's
              rather than anyone's: a model of the SHOP that never
              mentions a supplier. Includes the audit of the business
              profile that feeds it, what the engine needs from
              suppliers (almost nothing), and what happens when a
              wholesaler uploads ten thousand products.

  BOOK FOUR   BEFORE WE BUILD. Keeping the TWO Community tabs apart
              in the tree (there is a supplier For You as well as a
              business one), the flaws found reviewing all of the
              above, and the codebase changes that should land first.

THE ONE SENTENCE, if you read nothing else:

    THE ENGINE MODELS THE SHOP, NOT THE SHELF -- so a wholesaler with a
    huge catalogue can COVER more of what a shop needs, and can never
    MAKE it need more.

===========================================================================
V2_15 — FEED COMPOSITION: WHAT "FOR YOU" IS ACTUALLY FOR
===========================================================================

Written 2026-09-14, after the first real-data version of the business
feed shipped and was wrong. A PLAN. Nothing here is built.

Part 8 is what only Mandla can answer.


===========================================================================
PART 0 — WHAT THE FIRST VERSION GOT WRONG, PLAINLY
===========================================================================

Commit f7cf8e2 replaced the mocked Community tab with five real
sections. It was worse than the mock it replaced, for one reason that
runs through all of it:

  IT GATED DISCOVERY ON THE CONNECTION.

"Collect near you" EXCLUDED suppliers the business is connected to,
"Suppliers you haven't ordered from" only considered connected ones, and
nothing at all showed a product from a supplier the business had no
relationship with. So a new business — no orders, no connections — saw
category chips and nothing else. The mock showed them a market.

THE CODEBASE HAD ALREADY DECIDED AGAINST THIS, and said so in the file I
should have read. V2_00 2.3 removed the connection requirement from the
ordering gate, and ordering_gate.py's own docstring gives the third
reason:

    "IT BLOCKED THE DISCOVERY MODEL ENTIRELY. Browsing was already open
    -- catalog_service and the storefront services both say so -- and
    this was the last thing standing between the catalogue and A FEED A
    BUSINESS CAN ACTUALLY BUY FROM."

The gate was removed so this feed could exist. The feed was then built
with the gate put back in by hand.

THREE MORE THINGS MISSING, all of which the mock had:

  - MOVING FAST RIGHT NOW. Absent entirely.
  - INVITE A SUPPLIER. Absent, though the action already exists in the
    header of this very screen.
  - SPONSORED. Dropped on the reasoning that nothing is sold yet --
    which is exactly the reasoning V2_04 already rejected ("built
    before it is sold, on purpose").

AND THE CATEGORY CHIPS DO NOT WORK. Tapping "Drinks" routes to SUPPLIER
search filtered by category. It finds shops, not drinks. The code says
so: "Category browsing across ALL suppliers has no screen yet."


===========================================================================
PART 1 — THE RULE EVERYTHING ELSE FOLLOWS
===========================================================================

  SEEING IS OPEN. ORDERING IS GATED. THEY ARE NOT THE SAME QUESTION.

A business opening Community must be shown what is actually on
TruConnect — products, prices, suppliers, activity — whether or not they
have ever connected to anybody. That is the only way the tab can answer
the question a new user actually has, which is "is there anything here
for me?"

THE CONSTRAINT LIVES AT CHECKOUT, WHERE IT ALREADY IS:

  ordering_gate      the supplier's OWN choice (requires_connection,
                     default FALSE). A supplier who wants to know who is
                     buying before stock moves turns it on and gets the
                     old behaviour. Nobody else is affected.
  payment rails      accepts_direct_payment / accepts_digital_payment.
  the curtain        an UNVERIFIED supplier is invisible everywhere.
                     This one is NOT relaxed and never should be.

SO A FEED CARD FOR A SUPPLIER YOU CANNOT YET ORDER FROM IS CORRECT, and
it should say what it needs from you rather than hide. "Connected" is
already rendered as a badge on these cards — the same badge is how a
NOT-connected card explains itself.

WHAT THIS IS NOT. It is not a licence to show everything everywhere. A
street address still waits for a connection; a supplier's exact stock
counts stay theirs; the price-comparison restraint in V2_04 2.4 is
untouched. Open BROWSING, not open BOOKS.


===========================================================================
PART 2 — THE SECTIONS, ALL OF THEM
===========================================================================

Marked: [built] correct today, [fix] built but wrong, [new] absent.

2.1  CATEGORY CHIPS                                      [fix]

  Built, and inert. The chip must land on PRODUCTS in that category
  across every visible supplier — that is the whole promise of a
  resolved catalogue, and it is the cheapest proof to a new business
  that the platform has stock in it.

  Needs a catalogue-wide product browse (Part 3). Until that exists the
  chips are decoration.

2.2  BUY IT AGAIN                                        [built]

  Their own confirmed order history, across all suppliers, most recent
  first. Correct as built. Empty for a new user, which is right — it
  fills on order two.

2.3  YOUR SUPPLIERS                                      [built]

  Active connections as avatars. Correct as built.

2.4  COLLECT NEAR YOU                                    [fix]

  Today it EXCLUDES connected suppliers, on the reasoning that a
  discovery rail containing your own suppliers is a bug. That reasoning
  is right for a rail called "new near you" and wrong for one called
  "collect near you" — proximity is useful whoever they are, and the
  supplier you already buy from being nearest is information, not noise.

  SHOW ALL VISIBLE SUPPLIERS IN RADIUS. Badge the connected ones, which
  the card already does.

  RADIUS, NOT "ALL SORTED BY DISTANCE". Both sides have coordinates
  already (Mapbox geocoding at signup), so this is a filter, not a data
  problem. 5 / 10 / 15 km — see Part 8.

2.5  INVITE A SUPPLIER                                   [new]

  PROMO section. The easiest one in this document: the action already
  exists in this screen's own header. It belongs in the feed too,
  because a business whose usual wholesaler is not on TruConnect has a
  real, specific thing to do about it, and the header is not where
  anybody looks for something to do.

  Placed after the personal sections and before discovery.

2.6  MOVING FAST RIGHT NOW                               [new]

  The most-ordered products across the platform in a recent window,
  regardless of supplier.

  ITS JOB IS TO PROVE THE PLACE IS ALIVE. A new business with no history
  has nothing personal to show; this is what fills that space with
  something true. It is also the first section whose ordering comes from
  COMMUNITY ACTIVITY rather than from this one user's own data, which is
  the property Part 5 is about.

  AGGREGATE ONLY. Product and count, never "Supplier X sold 40" — a
  supplier's volume is their business, and V2_04 2.4's restraint about
  never turning the catalogue into a league table applies here even
  though it was written about price.

2.7  SPONSORED                                           [new]

  V2_04: "already in the contract, DELIBERATELY BUILT BEFORE IT IS
  SOLD." Dropping it because nothing is sold inverted that reasoning.

  The point of building it early is that the paid-placement design is
  proven and shipped BEFORE the first wholesaler conversation, rather
  than retrofitted under deadline once one is signed. The label is
  server-supplied so the wording can follow whatever the advertising
  disclosure rules turn out to require.

  What the backend needs: somewhere to say "this supplier is placed
  here, from this date to this date, with this label". Not a billing
  system.

2.8  SUPPLIERS YOU HAVEN'T ORDERED FROM                  [fix]

  Today: connected suppliers with no orders. That is one real case and
  the smaller one.

  Should be: suppliers this business has never ordered from, connected
  or not, nearest and most active first. A connection that stalled after
  the handshake and a supplier they have never met are the same
  opportunity from the platform's point of view, and the subtitle can
  distinguish them.


===========================================================================
PART 3 — THE DATA LAYER THIS NEEDS AND DOES NOT HAVE
===========================================================================

  3.1  CATALOGUE-WIDE PRODUCT BROWSE

  There is no query that answers "products in category X across all
  visible suppliers, ranked". Every product read today is scoped to ONE
  supplier's shopfront (catalog_service, list_profile_products).

  This is the single missing piece that blocks 2.1 and 2.6, and it is
  the foundation the rest of the feed's product rails stand on.

  Needs: filter by category, exclude archived and deleted, apply the
  curtain, batch inventory and approved media the way
  build_catalog_product_dicts already does, and page.

  3.2  AN ORDER-COUNT WINDOW

  For 2.6: order item counts grouped by product over N days. One query,
  same shape as the trading-history queries added for the ledger.

  3.3  SPONSORED PLACEMENT STORAGE

  A small table: supplier, label, starts_at, ends_at, and a position
  hint. Read by the composer, written by nobody yet — a CLI command is
  enough until there is a reason for a screen.


===========================================================================
PART 4 — WHAT A CHIP TAP MUST DO
===========================================================================

  Tap "Drinks" -> a product list, across suppliers, in that category.

  It is worth being exact about why this matters more than it looks. A
  business that has just installed the app has no orders and no
  connections. The chips are the ONLY section that works for them, and
  today they lead to a list of shops rather than a shelf of drinks. The
  first thing a new user does is therefore the thing that works least.

  The screen itself may be an existing one reused (the supplier
  catalogue screen already renders a category-filtered product list) or
  a new one. That is a smaller decision than the query behind it.


===========================================================================
PART 5 — THE FEED SHOULD IMPROVE WITHOUT BEING REWRITTEN
===========================================================================

  Mandla's requirement, and it is the right one: composition should
  respond to what is actually happening rather than to a hardcoded
  order.

  THE SHAPE THAT GETS THERE:

    1  EVERY SECTION IS A BUILDER THAT CAN RETURN NOTHING. Already true.
       This is what lets the order change without any section breaking.

    2  A SECTION EARNS ITS PLACE FROM A SCORE, not from a position in a
       list. Recency and volume of the user's own activity, distance,
       how much the section actually has to show. The current order
       becomes the tie-break rather than the rule.

    3  IMPRESSIONS ARE THE INPUT THAT DOES NOT EXIST YET. V2_04 names
       impression logging as the ONE future exception to "the feed owns
       no tables". Without it, "improving on its own" is guesswork
       dressed as an algorithm -- there is no signal saying which rails
       were scrolled past.

    4  AND IT STAYS EXPLAINABLE. A feed nobody can explain is a feed
       nobody can debug, and the same argument that makes logistic
       regression the right credit model here (V2_03) applies: a scoring
       function you can read beats a black box that scores better.

  NOT NOW: personalised ranking learned per user. There is not enough
  data, and a recommender trained on nothing recommends nothing twice.


===========================================================================
PART 6 — ORDER OF WORK
===========================================================================

  1  Catalogue-wide product browse (3.1). Everything below needs it.
  2  Chip tap lands on real products (Part 4). The new-user path.
  3  Collect near you: radius, and stop excluding connections (2.4).
  4  Suppliers you haven't ordered from: include unconnected (2.8).
  5  Invite a supplier (2.5). Half an hour.
  6  Moving fast right now (2.6) + the window query (3.2).
  7  Sponsored storage and section (2.7, 3.3).
  8  Scoring (Part 5 step 2), once there is more than one thing to rank.
  9  Impressions (Part 5 step 3), which is its own decision.

  1 AND 2 ARE THE ONES THAT CHANGE WHAT A NEW USER SEES. Everything
  after is depth for a user who already has a reason to open the tab.


===========================================================================
PART 7 — WHAT MUST NOT HAPPEN
===========================================================================

  THE CURTAIN DOES NOT RELAX. Everything in Part 1 is about the
  CONNECTION, never about verification. An unverified supplier stays
  invisible in every section here.

  NO LEAGUE TABLES. "Moving fast" ranks PRODUCTS. The moment it ranks
  suppliers, or shows one supplier's volume next to another's, it
  becomes the thing V2_04 2.4 refuses to build.

  NO STREET ADDRESSES IN DISCOVERY. Suburb or city, as now. Proximity
  can be shown as a distance without publishing where a supplier keeps
  their stock.

  SPONSORED MUST BE LABELLED, always, and never mixed silently into an
  organic rail.


===========================================================================
PART 8 — WHAT ONLY MANDLA CAN ANSWER
===========================================================================

  1  WHAT RADIUS? 5, 10 or 15 km for "Collect near you" -- and does it
     widen automatically when a radius returns too few, or stay fixed
     and show nothing? Widening is friendlier and makes "near" a
     softer claim than it appears.

  2  HOW LONG IS "RIGHT NOW"? The window for Moving fast. 7 days is the
     obvious guess and a slow-moving category would never appear in it.

  3  DOES A BUSINESS SEE PRODUCTS FROM A SUPPLIER WHOSE
     requires_connection IS ON? Part 1 says yes, with the card
     explaining what is needed. The alternative -- hiding them -- is the
     old model and makes those suppliers invisible rather than
     protected. Worth confirming, because it is the supplier's
     expectation that is being set.

  4  WHAT DOES SPONSORED COST AND WHO DECIDES PLACEMENT? Not needed to
     build the storage, needed before anything is sold.

  5  IMPRESSION LOGGING: yes or not yet? It is the difference between a
     feed that improves and a feed that is reordered by hand. It is also
     the first table the composer would own, which V2_04 allows exactly
     once.

#  BOOK TWO — THE PIPELINE AND THE INJECTION ENGINE

===========================================================================
V2_16 — THE FEED ENGINE
===========================================================================

Written 2026-09-14. The architecture V2_15 asked for. A PLAN; nothing
here is built. Part 14 is what only Mandla can answer.

V2_15 said WHAT the For You feed should contain and why the first
attempt was wrong. This says HOW it is assembled — the pipeline, the
scoring, and the injection engine that decides what a particular shop
sees on a particular morning.

THE ONE-LINE VERSION. A feed is not a list of queries glued together in
a service. It is a PIPELINE: many cheap sources propose candidates, one
gate decides what is eligible, one function scores them, and one engine
arranges them into sections. Every hard question in this document is a
question about one of those four stages, and keeping them separate is
what stops the feed becoming the 900-line service every marketplace
eventually grows.


===========================================================================
PART 1 — WHY THE CATALOGUE IS THE ENGINE, NOT A LOOKUP TABLE
===========================================================================

The first feed read SupplierOffer rows directly. Anyone can do that. It
is a shopfront list with extra steps, and a competitor with two weeks
and a copy of the screenshots would match it.

WHAT CANNOT BE COPIED QUICKLY is that "WHT SUGR 2.5KG" from one
wholesaler and "White Sugar 2.5kg" from another already resolve to ONE
catalogue product (V2_11, the matching ladder). SupplierOffer.product_id
is that resolution, and it is months of measurement — barcode, alias,
normalisation, rules, AI — already done.

    WITHOUT THE CATALOGUE          WITH IT
    ------------------------       ---------------------------------
    three unrelated strings        one product, three prices
    "products from your supplier"  "this product, from whoever is
                                    best for YOU right now"
    no comparison possible         comparison is a join
    popularity per supplier        DEMAND per product, across the
                                   whole platform

So every product-bearing section in the feed generates candidates at the
CATALOGUE PRODUCT level, and only at the last moment picks which
supplier's offer to show. That inversion is the whole architecture.

IT ALSO MAKES THE CATALOGUE PAY FOR ITSELF. Today the catalogue is
write-only: offers resolve INTO it and nothing reads OUT. Matching is
maintained, reviewed and paid for, and returns nothing a user can see.
The feed is where it starts earning.


===========================================================================
PART 2 — THE PIPELINE
===========================================================================

    SOURCES            "here are things this shop might want, and why"
       |               many, independent, cheap, none knows about the feed
       v
    ELIGIBILITY        one gate. curtain, deletion, demo, self-dealing
       |               nothing downstream re-checks these
       v
    SCORING            one readable function per candidate kind
       |               explainable on purpose (see Part 6)
       v
    INJECTION          sections built, thinned, ordered, diversified,
       |               sponsored placed, order stabilised
       v
    RENDER             the typed sections the app already draws

  WHY FOUR STAGES AND NOT ONE SERVICE. Each stage has a different
  failure mode and a different test. A source that returns nothing is a
  quiet feature gap; an eligibility leak is a SAFETY bug; a scoring
  mistake is a bad-but-harmless ordering; an injection mistake is a
  layout problem. Collapsing them means every bug is "the feed is
  wrong", which is the hardest sentence in software to act on.

  AND IT IS HOW THE FEED GETS TO IMPROVE ITSELF. New behaviour arrives
  as a new SOURCE or a changed WEIGHT, not as a rewrite.


===========================================================================
PART 3 — CANDIDATES: THE ONE TYPE EVERYTHING SPEAKS
===========================================================================

    Candidate
      kind          PRODUCT | SUPPLIER | ACTION
      ref_id        catalogue product id, supplier profile id, or an
                    action key ("invite_supplier")
      source        which source proposed it
      reason        A HUMAN SENTENCE. "You ordered this 3 weeks ago."
                    "2.1 km away." "12 shops near you bought this."
      signals       dict of raw numbers the scorer reads:
                    days_since_last_order, order_count, distance_km,
                    in_stock, price_advantage_cents, is_connected
      base_score    the source's own confidence, before global scoring

  THE reason FIELD IS NOT DECORATION AND IT IS NOT COPY. It is:

    1  THE UI. "Because you ordered this 3 weeks ago" under a card is
       the difference between a feed that feels like it knows the shop
       and one that feels like an advert. It costs nothing to carry and
       it is the single cheapest trust win available.
    2  THE DEBUGGER. "Why is this here?" is otherwise unanswerable, and
       an unanswerable feed is one nobody dares change.
    3  THE HONESTY CHECK. A candidate whose reason cannot be written in
       plain words should not be in the feed. That rule alone would
       have caught the first version.

  SIGNALS ARE RAW, NEVER PRE-SCORED. A source reports "14 days since
  last order"; it does not report 0.7. The moment a source scores, the
  weights are scattered across six files and nobody can retune the feed
  without reading all of them.


===========================================================================
PART 4 — THE SOURCES
===========================================================================

Each is a function of (business, context) -> list[Candidate]. None reads
another. None knows what section it ends up in — that is Part 7's job,
and it is what lets one source feed two sections.

  4.1  REPEAT PURCHASE            -> PRODUCT
       Catalogue products this shop has had CONFIRMED before. Signals:
       order_count, days_since_last_order, typical_gap_days.
       Reason: "You ordered this 3 weeks ago."
       THE HIGHEST-VALUE SOURCE IN THE SYSTEM and the cheapest.

  4.2  DUE TO REORDER             -> PRODUCT
       A subset of 4.1 where days_since_last > typical_gap. Separate
       source, not a flag, because it deserves its own section AND its
       own weight — reorder_gap already computes this (shared/algorithms).
       Reason: "You usually reorder this about now."

  4.3  CATEGORY AFFINITY          -> PRODUCT
       Products in categories this shop actually buys, that they have
       NOT bought. This is the bridge from "your list" to "the market",
       and it is only computable because categories live on the
       catalogue product rather than on each supplier's spelling.
       Reason: "Other shops that buy bread also buy this."

  4.4  PLATFORM DEMAND            -> PRODUCT
       Most-ordered catalogue products in a window, platform-wide.
       Its job is COLD START (Part 9) and proof of life.
       Reason: "Moving fast this week."
       AGGREGATE ONLY — never "Supplier X sold 40" (Part 12).

  4.5  PROXIMITY                  -> SUPPLIER
       Visible suppliers within a radius, connected or not. Signals:
       distance_km, is_connected, product_count, offers_delivery.
       Reason: "2.1 km away — collection."

  4.6  RELATIONSHIP               -> SUPPLIER
       Connections, and connections with no orders behind them.
       Reason: "Connected, no orders yet."

  4.7  UNTRIED SUPPLIERS          -> SUPPLIER
       Visible suppliers this shop has never ordered from, connected or
       not, whose catalogue overlaps what the shop actually buys.
       THE OVERLAP IS THE POINT: a hardware wholesaler is not an
       opportunity for a shop that sells bread, and a rail of
       irrelevant shops teaches people to ignore the tab.

  4.8  COMMERCE                   -> SUPPLIER
       Sponsored placements (Part 8).

  4.9  ACTIONS                    -> ACTION
       "Invite a supplier." Cheap, real, and the only source whose
       candidate is a thing to DO rather than a thing to buy.
       Eligible when the shop has few connections, or always — Part 14.

  A SOURCE MAY RETURN NOTHING AND THAT IS NORMAL. A new shop lights up
  4.4, 4.5 and 4.9 only. That is a complete, honest feed.


===========================================================================
PART 5 — ELIGIBILITY: ONE GATE, NOT A SCATTERING OF CHECKS
===========================================================================

Every candidate passes one function before anything is scored.

    THE CURTAIN                unverified supplier -> gone. Never
                               relaxed, no exceptions, already enforced
                               by an architecture test.
    DELETED / ARCHIVED         offers and profiles.
    OUT OF CATALOGUE           a product with no live offer from any
                               visible supplier cannot be bought, so it
                               cannot be shown.
    DEMO                       Part 10.
    SELF-DEALING               a supplier's own shop never appears to
                               them. (Matters once a user can hold both
                               roles; cheap to write now, expensive to
                               retrofit after the bug report.)
    DEDUPE                     the same product proposed by three
                               sources appears ONCE, keeping the
                               STRONGEST reason. A feed that shows a
                               shop the same bag of maize four times
                               looks broken and is.

  WHY ONE GATE. The first feed applied the curtain in three builders
  with three spellings, and the architecture test caught two of them.
  A single gate means new sources are safe by construction, which is
  the only kind of safety that survives a busy week.


===========================================================================
PART 6 — SCORING: READABLE ON PURPOSE
===========================================================================

  score = w1*recency + w2*frequency + w3*proximity + w4*availability
        + w5*price_advantage + w6*relationship - w7*fatigue

  Each term is a named function of ONE signal, each returns 0..1, and
  the weights live in one config object.

  IT MUST STAY EXPLAINABLE, and this is a position rather than a
  preference. V2_03's whole thesis is that informal businesses are
  excluded by models that cannot read their data — building an
  unexplainable ranker on top of that argument would be incoherent.
  When a supplier asks "why am I below them", there has to be an answer
  that is not "the model said so".

  THE TERMS, and what each is actually for:

    recency          days since this shop last bought it, decayed.
    frequency        how often. A weekly line outranks a one-off.
    proximity        distance, with a soft cliff at the radius rather
                     than a hard one — 5.1 km is not meaningfully
                     further than 4.9 km, and a hard threshold is the
                     CLIFF already recorded as a hazard in B12.
    availability     in stock, supplier accepts an orderable payment
                     rail, minimum order not absurd for this shop.
                     AN UNBUYABLE CARD IS WORSE THAN NO CARD.
    price_advantage  ONLY computable through the catalogue. Bounded
                     hard by V2_04 2.4: it may raise a product, it may
                     never render as a league table of suppliers.
    relationship     connected, previously ordered, or stranger.
                     Deliberately NOT dominant — the whole point of
                     V2_15 is that discovery is open.
    fatigue          shown recently and not acted on -> down. This is
                     the term that needs impressions (Part 11) and is
                     the reason they matter.

  NO LEARNED RANKER YET. There is not enough interaction data, and a
  recommender trained on nothing recommends nothing twice. Revisit when
  impressions exist and there are thousands of them, not before.


===========================================================================
PART 7 — THE INJECTION ENGINE
===========================================================================

Mandla's word, and it is the right one: sources produce a pile, and
something has to INJECT that pile into a screen in an order that makes
sense. This is that thing.

  7.1  SECTIONS ARE ASSEMBLED, NOT QUERIED

  A section is a TEMPLATE plus a candidate filter:

      section  BUY_IT_AGAIN
        takes    PRODUCT candidates from REPEAT_PURCHASE
        title    "Buy it again"
        min      3
        max      12

  Adding a section is then config, not code — and one source can feed
  several sections without being run twice.

  7.2  THINNING

  Below `min`, the section does not render. Already the rule in the app
  (MIN_ITEMS_PER_RAIL) and in the supplier feed. Stated once here so
  every future section inherits it.

  7.3  SECTION ORDER IS EARNED, WITH FLOORS AND CEILINGS

  Order by the aggregate score of a section's candidates, EXCEPT:

    PINNED FIRST      category chips. Always present, always first: the
                      only section that works for a shop with no
                      history, and the first thing a new user taps.
    PINNED LAST       actions and growth rails. A section asking a shop
                      to start something never outranks one helping
                      them finish something.
    SPONSORED SLOT    a fixed position, never first, never adjacent to
                      another sponsored section.

  7.4  DIVERSITY: NO SINGLE SUPPLIER OWNS THE FEED

  A wholesaler with 400 products would otherwise fill every rail —
  their catalogue is simply bigger, and score alone would reward that.

      cap: at most N cards per supplier per section (N ≈ 3)

  WITHOUT THIS THE FEED STOPS BEING A MARKET. It is also a commercial
  protection: the first supplier to notice they can dominate by bulk
  upload will do exactly that, and the fix after the fact is a fight.

  7.5  STABILITY: THE FEED MUST NOT RESHUFFLE UNDER A THUMB

  Pull-to-refresh returning a different order is disorienting and makes
  the thing a shop was reaching for disappear.

      tie-break with a seed derived from (business_id, calendar day)

  Same shop, same day, same order. It changes when the DATA changes, or
  tomorrow. Jitter without chaos.

  7.6  BUDGET

  The whole feed is ONE request and should stay near ~150 ms of
  database work. Sources are capped, batched, and none may run a query
  per candidate — the N+1 removed twice already this week is the
  failure mode this stage invites most.


===========================================================================
PART 8 — SPONSORED, BUILT BEFORE IT IS SOLD
===========================================================================

V2_04: "deliberately built before it is sold." The reason is leverage —
walking into a wholesaler conversation with the placement already
working is a different conversation from promising to build it.

  STORAGE  supplier_profile_id, label, starts_at, ends_at, position
           hint, and a cap (impressions or days). A small table in
           `marketplace`, which exists and is empty.

  RULES    always labelled; never first; never two adjacent; passes the
           SAME eligibility gate as everything else — a sponsored
           supplier who is unverified, deleted or has nothing to sell
           does not render, whatever was paid. Money does not move the
           curtain.

  NOT NOW  billing, self-serve, auction. A CLI command writes the row.


===========================================================================
PART 9 — COLD START
===========================================================================

The hardest screen in the product: a shop that installed the app this
morning. Nothing personal exists. The tab still has to answer "is there
anything here for me?"

    HAS NOTHING            categories, platform demand, nearby
                           suppliers, invite. A market, not an apology.
    HAS A LOCATION         proximity rails become real and specific.
    HAS ONE ORDER          repeat purchase lights up. Still no rhythm.
    HAS A RHYTHM (2+)      due-to-reorder, affinity, the real feed.

  EACH STEP UNLOCKS ITSELF FROM DATA THE SHOP CREATED BY TRADING. No
  onboarding questionnaire, no "tell us what you sell" — the app earns
  the information by being used, which is also the only version a
  shopkeeper at 6am will tolerate.

  AND NEVER AN EMPTY SCREEN. If every source returns nothing, the feed
  falls back to categories plus the newest visible suppliers. A blank
  Community tab is the app saying "there is nothing here", which is a
  lie that loses the user permanently.


===========================================================================
PART 10 — DEMO DATA THAT CANNOT LEAK
===========================================================================

Mandla's requirement: seed suppliers and products that can be switched
on for a demonstration and off in production, WITHOUT the logic knowing.

  THE DESIGN: A FLAG AND ONE FILTER, exactly like the curtain.

    is_demo      boolean, default false, on supplier_profiles and
                 supplier_offers. Demo rows are REAL rows.
    DEMO_DATA    config flag, default FALSE.
    demo_filter() one predicate, in the eligibility gate (Part 5).

  WHY A FILTER AND NOT A SEPARATE CODE PATH. A demo mode that swaps in
  fake services proves the fake services work. Demo rows travelling the
  real pipeline mean the demonstration exercises the SAME sources,
  scoring and injection the live feed uses — so the demo is a test, and
  a bug found on stage is a real bug.

  WHY IT CANNOT LEAK. One filter, in the gate every candidate passes,
  defaulting to OFF. Same shape as the curtain, and it gets the same
  treatment: an ARCHITECTURE TEST asserting no query outside the gate
  reads demo rows, so a future source cannot forget.

  AND IT IS REVERSIBLE. `flask demo seed` / `flask demo purge`, audited
  like every other privileged command.


===========================================================================
PART 11 — THE FEEDBACK LOOP
===========================================================================

  Without this, "the feed improves itself" is a slogan. With it, the
  fatigue term in Part 6 has an input and section order has evidence.

    IMPRESSION   section, candidate, position, business, day
    INTERACTION  tapped, added to cart, ordered

  V2_04 names impression logging as THE ONE exception to "the composer
  owns no tables". This is that exception being spent, and it should be
  spent deliberately rather than drifted into.

  WRITE CHEAPLY. Batched from the client, one row per section per view,
  never one per card. Sampled if volume demands. This is telemetry, not
  accounting: losing 5% of it costs nothing, and blocking a feed render
  on a write costs everything.

  PRIVACY. It records what a shop was SHOWN and what they tapped.
  Aggregated for ranking. Never exposed to suppliers as "who looked at
  you" — that is surveillance wearing an analytics hat.


===========================================================================
PART 12 — WHAT MUST NOT HAPPEN
===========================================================================

  THE CURTAIN NEVER RELAXES. Not for sponsorship, not for demo, not for
  a thin rail that would otherwise not render.

  NO LEAGUE TABLES. Products rank. Suppliers are never shown ranked
  against each other, and one supplier's volume is never visible to
  another. V2_04 2.4, and it survives everything in this document.

  NO UNBUYABLE CARDS. Out of stock, no orderable payment rail, or a
  minimum order far beyond this shop's basket — down-ranked or gone.

  NO STREET ADDRESSES IN DISCOVERY. Distance, suburb, city. That is all.

  THE FEED OWNS NO TABLES except impressions (Part 11), and the
  architecture test stays.

  NO DARK PATTERNS. No fake scarcity, no invented "3 people are viewing
  this". Everything the feed says must be true, because the entire
  product is a trust argument.

===========================================================================
V2_17 — THE DEMAND ENGINE
===========================================================================

Written 2026-09-14. The biggest thing in these documents. A PLAN — no
code until Part 15 is answered.

V2_15 said what the feed should contain. V2_16 said how candidates flow
into sections. Both were still organised around SUPPLY: what is in the
catalogue, who is nearby, what is on offer.

This inverts it. Mandla's proposal, and it is the right one:

    THE ENGINE MODELS THE SHOP, NOT THE SHELF.

An engine that understands a particular spaza in Soweto — what it
sells, who it serves, how often it restocks, what it spends — is
independent of which suppliers happen to exist today. Suppliers become
a QUERY against that understanding rather than the thing the
understanding is made of.


===========================================================================
PART 0 — THE FINDING THIS RESTS ON
===========================================================================

The app already asks every informal business:

    products            what they sell
    customer_types      who they serve
    supplier_sources    where they currently buy
    operating_days      when they trade
    opening/closing     the hours
    employee_range      how big they are
    years_operating     how established
    order_channels      how orders reach them
    payment_methods     how they take money
    province / city_or_town / latitude / longitude / physical_address
    business_type / category / description

All of it is collected, stored, and NEVER READ. Nothing outside the
profile module touches `products`, `customer_types` or
`supplier_sources` — verified 2026-09-14.

    WE ASK A SHOPKEEPER WHAT THEY SELL AND THEN THROW IT AWAY.

That is the whole opportunity. The fuel is already being gathered, at a
moment the user is willing to give it, and there is already a
`business_info_complete` flag counting whether they finished. The
engine does not need new data collection to begin. It needs to start
listening.


===========================================================================
PART 1 — THE PRINCIPLE: DEMAND IS MODELLED, SUPPLY IS RESOLVED
===========================================================================

    THE SHOP PROFILE          "this shop restocks bread twice a week,
    (supplier-independent)     spends about R1,400 a week, sells
       |                       cooldrinks and airtime, serves walk-in
       |                       customers in Soweto, opens at 6am"
       v
    NEED RESOLUTION           which CATALOGUE PRODUCTS satisfy that
       |                      (the catalogue, not any supplier)
       v
    SUPPLY RESOLUTION         which supplier's OFFER this shop should
       |                      see for each product, given distance,
       |                      price, relationship, stock
       v
    THE FEED

  THE INVARIANT THAT MAKES THIS AN ENGINE:

    NOTHING IN THE SHOP PROFILE MENTIONS A SUPPLIER.

  Test it by deleting every supplier on the platform. The shop profile
  is unchanged: this shop still sells bread, still restocks twice a
  week, still spends R1,400. Only the answer to "who can supply that"
  goes empty.

  AND THE CONVERSE, WHICH IS THE COMMERCIAL POINT. A new wholesaler
  joins on Tuesday. Every shop whose model already says "needs maize
  meal weekly, 4km radius" is instantly better served — with no
  recomputation of any shop, no retraining, no backfill. The engine got
  better because the market did.

  THIS IS ALSO THE ANSWER TO THE BULK-UPLOAD PROBLEM. A wholesaler with
  400 products cannot dominate a feed built on what the SHOP needs;
  they can only satisfy more of it. Dominance was possible when the
  feed was assembled out of supply.


===========================================================================
PART 2 — THE SHOP PROFILE HAS THREE LAYERS
===========================================================================

    DECLARED    what they told us. Available on day one, before a
                single order. Cheap, immediate, and a HYPOTHESIS.

    OBSERVED    what they actually did. Orders, baskets, categories,
                timing, taps. Slow to accumulate and it is EVIDENCE.

    DERIVED     what we compute from both. Category weights, reorder
                cadence, basket size, price sensitivity, radius
                tolerance. THIS IS THE ONLY LAYER THE FEED READS.

  WHY THREE AND NOT ONE. Declared alone is a survey — people describe
  the shop they mean to run. Observed alone cannot start: a new shop
  has nothing, which is the cold-start hole every recommender falls
  into. Derived is where the two are reconciled, and keeping it
  separate means the reconciliation rule lives in ONE place instead of
  being re-argued in every source.

  THE FEED NEVER READS DECLARED OR OBSERVED DIRECTLY. If a source wants
  to know "does this shop sell bread", it asks the derived model, which
  answers with a weight and a confidence — not with a checkbox the user
  ticked eight months ago.


===========================================================================
PART 3 — DECLARED SIGNALS, AND WHAT EACH IS ACTUALLY WORTH
===========================================================================

  products             THE STRONGEST DECLARED SIGNAL. Maps to category
                       weights immediately: a shop that sells bread and
                       cooldrinks gets BAKERY and BEVERAGES weighted on
                       day one. Needs a mapping table from the form's
                       vocabulary to ProductCategory (Part 15 q1).

  customer_types       Shapes pack size and price band. A shop serving
                       walk-in customers buys singles and small packs;
                       one supplying other businesses buys cases.

  employee_range       A proxy for VOLUME. One person behind a counter
                       and six staff are different basket sizes, and it
                       is the only size signal available before orders.

  operating_days
  opening / closing    WHEN to surface things, and a real one: a shop
                       opening at 6am is restocking the night before,
                       so a reorder nudge at 4pm beats one at 9am.

  supplier_sources     CORRECTED 2026-09-14 -- this field does NOT
                       name suppliers. It is a closed list of TYPES:
                       Local suppliers, Wholesalers, Distributors,
                       Manufacturers, Markets, Other businesses. An
                       earlier draft of this document claimed it gave
                       acquisition leads with warm introductions
                       attached; that was written without reading it.

                       What it is actually worth: a shop buying from
                       MANUFACTURERS or MARKETS buys differently from
                       one buying from WHOLESALERS -- pack size, price
                       expectation and cadence all differ. Useful, and
                       a fraction of what was claimed. Part 16 proposes
                       the version that would earn the claim.

  province/city/coords The radius, and the only geography discovery has.

  years_operating      Stability, not need. Weak for recommendation,
                       genuinely useful later for the lending thesis.

  business_type
  category             Coarse but free. The fallback when products is
                       empty.

  WHAT IS MISSING AND WOULD BE WORTH ASKING (Part 15 q2): typical
  restock day, and rough weekly spend. Both are things a shopkeeper
  knows instantly and both would sharpen the model more than anything
  else on the form. Neither is worth adding if it costs completion
  rate.


===========================================================================
PART 4 — OBSERVED SIGNALS
===========================================================================

  FROM ORDERS (the strongest, and already recorded)
    which catalogue products, how often, in what quantity, at what
    price, from whom, and — once V2_14 lands — whether it arrived.

  FROM BASKETS
    what was added and abandoned. An abandoned basket below a minimum
    order is a specific, fixable failure and the feed can fix it.

  FROM BROWSING
    categories opened, products viewed, searches run. Weaker than
    orders and available far sooner.

  FROM THE FEED ITSELF
    impressions and taps (V2_16 Part 11). The correction signal for
    everything above.

  ONE RULE: OBSERVED SIGNALS ARE APPEND-ONLY FACTS, never edited. The
  derived layer interprets; it does not rewrite history. Same
  discipline as stock_order_events.


===========================================================================
PART 5 — THE DERIVED MODEL: WHAT THE FEED ACTUALLY READS
===========================================================================

  Per shop, recomputed rather than accumulated by hand:

    category_weights      {BAKERY: 0.9, BEVERAGES: 0.7, ...}
                          with a CONFIDENCE per entry, because
                          "declared once" and "bought forty times"
                          must not look identical to a caller.

    product_cadence       per catalogue product: typical gap, last
                          bought, due-ness. reorder_gap already
                          computes this shape (shared/algorithms).

    basket_profile        typical order value, typical line count,
                          pack-size preference.

    price_sensitivity     does this shop take the cheapest offer or
                          stay with one supplier? Derived from actual
                          switching behaviour, and it is what tells
                          offer_selection whether to lead with price.

    radius_tolerance      how far they actually deal. A shop ordering
                          from 12km away tolerates more than the
                          default; one that never leaves 2km tolerates
                          less.

    restock_rhythm        day-of-week and time-of-day patterns.

    confidence            overall. Drives how much the feed leans on
                          the model versus falling back to
                          platform-wide defaults.

  EVERY DERIVED FIELD CARRIES ITS PROVENANCE — declared, observed, or
  both. A feed that cannot say "we think you sell bread because you
  told us" versus "because you have bought it nine times" cannot
  explain itself, and V2_16 Part 3 already made explainability a rule.


===========================================================================
PART 6 — WHEN DECLARED AND OBSERVED DISAGREE
===========================================================================

  A shop declares bread. Six months of orders contain no bread.

  THE WRONG READING: they lied, drop the signal.
  THE RIGHT READING: they buy bread SOMEWHERE ELSE.

  That is not a data problem. It is the single most valuable thing the
  engine can learn, because it names a category where TruConnect is
  losing an existing, confirmed need — and it is the demand-gap signal
  that makes a supplier conversation concrete (Part 10).

  THE RULE:

    observed CONFIRMS declared      confidence rises fast
    observed CONTRADICTS declared   the DECLARED weight stays, flagged
                                    as UNSERVED. Never silently dropped.
    observed EXCEEDS declared       add the category, lower confidence
                                    in the form's completeness, and
                                    consider prompting an update.

  DECLARED DECAYS, IT DOES NOT EXPIRE. A form filled fourteen months
  ago is weaker evidence than one filled last week, but a shop that
  still sells what it said it sells is the normal case. Decay the
  confidence, keep the signal.


===========================================================================
PART 7 — HOW IT GROWS ON ITS OWN
===========================================================================

  Three loops, at three speeds. Together they are what "the engine
  improves itself" actually means.

  FAST — ON EVERY ORDER (synchronous, incremental)
    update cadence for the products involved, basket profile, last-seen
    categories. Cheap: it touches only what the order touched.

  NIGHTLY — THE RECOMPUTE (a scheduled JobSpec, the existing pattern)
    rebuild weights and confidence from the full history, apply decay,
    recompute radius tolerance and price sensitivity, refresh
    unserved-need flags.

  ON DECLARATION — IMMEDIATELY
    the shop edits their profile; the model is rebuilt then and there,
    because the next thing they do is look at the feed, and a feed that
    ignores what they just told it teaches them the form is pointless.

  AND THE CORRECTION LOOP, once impressions exist: shown-and-ignored
  lowers a weight; shown-and-tapped raises it. This is the only loop
  that needs data we do not yet collect, and it is the one that turns
  three heuristics into something that learns.

  IDEMPOTENT AND REBUILDABLE. The derived model is a CACHE of a
  computation over facts we keep — droppable and rebuildable from
  scratch at any time. Nothing is lost if it is wrong; it is recomputed.
  That property is what makes it safe to change the weights later.


===========================================================================
PART 8 — COLD START IS SOLVED BY DECLARATION
===========================================================================

  This is the reason declared signals earn their place.

    MINUTE ONE      They finish the profile: sells bread, cooldrinks,
                    airtime; Soweto; walk-in customers; opens 6am.
                    The feed ALREADY has category weights, a radius and
                    a basket guess. Not a cold start — a warm one.

    WEEK ONE        First orders. Cadence begins. Declared confirmed
                    or contradicted.

    MONTH ONE       Rhythms real. Reorder timing becomes specific
                    rather than generic.

    MONTH THREE     Price sensitivity and radius tolerance are
                    observed rather than assumed.

  COMPARE WITH THE ALTERNATIVE: a shop that declares nothing sees the
  platform-wide default feed — popular products, nearby suppliers — for
  weeks. It works, and it is worse. That difference is what makes Part
  9 honest rather than manipulative.


===========================================================================
PART 9 — ASKING FOR THE PROFILE, WITHOUT A DARK PATTERN
===========================================================================

  Mandla asked for a prompt. The rule that keeps it clean:

    ONLY ASK FOR WHAT WILL VISIBLY CHANGE THE NEXT SCREEN, AND SAY
    EXACTLY WHAT IT WILL CHANGE.

    GOOD   "Tell us what you sell and we'll show you stock for it."
           -> true, specific, and the feed measurably changes.
    BAD    "Complete your profile!" with a progress ring.
           -> a chore with no stated benefit, which is the pattern
              every app uses and every user has learned to dismiss.

  WHERE   A dismissible card in the feed, not a modal. It appears when
          `business_info_complete` is false, is scored like any other
          candidate (V2_16), and sinks as the feed fills with real
          personalised content — because by then it has less to offer.

  ONE FIELD AT A TIME, at a moment it matters. "Which of these do you
  sell?" next to a category rail beats a twelve-field form. The form
  still exists for anyone who wants it in one go.

  AND IT MUST BE SKIPPABLE FOREVER. A shop that never fills it in gets
  a worse feed, not a blocked one.


===========================================================================
PART 10 — THE MODEL TELLS US THINGS TOO
===========================================================================

  The demand model is read by the feed. It should also be read by us,
  and this is where the ecosystem argument becomes concrete.

  UNSERVED NEED          Categories shops declare and cannot buy here.
                         "412 shops sell airtime; we have no airtime
                         products." That is a product gap, a supplier
                         acquisition target, and the single most
                         persuasive slide in any wholesaler meeting.

  CATALOGUE HOLES        A declared product with no catalogue match is
                         either a missing product or a MATCHING FAILURE.
                         Either way it belongs in the review queue that
                         already exists — the demand model becomes an
                         input to catalogue quality rather than only a
                         consumer of it.

  GEOGRAPHIC GAPS        Demand concentrated where no supplier delivers.
                         Says where to recruit, with numbers.

  SUPPLIER LEADS         ONLY IF THE FORM ASKS FOR NAMES, which
                         today it does not -- see the correction in
                         Part 3. With names it is a list of real
                         businesses with a real reason to join. Without
                         them it is a distribution of shop TYPES, which
                         says where to recruit but not from whom.

  NONE OF THIS IS SHOWN TO SUPPLIERS AS PER-SHOP DETAIL. Aggregate,
  always. "12 shops within 5km buy maize meal weekly" is a market fact;
  "Mama Ntuli buys 4 bags on Thursdays" is surveillance.

PART 11 — THE INVARIANTS, AND THE TESTS THAT HOLD THEM
===========================================================================

  1  THE SHOP PROFILE IS SUPPLIER-INDEPENDENT.
     Architecture test: nothing under demand/ imports a supplier
     repository, model or service. The same AST discipline as the
     composer and curtain tests. Without this the independence claim
     rots within a month of convenient imports.

  2  THE FEED READS ONLY THE DERIVED LAYER.
     Test: no feed source imports the declared or observed tables.

  3  THE DERIVED MODEL IS REBUILDABLE FROM SCRATCH.
     Test: drop it, recompute, compare — identical.

  4  NO PER-SHOP DATA REACHES A SUPPLIER.
     Test: supplier-facing serialisers expose no shop-level demand
     field.

  5  DECLARED IS NEVER SILENTLY DROPPED (Part 6).
     Test: a contradicted declaration survives as UNSERVED.

PART 12 — PRIVACY, STATED BEFORE IT IS BUILT
===========================================================================

  This engine knows more about an informal business than anything else
  in the platform. That is the point and the risk.

  IT IS THEIR DATA AND IT MUST BE VISIBLE TO THEM. A shop should be
  able to see what the model believes — "we think you sell bread and
  cooldrinks, and restock about weekly" — and correct it. A model a
  user cannot inspect is one they cannot trust, and this platform's
  entire argument is trust.

  DELETION MUST TAKE IT WITH THEM. The derived model is per-shop and
  goes when they go. It belongs on the deletion classification already
  in the backlog.

  IT IS NOT A CREDIT SCORE AND MUST NOT BE DRESSED AS ONE. V2_03 is
  explicit that self-reported data cannot support lending. This model
  is substantially self-reported. It recommends stock; it does not
  assess anybody.

PART 16 — THE ENGINE'S FUEL: AN AUDIT OF THE BUSINESS PROFILE
===========================================================================

Read against the real form, 2026-09-14. The question for every field is
only this: DOES THE ENGINE USE IT? A field that fails that test is
costing completion rate and returning nothing.

16.1  THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST

  `products` -- the signal Part 3 called the strongest -- IS UNUSABLE
  FOR MOST SHOPS.

  PRODUCT_OPTIONS_BY_CATEGORY covers three of eight business
  categories. The other five, INCLUDING "RETAIL" -- which is what a
  spaza shop would most naturally pick -- fall through to:

      DEFAULT_PRODUCT_OPTIONS = ["Products", "Services", "Other"]

  So the app asks a shopkeeper what they sell and offers them the
  answer "Products". That is not a weak signal, it is no signal, and it
  is the single thing most worth fixing before any of this is built.

16.2  FIELD BY FIELD

  KEEP AS IS -- the engine reads these directly

    customer_types        INDIVIDUALS vs OTHER_BUSINESSES is exactly
                          the pack-size signal. Small, closed, fast.
    latitude/longitude    radius. Nothing else provides geography.
    city_or_town          the "Closest to Soweto" line, and a coarse
                          fallback when coordinates are missing.
    operating_days
    opening/closing       WHEN to surface a reorder nudge. The only
                          timing signal that exists.
    employee_range        the only volume proxy before orders exist.

  CHANGE -- right question, wrong shape

    products              REPLACE THE VOCABULARY. Ask against
                          ProductCategory itself -- the seventeen real
                          categories, with the icons already built --
                          not a per-category list of adjectives.
                          "Which of these do you BUY?" as chips is
                          faster to answer than the current picker AND
                          lands directly on the taxonomy the catalogue
                          uses. No mapping table, no vocabulary drift,
                          and no shop ever answers "Products".

                          IT ALSO CHANGES THE QUESTION FROM SELL TO
                          BUY, which is what the engine actually wants.
                          A salon SELLS haircuts and BUYS relaxers;
                          only the second is orderable here.

    supplier_sources      EITHER ask for NAMES (free text, "who do you
                          buy from?") and earn the acquisition-lead
                          claim, OR cut it. As a list of types it is a
                          weak cadence hint no other field needs. Names
                          are worth one text box; types are not worth a
                          screen.

    business_type
    category              Keep ONE. Two overlapping coarse
                          classifications are two chances to pick
                          differently and no extra signal.

  CUT -- nothing reads them and nothing will

    identity_verified
    documents_verified    Mandla's own review: informal businesses are
                          NOT verification-gated in V2. These are the
                          supplier model's concepts sitting on the
                          business table. The curtain applies to
                          suppliers; a business is not checked to shop.

    order_channels        How orders reach THEIR customers. Real for
                          them, invisible to the engine -- it changes
                          nothing about what they buy or when.

    payment_methods       How they take money from walk-ins. Not how
                          they pay suppliers, which is the only payment
                          question this platform acts on.

    years_operating       Weak for recommendation. Keep the COLUMN for
                          the lending thesis later; take it off the
                          form now. Data we do not use should not cost
                          a question.

    physical_address      Never shown in discovery (V2_16 Part 12), and
                          the coordinates carry the geography. Needed
                          for DELIVERY, so keep the column -- but ask
                          for it in the delivery flow, at the moment it
                          is used, not in an onboarding form.

  ADD -- worth more than everything cut

    restock rhythm        "How often do you restock?" -- daily, twice a
                          week, weekly, fortnightly, monthly. ONE tap.
                          The single most valuable thing the engine
                          could know on day one, and today it waits
                          months of order history to guess it.

    weekly spend band     A range, not a number. Sizes the basket
                          immediately and stops the feed showing a
                          R5,000 pallet to a shop that buys R800 a
                          week -- which is an unbuyable card, and V2_16
                          Part 12 already forbids those.

16.3  WHAT THE SCREEN BECOMES

  Before:  twelve fields, several unusable, verification concepts that
           do not apply, and "Products" as an available answer.
  After:   five questions, each of which visibly changes the feed.

      1  What do you buy?           category chips (multi-select)
      2  Who are your customers?    individuals / businesses
      3  How often do you restock?  one tap
      4  Roughly how much a week?   a band
      5  Where are you?             map pin, already built

  UNDER TWO MINUTES, which was the requirement. Every question earns
  its place by changing what the shop sees next.

16.4  WHEN TO ASK

  NOT AT REGISTRATION. A form between a person and the app they just
  installed is the highest-drop-off screen in any product, and at that
  moment they have no reason to believe the answers matter.

  ASK IN THE FEED, ONE QUESTION AT A TIME, AT THE MOMENT IT PAYS:

    empty feed          -> "What do you buy? We will show you stock for
                           it." One question, then the feed visibly
                           changes. That change is the argument for
                           answering the next one.
    browsing a category -> "Do you sell these? We will keep them handy."
    after a first order -> "How often do you restock this?"

  EACH ANSWER IMPROVES THE NEXT SCREEN, VISIBLY. That is the only
  honest reason to ask, and the only one that survives a shopkeeper who
  is busy.


===========================================================================
PART 17 — WHAT THE ENGINE NEEDS FROM SUPPLIERS: ALMOST NOTHING
===========================================================================

  Mandla's requirement: the injection engine must not know who the
  supplier is. Stated precisely, so it can be tested:

    THE ENGINE ASKS "WHAT DOES THIS SHOP NEED?" AND THE ANSWER NEVER
    CONTAINS A SUPPLIER.

  The answer is a set of CATALOGUE PRODUCTS with weights and urgency.
  Only afterwards, in a separate stage the engine does not participate
  in, does supply resolution ask "who can fulfil this, for this shop,
  from here".

  WHAT SUPPLY MUST EXPOSE TO THAT LATER STAGE -- a short list of FACTS,
  none of which is an identity:

    does an offer exist for this catalogue product
    is it in stock
    what does it cost
    how far away is it
    minimum order
    which payment rails
    delivery or collection

  NOTHING ABOUT WHO. Not the name, not the size of their catalogue, not
  how long they have been on the platform, not what they pay us. Those
  belong to the supplier's own screens and to commerce (sponsorship,
  V2_16 Part 8), which is injected as a LABELLED slot rather than mixed
  into the organic answer.

  THE TEST, and it is the same shape as the curtain and composer tests:
  no module under demand/ imports a supplier model, repository or
  service. If that import is ever needed, the design is wrong and the
  import is the symptom.

  WHY THIS IS WORTH THE DISCIPLINE. The moment the engine can see who
  the supplier is, every future feature has a cheap way to favour one:
  a "trusted partner" boost, a "premium supplier" weight, a tie-break
  on catalogue size. Each is individually defensible and together they
  are a marketplace that sells its own results. Not being able to SEE
  the supplier is what makes that impossible rather than merely
  discouraged.


===========================================================================
PART 18 — WHAT IF A WHOLESALER UPLOADS TEN THOUSAND PRODUCTS?
===========================================================================

  The analysis Mandla asked for. It is the right question: it is the
  first thing a serious supplier will do, and it is how feeds usually
  break.

  18.1  UNDER A SUPPLY-FIRST FEED (what was built first)

  Catastrophic and quiet. Candidates come from what exists, so more
  rows means more candidates and the biggest catalogue wins every rail
  without anybody intending it. The shop sees one wholesaler's shelf
  and calls it "the app".

  18.2  UNDER A DEMAND-FIRST ENGINE

  Structurally prevented, and this is the load-bearing property of the
  whole design. The engine asks "what does this shop need?" and gets
  back, say, eleven catalogue products. ELEVEN. That number comes from
  the SHOP, and no upload changes it.

  A supplier with ten thousand products can satisfy more of those
  eleven than a supplier with forty. They cannot make the shop need a
  twelfth. COVERAGE IS THE REWARD; DOMINATION IS NOT AVAILABLE.

  18.3  THE THREE RESIDUAL RISKS, AND THE ANSWERS

    COVERAGE MONOPOLY     One supplier is the only match for all
                          eleven, so every card is theirs -- honestly
                          earned, and still a feed that looks bought.
                          ANSWER: the per-supplier cap per section
                          (V2_16 7.4). Below the cap coverage wins;
                          above it the rail makes room.

    CATALOGUE POLLUTION   Ten thousand rows matched badly inflate a
                          supplier's apparent coverage. This is a
                          MATCHING problem, not a feed one, and the
                          review queue already exists for it -- but the
                          feed makes its consequences visible for the
                          first time, which is a reason to watch that
                          queue rather than to change the feed.

    PRICE FLOODING        Undercutting everything to own the
                          cheapest-offer slot. ANSWER: offer_selection
                          is not price-only (V2_16 Part 15 q3), price
                          sensitivity is per shop, and a supplier who
                          sustainably undercuts everyone is one the
                          shops should be seeing. That is a market
                          working, not a bug.

  18.4  WHAT WE SHOULD MEASURE FROM DAY ONE

    share of feed cards by supplier, per shop and platform-wide
    how many distinct suppliers appear in a typical feed
    coverage: what fraction of a shop's needs anyone can fulfil

  IF ONE SUPPLIER'S SHARE CLIMBS WITHOUT THEIR COVERAGE CLIMBING, the
  caps are wrong. A number to watch rather than a thing to assume --
  and cheap to compute once impressions exist.

PART A — TWO COMMUNITY TABS, ONE CODEBASE, KEPT APART
===========================================================================

  THERE ARE TWO FEEDS AND THEY ARE NOT THE SAME PRODUCT.

    BUSINESS "For You"     a MARKET. What can I buy, from whom, at what
                           price, near me. Driven by the demand engine.
                           Sections: buy it again, categories, collect
                           near you, moving fast.

    SUPPLIER "For You"     a DEMAND SIGNAL. Who is buying, who has gone
                           quiet, what is stopping me selling. Already
                           built: reorder_due, wants_to_connect,
                           businesses_near_you, shopfront_health.

  V2_04 3.1 already named the mistake of mirroring one onto the other:
  "a supplier does not shop. Handing them a For You rail of other
  suppliers is symmetry mistaken for design."

  THE RISK IS NOT CONCEPTUAL, IT IS FILENAMES. Today both live in
  network/community/services/ as business_sections.py and
  supplier_sections.py, side by side. Add a pipeline, sources, scoring
  and a demand engine to that flat folder and within a month somebody
  imports a business source into a supplier section because the import
  looked plausible. The tree has to make that mistake hard to make.

  A.1  THE REVISED TREE

  backend/src/domains/network/community/
  │  THE COMPOSER. Still owns no tables (architecture test), still
  │  imported by nobody.
  ├── api/community_routes.py           ONE endpoint, both roles. The
  │                                     role comes from the TOKEN.
  ├── feed_service.py                   the ONLY dispatcher. Reads the
  │                                     role, calls one side. Nothing
  │                                     else in the tree branches on
  │                                     role, ever.
  ├── core/                             ROLE-AGNOSTIC MACHINERY
  │   ├── candidate.py                  the Candidate type
  │   ├── eligibility.py                the one gate (curtain, demo,
  │   │                                 deleted, dedupe)
  │   ├── scoring.py                    the readable score
  │   ├── weights.py                    every tunable number
  │   └── engine.py                     the injection engine
  │                                     NOTHING HERE KNOWS WHAT A
  │                                     SUPPLIER OR A SHOP IS. It moves
  │                                     Candidates into sections.
  ├── business/                         THE SHOP'S FEED
  │   ├── sources/                      repeat_purchase, due_to_reorder,
  │   │                                 category_affinity,
  │   │                                 platform_demand, proximity,
  │   │                                 untried_suppliers, actions
  │   └── sections.py                   templates: source -> section
  └── supplier/                         THE WHOLESALER'S FEED
      ├── sources/                      customers_going_quiet,
      │                                 wants_to_connect,
      │                                 businesses_near_you,
      │                                 shopfront_health
      └── sections.py                   (today's supplier_sections.py,
                                        ported onto core/)

  backend/src/domains/business/demand/       THE DEMAND ENGINE
  │  Under `business` because it is a model OF a business. Its path
  │  says whose it is. Imports no supplier anything, ever.
  └── (as Book Three Part 13)

  A.2  THE NAMING RULE, so this cannot rot

    core/       may import NEITHER business/ NOR supplier/
    business/   may import core/, never supplier/
    supplier/   may import core/, never business/

  One architecture test asserts exactly that, in the same AST style as
  the composer and curtain tests. It is four assertions and it makes
  the mistake impossible rather than merely discouraged.

  A.3  WHAT IS SHARED AND WHAT IS NOT

  SHARED: the Candidate type, the eligibility gate, scoring, the
  engine, diversity caps, stability seeding. These are about arranging
  a screen and have no opinion about who is reading it.

  NOT SHARED: every source, every section template, every title. A
  supplier source answers "who among MY customers", a business source
  answers "what should I BUY". They read different tables, mean
  different things, and the moment one is reused for the other the
  result is the mirrored feed V2_04 already rejected.

  DO NOT FORCE THE SUPPLIER FEED THROUGH PRODUCT-SHAPED MACHINERY.
  Business candidates are mostly PRODUCTS; supplier candidates are
  mostly PEOPLE. The Candidate type already carries `kind` for exactly
  this, and porting the supplier feed onto core/ is worth doing only
  for the engine's arranging, thinning and stability -- not to make it
  look like the other one.


===========================================================================
PART B — FLAWS FOUND READING THIS PLAN BACK
===========================================================================

  B.1  DECLARED GIVES CATEGORIES, NOT PRODUCTS -- AND THE PLAN IMPLIED
       MORE PRECISION THAN THAT.

  Knowing a shop buys BAKERY does not say which bread. Book Three reads
  as though declaration produces product-level recommendations; it does
  not. It produces category weights, and the feed fills those with
  platform-popular products until orders say otherwise.

  FIX: state the ladder honestly. DECLARED -> category targeting.
  OBSERVED -> product targeting. Anything product-specific before the
  first order is a guess dressed as personalisation.

  B.2  A NEED WITH NO CATALOGUE PRODUCT DISAPPEARS SILENTLY.

  And we created one on purpose: DIGITAL_VAS was deliberately left out
  of the taxonomy (2026-09-13). A shop that declares airtime gets
  nothing back and no explanation.

  FIX: needs_service returns unmatched needs EXPLICITLY rather than
  dropping them. The feed can then say "nobody near you sells this
  yet", which is honest, and gap_service counts it, which is valuable.
  Silence is the one unacceptable outcome.

  B.3  A NIGHTLY REBUILD OF EVERY SHOP DOES NOT SCALE.

  FIX: rebuild only shops with activity since the last run, plus a slow
  rolling pass for decay. The derived model is a cache (Book Three Part
  7) so a stale one is a degradation, never a fault.

  B.4  OFFER SELECTION IS AN N+1 WAITING TO HAPPEN.

  Eleven needs, each asking "who has this, in stock, how far" is
  eleven-times-N queries. This exact bug has been written and removed
  twice in one week.

  FIX: offer_selection takes a LIST of catalogue product ids and
  returns a map, batched, once. Make the batch signature the only
  signature -- a single-product version will be used in a loop.

  B.5  RADIUS TOLERANCE IS MEANINGLESS WITHOUT COORDINATES.

  Most shops today have none (0 of 2 in dev).

  FIX: the model must carry confidence per field, not just overall, and
  every consumer must handle "unknown" rather than defaulting to a
  number that looks real.

  B.6  DEMO DATA AND THE DEMAND ENGINE INTERACT.

  A demo shop with no demand model shows the platform-wide fallback
  feed, which is exactly the boring screen a demonstration must avoid.

  FIX: the seeder builds demand models for demo shops too, and the demo
  filter applies to the model as well as the rows.

  B.7  THE INITIAL WEIGHTS ARE GUESSES AND THE PLAN DID NOT SAY SO.

  FIX: ship them as guesses, in one file, with a comment naming what
  would falsify each. Until impressions exist there is nothing to tune
  against, and pretending otherwise is how a number nobody chose
  becomes permanent.

  B.8  business_info_complete CHANGES MEANING IF THE FORM CHANGES.

  It counts today's twelve fields. Book Three Part 16 proposes five.

  FIX: recompute it from the new set in the same migration, or it
  silently reports completeness against fields that no longer exist.

  B.9  "UNSERVED" NEEDS A TIME LIMIT.

  A category flagged unserved because nobody stocked it two years ago
  should not still be flagged after three suppliers arrived.

  FIX: unserved is DERIVED on every rebuild, never stored as a
  permanent flag.

  B.10 THE SUPPLIER FEED HAS NO DEMAND ENGINE AND SHOULD NOT PRETEND TO.

  Book Three is entirely about shops. The supplier equivalent ("what
  should I stock?") is a real idea and a different one -- it needs
  aggregate demand near them, not a model of themselves.

  FIX: out of scope, named as out of scope, with its own document when
  it comes. Part 15 q6 stays open.

PART C — WHAT THE CODEBASE NEEDS BEFORE ANY ENGINE CODE
===========================================================================

  Ordered. Each is small, each is independently shippable, and none
  requires the engine to exist.

  C.1  RESHAPE THE BUSINESS PROFILE (Book Three Part 16)

    - `products` (free-ish strings) -> `buys_categories`
      (ProductCategory[]), asked against the seventeen real categories

    - DROP order_channels, payment_methods from the FORM

    the database. This will never be cheaper, and every week of
    signups makes it a data migration instead of a schema change.

  C.2  CATALOGUE-WIDE PRODUCT BROWSE (Book Two Part 3.1)

    The single missing query. Blocks the category chips, "moving fast",
    and every product rail that is not pure order history. Nothing in
    the engine is useful without it.

  C.3  THE CHIP DESTINATION

    Tapping "Drinks" currently routes to SUPPLIER search. It should
    land on products. Biggest visible improvement available to a new
    user, and it needs only C.2.

  C.4  MOVE THE FEED INTO THE ROLE-SEPARATED TREE (Part A)

    business_sections.py and supplier_sections.py move into
    business/ and supplier/. Pure refactor, no behaviour change, and
    it must happen BEFORE the tree fills up rather than after.

  C.5  THE ARCHITECTURE TESTS, WRITTEN BEFORE THE CODE THEY GUARD

    - core/ imports neither role (A.2)
    - demand/ imports no supplier anything (Book Three Part 11)
    - no query reads demo rows outside the gate (Book Three Part 10)

    Written first, they shape the code. Written after, they are an
    argument with code that already exists.

  C.6  FIX WHAT THE FIRST FEED GOT WRONG (Book One)

    collect_near_you stops excluding connected suppliers; untried
    suppliers includes unconnected ones. Small, and it stops the
    current feed being actively worse than the mock it replaced.

  WHAT IS NOT ON THIS LIST, deliberately: impressions, sponsorship,
  scoring, the demand model itself. All of them are easier once C.1 to
  C.6 exist, and none of them is blocked by anything except C.2.
