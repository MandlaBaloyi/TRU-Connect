PART 1 — WHAT ALREADY EXISTS, SO WE BUILD ON IT RATHER THAN OVER IT
===========================================================================

More is standing than the empty screens suggest.

  community/types.ts          A server-driven feed contract. The SERVER
                              chooses which sections appear and in what
                              order; the app renders what it is handed and
                              knows nothing about the ordering rules.
                              Unknown section types render nothing, so the
                              server can ship a new one before every
                              installed build understands it.

  Section types today         CATEGORY_CHIPS, SUPPLIER_AVATARS,
                              SUPPLIER_RAIL, PRODUCT_RAIL, PROMO,
                              SPONSORED_SUPPLIER.

  MIN_ITEMS_PER_RAIL = 3      A thin rail looks worse than no rail.
                              Enforced in the app, not trusted from the
                              server.

  Components                  ProductTile, SupplierCard, SupplierAvatarTile,
                              PromoCard, CategoryChipRow, Rail,
                              FeedSectionView, CommunityFeed, CartButton.

  Search                      Lives in the top bar on both roles.
                              InlineSupplierSearch / InlineBusinessSearch.

  My Connections              Shared component, both roles,
                              shared/connections/.

  Backing                     mockCommunityFeed today; communityFeedService
                              is the seam to the real endpoint.

THAT CONTRACT IS THE MOST VALUABLE THING IN THE APP AND NOTHING BELOW
CHANGES IT. Everything proposed here is either a new section TYPE or a new
rule for choosing sections — both server-side, both shippable without an
app release. That property is what lets a wholesaler's range be placed
above the fold during a negotiation instead of two weeks after it.


===========================================================================
PART 2 — THE BUSINESS'S COMMUNITY: AN E-COMMERCE SURFACE ABOUT SUPPLIERS
===========================================================================

The brief: it must feel like e-commerce, centred on suppliers — search,
For You, My Connections, invite, cart, supplier profile — and it is where
the supplier↔business relationship is optimised.

2.1 THE ORGANISING IDEA

  Uber Eats organises around restaurants. Takealot organises around
  products. TruConnect organises around SUPPLIERS, because a business does
  not buy one item — it buys a basket from one supplier, on that
  supplier's minimum, on that supplier's delivery day. A product rail that
  scatters across six suppliers produces six baskets, six minimums and six
  deliveries, which is worse than useless in a shop.

  So: PRODUCTS ARE THE BAIT, SUPPLIERS ARE THE UNIT. Every product tile
  names its supplier and every path through the feed converges on a
  supplier's shopfront.

2.2 THE FEED, SECTION BY SECTION

  The server composes these. Order is illustrative, not hardcoded.

  1  BUY IT AGAIN                          new section type: PRODUCT_RAIL
     Products this business has ordered before, from suppliers they are
     still connected to. THE HIGHEST-VALUE RAIL IN THE APP and the one
     the research names first ("their own prices"). It is also the
     cheapest to compute: their own order history.
     Empty for a new user. That is correct — it fills on order two.

  2  YOUR SUPPLIERS                        SUPPLIER_AVATARS
     Existing connections, as a row of avatars. The fastest path back to
     a shopfront they already trust.

  3  CATEGORY CHIPS                        CATEGORY_CHIPS
     The 16 categories from taxonomy.py. See Part 4 on imagery.

  4  PRICE DROPPED ON WHAT YOU BUY         new: PRICE_MOVEMENT_RAIL
     A catalogue product this business buys, now cheaper — either from
     their own supplier or from another one they could reach.
     ONLY THE CATALOGUE MAKES THIS POSSIBLE. "WHT SUGR 2.5KG" from one
     supplier and "White Sugar 2.5kg" from another resolve to the same
     catalogue product, so the comparison is real rather than a string
     match. No competitor without a resolved catalogue can show this.
     HANDLE WITH CARE — see 2.4.

  5  NEW NEAR YOU                          SUPPLIER_RAIL
     Suppliers within delivery range not yet connected. distanceKm is
     already in the contract and marked NOT YET REAL; both sides have
     coordinates, so it is a query rather than a data model.

  6  SPONSORED                             SPONSORED_SUPPLIER
     Already in the contract, deliberately built before it is sold.

  7  COMPLETE YOUR BASKET                  new: PRODUCT_RAIL
     Only when a cart is open and below the supplier's minimum order.
     "R180 more to reach MacMan's R500 minimum" — and only that
     supplier's products. Directly attacks abandoned baskets caused by
     minimums, which is a real and specific failure in this market.

2.3 MY CONNECTIONS IS A RELATIONSHIP LEDGER, NOT A CONTACT LIST

  The brief is right that suppliers a business has bought from belong
  here. Going further: this list should be ordered by TRADING REALITY,
  not alphabetically.

  Per row:  supplier avatar and name (real photo — Part C of the earlier
            community plan)
            last ordered "3 weeks ago"
            typical reorder gap, when there is enough history
            an outstanding order, if one is open

  Ordered by: open order first, then reorder-due, then recency.

  WHY THIS MATTERS MORE THAN IT LOOKS. A shop's buying is rhythmic —
  bread twice a week, cooldrink on a Thursday, cleaning monthly. A list
  that surfaces "you usually reorder from Sipho about now" is doing the
  job the shopkeeper currently does from memory, and memory is what
  fails when they are busy. This is a retention mechanic disguised as a
  list.

2.4 THE ONE PLACE TO BE CAREFUL: PRICE COMPARISON

  Showing a business that their current supplier is more expensive is
  the most powerful thing the catalogue can do and the fastest way to
  destroy the platform's relationship with suppliers.

  THE RULE: comparison is always framed as SAVING FOR THE BUYER, never as
  a league table of suppliers. No "cheapest supplier" ranking, no
  supplier-versus-supplier screen. A price-drop rail shows a product and
  a price. It never says "your supplier is overcharging you."

  A supplier who feels ambushed by our UI leaves, and a marketplace with
  no suppliers has nothing to compare. This restraint is commercial, not
  squeamish.

2.5 CART AND CHECKOUT

  Cart is per supplier. It already is, structurally (checkout.stock_orders
  carries a supplier_profile_id), and it must stay that way for the
  reasons in 2.1.

  The cart shows, at all times: subtotal, the supplier's minimum and the
  gap to it, delivery or collection, and which payment methods that
  supplier accepts. Every one of those is already a real field on
  SupplierProfile. Nothing is estimated.


===========================================================================
PART 3 — THE SUPPLIER'S COMMUNITY: THE ANSWER TO "I DON'T HAVE AN IDEA"
===========================================================================

This is the part of the brief with no answer yet, so here is a proposed
one, argued rather than asserted.

3.1 THE MISTAKE TO AVOID

  Today the supplier's Community is a direct mirror of the business's:
  For You, My Connections, search. That is why it feels like nothing.
  A supplier does not shop. Handing them a "For You" rail of other
  suppliers is symmetry mistaken for design, and it is exactly the
  generic-admin-list problem that started V2.

3.2 THE ORGANISING IDEA

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  For the business, Community is a MARKET.
  For the supplier, Community is a DEMAND SIGNAL.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  A supplier's questions are not "what can I buy" but:

      Who is buying from me, and who stopped?
      Who nearby could buy from me and does not?
      What are they asking for that I do not sell?

  Answer those three and the tab is indispensable. Two of them are
  answerable ONLY because the catalogue exists.

3.3 THE FEED, SECTION BY SECTION

  1  ASKED FOR NEARBY, NOT IN YOUR RANGE    new: DEMAND_GAP_RAIL
     ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
     THE SINGLE MOST VALUABLE THING TRUCONNECT CAN TELL A SUPPLIER.

     Catalogue products that businesses within this supplier's delivery
     area searched for or ordered, for which this supplier has no offer.
     "14 shops near you bought 2kg maize meal this month. You don't
     stock it."

     This is a stock recommendation derived from real local demand, and
     it is worth money. It is only computable because matching resolves
     every supplier's messy line item onto a shared catalogue product —
     without that, "maize meal" is a string, not a fact.

     PRIVACY, NON-NEGOTIABLE: aggregate only, minimum threshold before
     a row appears (propose 5 distinct businesses), never named
     businesses, never a specific shop's basket. Principle 2 in V2_03.
     If we cannot hit the threshold, the row does not exist.

  2  REORDER DUE                            new: CUSTOMER_RAIL
     Connected businesses whose usual reorder window has passed. The
     supplier's own customers, their own trading history — no privacy
     question at all. This is the row that turns the app into a sales
     tool rather than an inbox.

  3  WANTS TO CONNECT                       existing requests, surfaced
     Pending connection requests. Already built
     (supplier/customer-requests) but currently lives on Home.

  4  BUSINESSES NEAR YOU                    new: BUSINESS_RAIL
     Shops in the delivery area with no connection. The growth surface.
     Subject to the same visibility rules the business profile already
     enforces (_resolve_visible_photo_url and friends).

  5  YOUR SHOPFRONT                         new: STOREFRONT_HEALTH
     A single honest card: is the shopfront open, how many offers have
     no price, how many import lines are unmatched, is the minimum order
     set. Links straight into Account. Not vanity metrics — the specific
     things blocking them from being findable.

3.4 MY CONNECTIONS, SUPPLIER SIDE

  This is the supplier's CUSTOMER BOOK. Same component, different
  ordering: by trading value and by risk of going quiet.

  Per row: business name and photo, last order, typical gap, whether
  anything is outstanding.

  A supplier who opens this and sees "Thabo's Corner Shop — usually
  orders weekly, nothing for 3 weeks" has been handed a phone call worth
  making. That is a feature no ratings screen can match.

PART 4 — CATEGORY IMAGERY, AND THE LINE THAT MUST NOT BE CROSSED
===========================================================================

The decision: TruConnect owns imagery for its 16 categories. Suppliers
choose which category art represents their shopfront, with sensible
defaults. Supplier product photography becomes an enhancement, never a
prerequisite.

WHY IT WORKS, AND WHY IT IS THE CATALOGUE AGAIN. ProductCategory in
catalogue/products/models/taxonomy.py is a closed set of 16, shared
verbatim by both codebases. Matching already maps every supplier offer
onto a catalogue product, and that product carries its category. So a
supplier who uploads a price file with no images at all still produces a
fully-rendered shopfront: we already know what each line IS. They are
asked for nothing.

Bounded cost: 16 categories, one to three images each. Sourced once.
Against an unbounded number of supplier photos we can neither commission
nor control.

THE FALLBACK CHAIN, one helper, used everywhere, no screen inventing its
own rule:

    product photo  →  brand mark  →  category art  →  typographic block

THE LINE:

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Category art may carry CHROME. It may never carry PRODUCT IDENTITY.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  ALLOWED: category chips, rail headers, empty states, shopfront covers,
  cart section dividers, Discovery cards.

  FORBIDDEN: the image slot of a tile that states a specific product at a
  specific price.

  The reason is trust, not taste. If a business sees a photograph of a
  particular brand of maize meal and receives a different one, that is
  not a disappointing image — it is the platform having misled them about
  what they were buying, in the one transaction where they are spending
  real money. In the product slot the honest fallback is the brand mark,
  and failing that a typographic block. Never a photograph of something
  adjacent.

  A supplier choosing a category cover for their own shopfront is on the
  safe side of that line: they are decorating their shop, not making a
  claim about an item.

AND TWO CATEGORIES ARE MISSING, raised in review 2026-09-11 and worth
deciding BEFORE the enum has data behind it -- the category lives on the
catalogue PRODUCT, so adding a value later means a migration plus
re-sorting everything already categorised:

  BEAUTY AND HAIR. Extensions, braids, relaxers, nails. An enormous
  township category and exactly the V2_03 Part 1 user -- a salon buying
  stock. PERSONAL_CARE stretches to cover it but does not describe it,
  and a supplier browsing for where to list their range will not find
  themselves in "personal care".

  DIGITAL / VAS. Airtime, data, electricity, vouchers. V2_08's entire
  argument is that a VAS distributor is a supplier whose products are
  digital -- and there is currently no category those products belong
  to.

  ALSO WORTH WATCHING RATHER THAN FIXING: OTHER exists, so nothing is
  unlistable, which is the right design. But OTHER filling up is the
  signal that the taxonomy is wrong. Track its share as a metric.

  DEPTH IS DELIBERATELY FLAT. Sixteen top-level categories with no
  sub-categories makes FOOD_GROCERY enormous for browsing. That is
  acceptable for MVP, and a hierarchy can be added later as a SEPARATE
  field without touching the enum -- so it is not a decision that needs
  making now.

LICENSING, WHICH WILL BE ASKED ABOUT LATER SO RECORD IT NOW. Every asset
gets source URL, licence name, licence URL and the date acquired, stored
with the asset rather than in someone's memory. Free-to-use is not
licence-free, and an investor or a rights-holder asking in eighteen
months is a question with a cheap answer today and an expensive one then.

DELIVERY: Cloudinary, which is already the media provider, with the
category value as the public id (`categories/FOOD_GROCERY/...`) so a
lookup needs no table join and no app release to add one.

PART 5 — WHAT THE SERVER NEEDS TO BUILD
===========================================================================

New backend work implied by the above, named so it can be scheduled.

  GET /community/feed?role=business        composes sections per user
  GET /community/feed?role=supplier        composes sections per user

  Both are one endpoint per role that returns the existing FeedSection
  union plus the new types. The composition rules live in a single
  service so ordering is one file, not scattered.

  New section types:  PRICE_MOVEMENT_RAIL, DEMAND_GAP_RAIL,
                      CUSTOMER_RAIL, BUSINESS_RAIL, STOREFRONT_HEALTH

  New computations:
    buy-it-again        from stock_orders history           (cheap)
    reorder gap         mean interval per (business, supplier)  (cheap)
    price movement      catalogue product → offers → history  (medium)
    demand gap          area demand minus this supplier's offers
                        (medium; needs the aggregate threshold)
    distance            both sides already carry coordinates  (cheap)

  Where it lives: a new domain, src/domains/network/community/, because
  the feed spans supplier, business, network and catalogue and belongs to
  none of them. It READS from those domains' repositories and owns no
  tables of its own except, later, impression logging.

  THE ARCHITECTURE RULE IT MUST OBEY: the feed service may import from
  domain repositories, but no domain may import the feed service. It is a
  composer, and the coordinator never gets imported by the thing it
  coordinates — the same rule tests/architecture/ already enforces for
  shared/finance and shared/ai. Add a boundary test for it in the same
  commit that creates it, not later.

PART 6 — OPEN QUESTIONS FOR REVIEW
===========================================================================

  1  DEMAND GAP THRESHOLD. Proposed: a row appears only when at least 5
     distinct businesses in the area bought the product in 30 days. Is 5
     right? Too low and it identifies a shop; too high and it never fires
     in a thin area.

  2  PRICE MOVEMENT — OWN SUPPLIER ONLY, OR ALL REACHABLE? Showing a
     cheaper price at a supplier they are NOT connected to is the most
     valuable version for the business and the most dangerous for
     supplier relations. Recommendation: MVP shows movement only within
     suppliers they are already connected to. Widen later, deliberately,
     with suppliers told in advance.

  3  DOES THE SUPPLIER SEE "REORDER DUE" BEFORE THE BUSINESS DOES? If a
     supplier is prompted to call a shop the same morning the shop is
     prompted to reorder, one of them feels watched. Recommendation:
     the business sees their own prompt; the supplier's row appears only
     after the window has clearly passed.

  4  CATEGORY ART STYLE. RESOLVED 2026-09-11, and the answer was better
     than either option on offer.

     The original choice was photographic (looks best, edges toward the
     forbidden line) versus illustrated/flat (safe, looks cheaper). The
     resolution dissolves it:

       PHOTOGRAPH A SECTION OR A SCENE, NEVER A SINGLE ITEM.

     A photograph of the fruit-and-veg section of a store cannot be
     mistaken for a claim about one product -- it is visibly a place,
     not a thing. A photograph of one bag of a specific brand of maize
     meal reads as a promise about what arrives.

     So we get photographic quality with none of the trust risk, and
     the rule is easy to apply when sourcing: if you can name the brand
     in the picture, it is the wrong picture.
