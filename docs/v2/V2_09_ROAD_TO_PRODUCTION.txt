===========================================================================
V2_09 — THE ROAD TO PRODUCTION
       CIPC · Play Store · FNB App of the Year · hosting
===========================================================================

Written 2026-09-11. Depends on V2_03 and V2_07.

Two goals, with a target of roughly two months:

  1. TruConnect live on the Google Play Store.
  2. Entered into FNB App of the Year.

The reason for the second is capital. There is not enough to fund the
app and the web at once, and an award that puts a South African app
solving a South African problem in front of judges and press is a
cheaper route to an investor conversation than cold outreach.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  THE DATE IS A TARGET, NOT A CONSTRAINT, and this was stated plainly:
  "we dont focus on spend, we focus on solving the problem... its
  better to take more tym to build the app, than building an app in
  short tym that has vulnerability, can be copied, can cause problem."

  That reorders everything below. A schedule slip is recoverable. A
  breach, a wrong price on a real order, or an unvetted product
  reaching a real shop is not. Where the two conflict, the date gives
  way -- and this document should be read as an ORDER OF WORK with a
  target attached, never as a list to cut until it fits.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

This document is mostly about sequencing and about four findings that
change the plan. Every fact below was checked rather than assumed, and
the sources are at the end.


===========================================================================
PART 1 — FOUR FINDINGS, IN ORDER OF HOW MUCH THEY MATTER
===========================================================================

1.1 THE APP'S PACKAGE NAME IS STILL THE EXPO PLACEHOLDER

  frontend/mobile/app.json today:

      "name":    "mobile"
      "slug":    "mobile"
      "package": "com.anonymous.mobile"

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  A PACKAGE NAME IS PERMANENT. Once an app is published under it, it
  can never be changed -- not by renaming, not by support request. The
  only remedy is a NEW listing, with zero installs, zero reviews and a
  new URL.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  "com.anonymous.mobile" would also very likely be rejected outright,
  and it is the default placeholder every Expo project starts with, so
  it reads to a reviewer exactly as what it is.

  FIX BEFORE ANY UPLOAD, and it is five minutes:
      name     "TruConnect"
      slug     "truconnect"
      package  "co.za.truconnect" or "com.truconnect.app"
      plus a real versionCode and a permanent iOS bundle identifier,
      chosen now even though iOS comes later -- same permanence rule.

  This is first in this document because it is the only item that
  cannot be undone after the fact.

1.2 FNB DOES NOT REQUIRE THE APP TO BE ON A STORE

  From the competition's own FAQ: an app need not be published. What
  the judges need is REMOTE ACCESS -- "we will need all login details
  in order to use the app" -- and a submission "complete to the point
  where the judges need to be able to use it".

  THIS DECOUPLES THE TWO DEADLINES, which looked joined and are not.
  The store timeline and the award timeline are independent. An
  internal build plus demo credentials satisfies FNB; the store queue
  does not gate it.

  Judging is described as two phases plus an awards evening, with a
  three-stage process for finalists assessing creativity, feasibility,
  technical execution and impact, ending in a live pitch. The 2025
  round has closed; 2027 dates are not published yet, so the entry
  window is the thing to watch rather than plan around.

1.3 THE GOOGLE PLAY TESTING RULE — AND THE WAY AROUND IT

  A PERSONAL developer account created after 13 November 2023 must run
  a closed test with at least 12 testers, opted in CONTINUOUSLY for 14
  days, before it can even apply for production access. Testers who
  opt out and back in restart the clock.

  On a sub-two-month timeline that is not a formality. It is fourteen
  days of calendar, plus the recruiting, plus the application review
  afterwards -- and it happens at the END, when everything else has
  already slipped.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  ORGANISATION ACCOUNTS REGISTERED TO A LEGAL BUSINESS ENTITY ARE
  EXEMPT FROM THE REQUIREMENT ENTIRELY.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  RESOLVED 2026-09-11: CIPC registration was always the plan and is a
  prerequisite for production regardless of Google -- it was simply not
  written down here. So the Play account is opened as an ORGANISATION
  and this requirement does not apply at all.

  That removes the only immovable object the schedule had. The 14-day
  tester clock is no longer on the critical path.

  AND IT IS NOT ONLY ABOUT GOOGLE. The entity is the bottom of the
  trust stack this whole product rests on:

      CIPC entity        who we legally are
      domain             truconnect.co.za, owned by that entity
      domain email       not a gmail address on a supplier contract
      Play listing       published by a registered company
      operator contracts POPIA requires a real party to sign them
      verification       we ask suppliers for documents (V2_03 Part 4).
                         Asking that while trading as an individual with
                         a free email address is not a position we can
                         hold.

  The last line is the one that matters. A platform that verifies its
  suppliers has to be verifiable itself.

1.4 TARGET API LEVEL

  New apps must target Android 15 (API 35) or higher; the deadline for
  existing apps was 31 August 2026, now passed. Expo 57 with React
  Native 0.86 targets a current API level, so this should be satisfied
  already -- but it is a build-output check, not an assumption, and it
  is checked once in five minutes.

PART 2 — THE WEB, AND THE PASSWORD-RESET QUESTION
===========================================================================

The open question: a reset ticket is emailed; the link goes to the web;
should it go to the app instead, or both?

THE ANSWER IS BOTH, AND IT IS A SOLVED PROBLEM. Android App Links (and
iOS Universal Links) make one HTTPS URL do two jobs:

    tapped with the app installed      -> opens the app, on the reset
                                          screen, with the ticket
    tapped without it                  -> opens the web page

No second link, no "open in app?" prompt, no choice for the user to get
wrong. It needs:

  · a verified domain, which we need anyway
  · /.well-known/assetlinks.json on it (Android)
  · /.well-known/apple-app-site-association (iOS, later)
  · intentFilters in app.json -- NOT present today, and the app has
    only a custom scheme ("truconnect"), which is not the same thing.
    A custom scheme cannot be verified, can be hijacked by any other
    app claiming it, and is not what an email client will follow.

THE WEB PAGE IS STILL REQUIRED, and that is the honest answer to
"do we need the web at all". Somebody will open that email on a desktop,
or on a phone with the app uninstalled, and the fallback has to exist.
But it is ONE PAGE, not a product.

  WHAT THE WEB MUST SHIP FOR PRODUCTION
    · the reset-password page (already exists, and the backend already
      has the bounce route)
    · assetlinks.json
    · terms and privacy policy -- Play requires a reachable privacy
      policy URL, and POPIA requires it independently
    · a landing page, because a Play listing pointing at nothing looks
      abandoned

  WHAT IT MUST NOT SHIP: the supplier portal. It exists, it builds, it
  is not deployed, and per the earlier decision it stays that way. The
  only cost of keeping it is drift, and `npm run build` catches that
  in ten seconds.


===========================================================================
PART 3 — HOSTING
===========================================================================

The shape proposed: Cloudflare in front, AWS behind.

  CLOUDFLARE      DNS, TLS, CDN, DDoS protection, WAF. The free tier
                  covers all of it at our scale. It also terminates
                  TLS, which means the origin can be simpler.

  AWS             the Flask API and PostgreSQL.

  REGION: af-south-1 (Cape Town). Two independent reasons, and the
  second is the one that matters:

    LATENCY. Every user is in South Africa. eu-west-1 adds roughly
    150-180ms per round trip to people already on a slow connection.

    DATA RESIDENCY. POPIA does not forbid cross-border transfer, but it
    conditions it -- section 72 requires comparable protection or
    consent. Keeping personal information in-country removes an entire
    category of question from any future investor or partner due
    diligence, and costs nothing to choose on day one. Choosing it
    later means a migration.

  START SMALL AND HONESTLY: one modest EC2 instance or App Runner
  service, plus RDS PostgreSQL with automated backups. The load
  arithmetic from the C++ discussion still holds -- at 15,000 active
  businesses placing two orders a week the system sees about 0.05
  orders per second. This is not a scale problem yet, and provisioning
  as though it were is how a company with no capital spends it.

  WHAT IS ALREADY EXTERNAL, and therefore not our infrastructure:
  Cloudinary for media, PayFast for payments, Mapbox for geocoding,
  Anthropic for the AI tier (off by default).


===========================================================================
PART 4 — POPIA, THE PARTS THAT ARE ACTUALLY BLOCKING
===========================================================================

POPIA applies to anyone processing the personal information of people
in South Africa. Most of what it asks for is documentation rather than
engineering, but three items are real work.

  INFORMATION OFFICER. Must be designated and registered with the
  Information Regulator. For a one-person company that is you. It is a
  form, and it is required.

  OPERATOR AGREEMENTS. POPIA requires a WRITTEN CONTRACT with every
  operator processing on our behalf -- AWS, Cloudflare, Cloudinary,
  PayFast, Anthropic. All of them publish standard data-processing
  addenda; they need to be accepted and filed, not negotiated.

  ACCOUNT DELETION ACTUALLY WORKING. This is the engineering one, and
  it is already a known defect: the foreign-key audit on 2026-09-08
  found supplier and business rows referenced by NO ACTION foreign keys
  across ten tables, so "delete my account" does not currently complete
  cleanly. A data-subject deletion request is a legal obligation with a
  deadline attached, and discovering this when one arrives is the worst
  possible time.

  Also required and mostly written already: a privacy policy that
  matches the Play Data Safety form. Those two disagreeing is a common
  rejection reason and an easy one to avoid -- fill the form FROM the
  policy, not separately.

PART 5 — THE ORDER OF WORK
===========================================================================

REVISED 2026-09-11. This part was originally "what fits in two months
and what does not", which was the wrong shape once the date became a
target rather than a constraint. It is now an ORDER. Everything on it
gets built; the only question is what comes first, and nothing ships
half-finished to hit a date.

V2_07 lists eleven MVP items (A-K). Grouped by what they are for:

  GROUP 1 — THE APP LOOKS AND FEELS REAL
    B   category imagery + the fallback chain
    D   My Connections as a relationship ledger
    A   business Community feed on real data (replaces the mock)
    E   Home, both roles
    F   Stock Orders into the Account tab

  GROUP 2 — NOBODY CAN GET HURT
    K   the supplier curtain, BOTH gates (identity + catalogue)
    +   account deletion that actually completes (Part 4)
    +   a security pass before anything is public

    THIS GROUP IS NOT NEGOTIABLE AND NOT COMPRESSIBLE. It is the
    difference between a slip and an incident. Group 1 without Group 2
    is an app that looks finished and is not safe to show anyone.

  GROUP 3 — IT IS A REAL PRODUCT, NOT A BUILD
    +   app.json identity (1.1) -- before ANY upload, it is permanent
    +   App Links + the reset page (Part 2)
    +   privacy policy, Data Safety form, operator contracts (Part 4)
    +   deploy: af-south-1, Cloudflare, backups that are restored once
        rather than merely configured

  GROUP 4 — THE REST OF THE MVP
    C   supplier Community feed
    G   Cash Up
    I   Discovery: announcements + opportunities
    H   API keys screen
    J   the More audit

  DEFERRED BY DESIGN: all of V2_08 (third-party layer), the
  price-movement rail, YouTube ingestion, shelf list, group buying.

THE ONE EXCEPTION WORTH ARGUING ABOUT. FNB judges on seven criteria,
one of which is "INTELLIGENCE: predictive capabilities using data
analysis". The demand-gap rail -- telling a supplier what shops near
them buy that the supplier does not stock -- is exactly that, and it is
the only feature in the plan that is genuinely hard to copy, because it
needs the resolved catalogue underneath it.

It is post-MVP because it needs data volume to be non-embarrassing. A
JUDGED DEMO does not have that constraint: seeded catalogue data shows
the idea without a live user base. Worth building as a demo path behind
a flag.

  AND IT SPEAKS TO THE COPYING CONCERN DIRECTLY. What cannot be copied
  quickly is not the screens -- anyone can rebuild a rail. It is the
  resolved catalogue: two suppliers' messy line items collapsing to one
  product, at 80% with zero wrong links, built out of real measurement
  over weeks. That is months of work behind a competitor, and it is
  already done. The features that sit on top of it are the moat's
  visible part, not the moat.

PART 6 — SEQUENCE
===========================================================================

  NOW, AND MOSTLY NOT CODE
    · CIPC registration (decided -- see 1.3)
    · domain + domain email on the entity
    · app.json identity fix (1.1) -- permanent, so do it before
      anything is ever uploaded anywhere
    · Play Console account as an ORGANISATION
    · point the domain at Cloudflare

  THEN, IN THIS ORDER
    1  Group 1 (B, D, A, E, F) -- the app becomes something you would
       show a stranger
    2  Group 2 (K, deletion, security pass) -- it becomes something you
       could let a stranger USE
    3  Group 3 -- it becomes a product with a listing, a policy and a
       backup that has been restored at least once
    4  FNB entry, from an internal build with demo credentials.
       Independent of the store queue (1.2), so it can happen as soon
       as Groups 1-2 are done
    5  Group 4, as the weeks allow
    6  Store submission when Groups 1-3 are genuinely finished, not
       when a calendar says so

  WHAT STILL HAS NO OWNER, and it is the honest gap in this plan: the
  catalogue review queue (K) needs a PERSON looking at it, every day,
  once suppliers are real. Everything else in this document is code
  that runs itself. Early on that person is the developer, assisted by
  AI that flags rather than clears (V2_03 Part 4.3) -- but the answer
  cannot be "the founder, forever".


===========================================================================
PART 7 — THE ADMIN CENTRE IS NOT MVP. THE REVIEW MECHANISM IS.
===========================================================================

These are two different things and separating them is the whole answer.

  THE CONTROL CENTRE -- queues, dashboards, audit views, more than one
  reviewer. NOT MVP. It earns its place when the queue is daily rather
  than weekly, and when it arrives it lands in the web codebase that
  already exists and builds. That is the real reason to keep the portal
  alive rather than delete it.

  THE ABILITY TO APPROVE OR REJECT -- absolutely MVP, because the
  curtain (V2_07 item K) blocks every supplier until somebody can act.
  Without it, nobody can be onboarded at all.

CAN CLOUDINARY DO THE REVIEWING? PARTIALLY, AND IT IS A FAIR STOPGAP.
Its Media Library is a real interface for LOOKING at uploaded documents
and images, so the viewing problem is solved for free. What it cannot
do is record the DECISION, which has to land in our database as a
status change plus an audit row.

  SO THE CHEAP HONEST VERSION IS: Cloudinary to look, a management
  command to decide.

      flask verification approve <supplier-id> --note "..."

  Roughly a day's work, and at MVP volume -- tens of suppliers, not
  thousands -- it is genuinely sufficient rather than merely tolerable.

  ONE CONDITION TO HOLD FIRM ON: those commands go through the same
  SERVICE layer that writes the audit events. Never raw SQL against
  production. A human changing rows by hand with no trail is how you
  end up unable to answer "who approved this supplier, and when" --
  which is precisely the question asked after something goes wrong, and
  the reason the audit domain exists at all.
