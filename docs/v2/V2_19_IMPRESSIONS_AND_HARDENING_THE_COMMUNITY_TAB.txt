===========================================================================
PART 1 — IMPRESSIONS: WHAT THE FEED SHOWED, AND WHAT WAS TAPPED
===========================================================================

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1.1  WHY THIS IS THE NEXT THING
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  The demand engine reads DECLARED answers and CONFIRMED orders. It has
  no idea what a shopkeeper LOOKED AT.

  Every number tuned across V2_15, V2_16 and V2_17 carries a comment
  naming what would falsify it, and almost all of them say the same
  thing: "visible the day impressions exist".

      interleave.py       the 40% proportional rail ceiling
      tier_rules.py       TOLERANCE_KM = 2.0, the tie-break band
      tier_rules.py       ABSOLUTE_CAP_BY_TIER, the one-card steps
      velocity.py         the moving-fast window

  NOT ONE OF THEM CAN CURRENTLY BE PROVED WRONG. They are careful
  guesses, labelled as guesses. Impressions are what turn the feed from
  DESIGNED into MEASURED.

  IT ALSO UNBLOCKS THE ONLY REVENUE MECHANISM THAT EXISTS. The sponsored
  slot is picked alphabetically and nobody can be billed for it, because
  no advertiser can be shown what they got.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1.2  THE ONE HARD DECISION: GRAIN
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  A feed render is roughly nine rails of up to twelve cards -- about a
  hundred cards. Thirty shops opening the tab three times a day is 9,000
  rows a day in development. Ten thousand shops is THREE MILLION A DAY.
  Raw event rows would be the largest table in the system within a month
  and would answer nothing that a counter does not.

  THE GRAIN IS ONE ROW PER (shop, day, rail, position, item), with two
  counters: delivered and tapped.

  That bounds the table by DISTINCT COMBINATIONS rather than by traffic,
  and it still answers every question the falsifiers ask:

      tap rate by rail          needs rail
      tap rate by POSITION      needs position   <- the 2km falsifier
      seen but never bought     needs item
      sponsored billing         needs supplier
      "the same wholesaler everywhere"

  POSITION IS NOT OPTIONAL. tier_rules' own falsifier is "a tap-rate gap
  between the first and second card of a banded pair". Without position
  that sentence is unanswerable and the band can never be argued with.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1.3  TWO HALVES, WRITTEN DIFFERENTLY
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  DELIVERED IS RECORDED SERVER-SIDE, at compose time, through
  shared/queue so it never slows the response. The composer already
  knows exactly what it returned: no client to trust, no extra round
  trip, and no double counting when a screen re-renders.

  AND IT IS CALLED "delivered", NOT "shown", BECAUSE THAT IS WHAT IT IS.
  A card below the fold was sent and never looked at. Naming the column
  "shown" would make every tap rate quietly a claim the data cannot
  support. Tap rate stays meaningful as long as the denominator is
  defined consistently and honestly -- taps per card DELIVERED.

  True "seen" needs viewport detection on the device. That is a second,
  separate piece of work and should not be smuggled in by naming.

  TAPPED COMES FROM THE APP, because only the device knows. One small
  batched POST, rate limited.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1.4  THE BOUNDARY THAT NEEDS CARE
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  An impression names a SUPPLIER -- sponsored billing requires it. The
  demand engine is SUPPLIER-BLIND, enforced by
  tests/architecture/test_demand_independence.py.

  So the table and the writer live in network/community/ (a record of
  what the FEED did), and the demand engine reads a NARROW,
  SUPPLIER-BLIND view of it: "which categories did this shop look at and
  never buy". That is the same trick observed.py already uses with
  orders, and its docstring explains why it is not a loophole: the model
  describes the SHOP, and what it must never contain is who sold to
  them.

  Putting the table under demand/ would either break the architecture
  test or force the sponsored billing question somewhere worse.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1.6  DELIBERATELY NOT IN SCOPE
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  NO SUPPLIER-FACING "12 SHOPS VIEWED YOU". It is creepy, and it leaks
  competitive information about a shop's shopping to the people selling
  to them.

  NO VIEWPORT DETECTION YET. See 1.3 -- that is how "delivered" becomes
  "seen", and it is its own piece of work.

  A RETENTION POLICY IS REQUIRED BEFORE PRODUCTION and is not designed
  here. This table grows forever otherwise, and it is behavioural data
  about identifiable businesses.


===========================================================================
PART 2 — HARDENING THE COMMUNITY TAB
===========================================================================

Mandla asked for this against the OWASP API Top 10, which he shared as a
table. Below is that list mapped to WHAT IS ACTUALLY IN THIS CODEBASE,
with each item marked VERIFIED, MISSING or UNVERIFIED. The greps behind
each were run on 2026-09-16; re-run them rather than trusting this list
once the code has moved.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1  BROKEN OBJECT LEVEL AUTHORIZATION (IDOR)        VERIFIED CLEAN
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Every Community route takes its caller from the TOKEN and never from
  the request. The only request-supplied values across the whole surface
  are limit, offset and kind -- no object identifiers at all:

      feed_detail_routes.py:47  limit
      feed_detail_routes.py:51  offset
      explore_routes.py:32      kind

  So there is nothing here to enumerate. This is the strongest item on
  the list and it got that way by the standing rule, stated in
  business_profile_routes' own docstring: business_id comes from
  get_jwt_identity(), NEVER from a body or a URL.

  WHAT STILL NEEDS CHECKING: the same sweep across network/connections
  and network/orders, which DO take supplier and order ids in their
  paths. They have ownership checks
  (get_by_id_for_business_and_supplier and friends) but this audit has
  not been done route by route.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  2  BROKEN AUTHENTICATION                            VERIFIED
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  @fully_authenticated on every route: login + verified + active,
  re-queried against the database each request rather than trusted from
  JWT claims.

  ONE REAL BUG WAS FOUND AND FIXED IN THIS AREA on 2026-09-16 -- OTP
  verification compared the destination case-sensitively, so nobody
  whose email carried a capital letter could ever verify. See the commit
  "Nobody with a capital letter in their email could ever verify".

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  3  EXCESSIVE DATA EXPOSURE                          LOOKS RIGHT
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  A feed card carries: id, supplierId, supplierName, verification
  status, name, image, price, inStock, buyerCount, category, location,
  offersDelivery, minimumOrderCents, distanceKm, connectedBusinessCount.

  No email, no phone, no street address. location is suburb/city only,
  which the SupplierProfile model states as a rule rather than a habit.

  WORTH A SECOND LOOK ANYWAY, because the card shape has changed four
  times this week. The question to ask of each field is not "is it
  sensitive" but "does the reader need it to make the decision this card
  is for".

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  4  LACK OF RESOURCES AND RATE LIMITING              MISSING
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  THE REAL GAP, and Mandla suspected it before it was checked.

  There are NO rate limits on ANY Community route. Not the feed, not the
  five see-all screens, not explore, not browse. Only auth has any
  (policies.py: LOGIN, REGISTER, OTP_REQUEST and so on) and the supplier
  domain has four.

  The machinery already exists and is already used:

      @limiter.limit(rate_limit_policies.PRODUCT_CREATE,
                     key_func=rate_limit_policies.supplier_key_func)

  What is needed is a business_key_func alongside the supplier one, a
  policies module for the community domain, and the decorator on each
  route. The feed is the single most expensive endpoint in the app --
  nine rails, several of them doing trigram or distance work -- so it is
  also the cheapest thing in the system to abuse.

  THE NEW POST /community/impressions/tap NEEDS ONE FROM DAY ONE, which
  is why this is folded into Part 1 rather than left for later.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  5  BROKEN FUNCTION LEVEL AUTHORIZATION              VERIFIED
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Every Community route pins a role. Counted: community_routes 1,
  feed_detail_routes 7 (five routes, two with extra decorators),
  explore_routes 2.

  AND IT IS TESTED, not just present: test_feed_detail_routes.py asserts
  a supplier gets 403 on every one of the five endpoints, and an
  anonymous caller gets 401. That test exists because CLAUDE.md's rule
  is that "authenticated" stopped meaning "is a buyer" the moment ADMIN
  existed.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  6  MASS ASSIGNMENT                        ONE THING TO VERIFY
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  BusinessProfileService is SAFE. Every update loops over a HARDCODED
  list of (apiField, column) pairs and ignores anything else, so a
  request cannot reach a column the endpoint did not intend.

  SupplierProfileRepository.apply_updates IS THE ONE TO CHECK:

      for key, value in fields.items():
          if hasattr(profile, key):
              setattr(profile, key, value)

  It applies ANY attribute that exists on the model, and its own
  docstring says so plainly -- "the service layer is responsible for
  only ever passing real column names". That is a correct division of
  labour AND an unenforced one. If any caller ever passes a validated-
  but-unfiltered dict, verification_status and plan_tier are both
  reachable.

  ACTION: read every caller. If they all pass explicit kwargs, write the
  test that keeps it that way. If any passes a dict from a request, that
  is a real vulnerability.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  7  SECURITY MISCONFIGURATION                        UNVERIFIED
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Not audited. What to check: CORS origins in production config, DEBUG
  off, security headers, and the fact that .env currently holds real
  Mapbox and Gmail credentials (Mandla's standing rule is that every
  environment variable changes before production -- that rule needs to
  be a checklist somewhere, not only a memory).

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  8  INJECTION                                        VERIFIED CLEAN
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  No raw SQL anywhere in community/ or catalogue/discovery/ -- no text(),
  no execute(). Everything goes through SQLAlchemy with bound
  parameters.

  AND THE ONE REAL RISK WAS ALREADY FOUND AND FIXED: LIKE-pattern
  escaping in name search, which has its own test
  (test_like_escaping.py). A percent sign in a supplier's name would
  otherwise have been a wildcard.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  9  IMPROPER ASSETS MANAGEMENT                       UNVERIFIED
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Not audited. What to check: any route registered and no longer used,
  the api_keys surface, and whether the web portal exposes anything the
  mobile app does not.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  10 INSUFFICIENT LOGGING AND MONITORING              PARTIAL
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  There is a real audit domain (src/shared/audit + domains/security/
  audit) and it is used for auth, admin actions, verification decisions,
  plan changes and rate-limit trips.

  NOTHING IN THE COMMUNITY TAB IS AUDITED, and mostly that is right -- a
  shopkeeper looking at a feed is not a security event.

  THE QUESTION WORTH ASKING is whether the impressions table becomes the
  monitoring surface for the feed by accident. It should not: an
  impression is product analytics, an audit event is a security record,
  and mixing them means retention rules fight each other.
