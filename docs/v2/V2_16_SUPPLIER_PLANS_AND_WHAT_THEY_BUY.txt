===========================================================================
V2_16 — SUPPLIER PLANS: WHAT A TIER CAN BUY, AND WHAT IT MUST NEVER
===========================================================================

Written 2026-09-15. A PLAN, and an argument before it is a plan.

NOT TO BE CONFUSED WITH V2_13, despite both saying "tiers". That
document is about how a supplier CONNECTS — a phone, a spreadsheet, an
ERP. This one is about what a supplier PAYS FOR, and what that money is
and is not allowed to move. They share a word and nothing else.

WHY THIS DOCUMENT EXISTS AT ALL, stated plainly because it is a
correction: the feed was designed, built and shipped across four phases
without anybody asking what a supplier's plan buys them in it.
Sponsorship was repeatedly deferred with "there is nothing to sell
yet", which is true and is not the same as "there is nothing to
decide yet". Mandla asked the question; it should have been asked
during Book Two.


===========================================================================
PART 1 — THE INSIGHT THIS TURNS ON
===========================================================================

Mandla's, and it is the cleanest framing of TruConnect's economics
anybody has written down:

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  MY CONNECTIONS is the shop's OWN network. Suppliers they already
  know, already trade with, already chose. That is theirs. It is free
  and it must stay free.

  FOR YOU is REACH. It puts a supplier in front of a shop that does
  not know them yet. Reach is the thing we create. Reach is the thing
  that can be sold.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

That line is not a pricing decision. It is a PRODUCT decision that
happens to make pricing obvious, and it draws the boundary in exactly
the place where the value is created rather than where the money is
easiest to take.

It also explains, after the fact, why "Buy it again" always felt like
the highest-value rail in the app and the least appropriate one to
touch. It is not discovery. It is the shop's own memory, handed back.


===========================================================================
PART 2 — THE INVARIANT AT STAKE
===========================================================================

Everything under `demand/` is supplier-blind, and not by convention:

    tests/architecture/test_demand_independence.py
    FORBIDDEN_PREFIXES = supplier, network.connections, verification

The demand engine cannot see who sells anything. It answers one
question — WHAT DOES THIS SHOP NEED — and who can fulfil that is asked
afterwards by somebody else. The whole anti-domination property rests
on it: a wholesaler with four hundred products cannot fill a shop's
feed, because the feed returns N products for the reasons the SHOP has,
not the reasons a supplier has.

  IF TIER MONEY REACHES THAT RANKING, THE PROPERTY IS GONE. Not
  weakened — gone. A feed where spend moves organic results is an
  auction wearing a recommendation's clothes, and the shopkeeper finds
  out. They always find out: they notice the same wholesaler at the top
  of everything, they stop reading the rail, and the rail becomes
  worthless to the very suppliers who paid for it.

So this document's job is not to decide whether to monetise the feed.
It is to decide WHERE the money is allowed to touch it, precisely
enough that an architecture test can hold the line.


===========================================================================
PART 3 — THE RULE: TIER MAY BREAK A TIE, NEVER BEAT A FACT
===========================================================================

Every surface in the feed falls into one of three classes. The class
decides what money can do there, and nothing else does.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  EARNED — ranked by the shop's own behaviour. NOT FOR SALE, at any
           tier, for any amount, ever.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

    Buy it again          their own confirmed orders
    Stock for your shop   their declared + observed needs
    Moving fast           measured velocity, real units, real shops

  These are the rails that make the tab worth opening. Their entire
  value is that nobody paid for them. The moment one is sellable it
  stops being evidence and starts being an advert, and the platform
  has sold the only thing it had.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  PROMOTED — explicitly a promotion, labelled as one, sold by tier.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

    Sponsored slot        the one V2_15 Book Two Part 8 already reserves
    Explore wholesalers   a browsing surface that is honest about being
                          browsing rather than recommendation

  A promoted slot is not dishonest. A magazine has advertising pages
  and nobody is deceived, because they look like advertising pages.
  What is dishonest is an advert dressed as a recommendation.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  TIE-BROKEN — an organic FACT ranks first. Tier orders the equals.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

    Collect near you           distance is the fact
    Suppliers you haven't      relevance and reach are the facts
      ordered from

  THIS IS WHERE MOST OF THE REVENUE ACTUALLY LIVES, and it is the class
  that needs the most discipline. The rule is one sentence:

      A MAX SUPPLIER 20KM AWAY NEVER OUTRANKS A FREE SUPPLIER 1KM AWAY
      IN A RAIL WHOSE TITLE PROMISES PROXIMITY.

  But two suppliers both about 2km away, both stocking what this shop
  buys? Nothing in the shop's own data separates them. Ordering them by
  tier costs the reader nothing, because the fact the rail promised has
  already been honoured. That is sellable and it is honest.

  The implementation follows from the sentence: tier enters the sort
  key AFTER the organic one, never inside it, and only within a
  tolerance band the rail declares (e.g. distances within 2km of each
  other are "equal" for ordering purposes).


===========================================================================
PART 4 — THE LADDER
===========================================================================

Four levels. The names are Mandla's; what each buys is a proposal.

  FREE          Verified, findable, orderable. Present in every EARNED
                rail whenever the shop's own data says they belong
                there. No promoted placement, no tie-break priority.

  PRO           Tie-break priority in the TIE-BROKEN rails. Limited
                sponsored eligibility. A higher ceiling on how many of
                their products may surface in one shop's feed.

  MAX           Stronger tie-break. Full sponsored eligibility.
                Highest product ceiling. Featured placement in
                Explore wholesalers.

  PARTNER       Sales-negotiated, and the answer to Mandla's pilot
                question. Time-boxed and labelled like any other
                promotion — a pilot partner gets reach, not a secret.
                Written as a contract with an END DATE, because a
                permanent unlabelled advantage is how a marketplace
                quietly becomes a storefront for one supplier.

FREE TRIALS AND PROMOTIONAL WINDOWS belong here and are not a
discount — they are how a new supplier finds out whether the platform
works before paying for it. A supplier who joins, sells nothing for
sixty days and never had reach has learned nothing about us except
that we charge.


===========================================================================
PART 5 — WHAT A FREE SUPPLIER MUST ALWAYS KEEP
===========================================================================

This part is self-interest, not charity.

  A free verified supplier is FINDABLE, ORDERABLE, and appears in the
  EARNED rails whenever a shop's own needs match what they stock.

If free suppliers get no traction at all they leave. When they leave
the catalogue thins. When the catalogue thins the feed has less to
recommend, matching has less to resolve, "also from 3 suppliers"
disappears, and the rails that PAYING suppliers bought placement in
stop being worth opening.

  THE PAYING SUPPLIERS ARE BUYING ATTENTION THAT A HEALTHY CATALOGUE
  CREATES. Starving the free tier destroys the inventory they are
  paying to stand in front of.

Tiers buy REACH BEYOND WHAT YOU HAVE EARNED. They do not buy the right
to exist, and they cannot buy somebody else's absence.


===========================================================================
PART 6 — LABELLING, WHICH IS NOT NEGOTIABLE
===========================================================================

V2_15 Part 12 already bans dark patterns: no fake scarcity, no invented
"3 people are viewing this", everything the feed says must be true. A
promoted card that does not say it is promoted breaks that rule as
surely as an invented number would.

  SAY IT PLAINLY. Not six-point grey text in a corner. The card says
  what it is, in the same size as everything else on it.

The cost of clear labelling is smaller than it looks and the benefit is
structural: once promotion is visibly separate, the EARNED rails become
MORE credible, not less. The reader can see which is which, so they can
trust the part that was never for sale.


===========================================================================
PART 7 — WHAT MUST NEVER HAPPEN
===========================================================================

The list an architecture test should eventually hold:

  1  A shop's own purchase history reordered by supplier spend.
  2  The needs model influenced by tier. `demand/` stays supplier-blind
     and test_demand_independence.py keeps passing, unchanged.
  3  Unlabelled promotion anywhere.
  4  A paid supplier surfacing for a category the shop does not buy.
     Money buys priority AMONG relevant results; it does not buy
     relevance itself.
  5  A free supplier hidden, delisted, or made unorderable.
  6  Tier visible to another supplier. One supplier's plan is not
     another supplier's business, the same rule V2_04 2.4 applies to
     volume.
  7  Tier reaching `offer_selection`. Which supplier's offer a shop
     sees for a product they already want is a RELATIONSHIP and PRICE
     decision. If that becomes purchasable, the price comparison is a
     lie, and the catalogue -- the most expensive thing we own -- stops
     meaning anything.

Point 7 is the one most likely to be argued for later and it is the
most important on the list.


===========================================================================
PART 8 — WHAT THIS CHANGES IN THE CODE
===========================================================================

Smaller than it sounds, because the boundary was already drawn:

  NEW     supplier_profiles.plan_tier          enum, default FREE
          supplier_profiles.plan_expires_at    for trials and PARTNER
  NEW     a placement module that owns the tie-break, OUTSIDE demand/.
          The feed composer may read it; the demand engine may not,
          and the existing architecture test already enforces that.
  NEW     an architecture test for Part 7 items 1, 2 and 7 -- the same
          AST discipline as the curtain and the composer tests.
  EDIT    the TIE-BROKEN rails, to sort by (organic fact, tier) with a
          declared tolerance band.
  EDIT    the card shape, to carry a promoted flag the app renders.

NOT TOUCHED: demand/, offer_selection.py, buy_it_again, the matching
ladder. That is the point of the boundary.


===========================================================================
PART 9 — WHAT ONLY MANDLA CAN ANSWER
===========================================================================

  1  PRICE. What do Pro and Max cost, per month, in rands? Everything
     above is structure; none of it says what to charge.

  2  THE TOLERANCE BAND. How close is "equally near"? 2km? 5km? It
     decides how much tie-break priority is actually worth, and it is
     the number a supplier is really buying.

  3  THE PRODUCT CEILING. How many of one supplier's products may
     appear in one shop's feed at each tier? Today it is
     MAX_CARDS_PER_SUPPLIER = 3 for everybody. Does Max get 5? And
     does that ceiling apply to EARNED rails too, or only to the
     ones where money is allowed?  (My reading: it must apply
     everywhere, because it is an anti-domination rule before it is a
     pricing lever -- otherwise tier has silently entered an EARNED
     rail through the back door.)

  4  EXPLORE WHOLESALERS. A new surface, named but not designed. Is it
     a tab, a rail, or a screen behind a chip?

  5  WHETHER "COLLECT NEAR YOU" IS SELLABLE AT ALL. It is the rail most
     obviously about a fact (distance) and therefore the one where a
     tie-break is least visible and most defensible -- but also the one
     a shopkeeper would feel most betrayed by if it were wrong.
