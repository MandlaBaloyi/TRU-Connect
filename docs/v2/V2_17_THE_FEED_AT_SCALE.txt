===========================================================================
V2_17 — THE FEED AT SCALE: CAPS, SCREENS, AND WHAT IS ACTUALLY SOLD
===========================================================================

Written 2026-09-15. A PLAN, and the answers to V2_16's open questions.

WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS. V2_16 drew the boundary between what a supplier's
plan may buy and what it may never touch, and ended with five questions
only Mandla could answer. He answered one, delegated four, and added a
catch nobody had noticed. This document does that research, answers
them, and turns the whole thing into a build order.

It also absorbs the feed-at-scale work that has been accumulating in
conversation and was never written down: rail caps, "see all" screens,
grid layouts, and the anti-domination arithmetic that has to survive a
wholesaler arriving with four hundred products.


===========================================================================
PART 1 — THE FIVE QUESTIONS, ANSWERED
===========================================================================

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Q1  PRICE
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  PLACEHOLDERS FOR MODELLING, NOT DECISIONS, and they should be argued
  with rather than adopted:

      FREE      R0
      PRO       ~R299 / month
      MAX       ~R899 / month
      PARTNER   negotiated, per agreement

  THE REASONING, such as it is. A small cash-and-carry must be able to
  say yes to PRO without a meeting — that is the entire point of a
  self-serve tier, and anything that needs a meeting is PARTNER by
  definition. R299 sits inside the band South African SMEs already pay
  for accounting and point-of-sale software, so it is a familiar number
  rather than a new category of expense.

  MAX is priced for a supplier who has already proven the platform
  works for them — it is not a first purchase, and pricing it as though
  it were would just make PRO look expensive.

  WHAT WOULD FALSIFY THESE: the first ten real conversations. Nothing
  here is evidence; it is a starting position so the rest of the
  document can be concrete about what is being bought.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Q2  THE TOLERANCE BAND — 2.0 KM, and it is narrower than it looks
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  The band decides when two suppliers count as "equally near", which is
  when tier is allowed to order them. Get it wrong in either direction
  and the feature is worthless:

      TOO WIDE (say 10km)   a supplier 1km away and one 11km away are
                            "tied", so tier has beaten a fact. The rule
                            V2_16 exists to hold is broken on day one.
      TOO NARROW (0.5km)    the tie-break almost never fires, and PRO
                            and MAX buy nothing they can feel.

  2km is about the same trip in township and peri-urban density — the
  same taxi, the same bakkie run, the same decision. It is also small
  enough, against MAX_COLLECTION_KM = 25, that the rail's proximity
  promise visibly holds: a band is 8% of the radius.

  ABSOLUTE, NOT PROPORTIONAL. "Within 25% of each other" was the
  tempting alternative and it fails at the top of the range: 20km and
  25km are within 25% and are not the same trip by any reading.

  FALSIFIED BY: shopkeepers treating 2km as a meaningful difference —
  visible the day impressions exist, as a tap-rate gap between the
  first and second card of a banded pair.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Q3  THE PRODUCT CEILING — two ceilings, and tier can only move one
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Today MAX_CARDS_PER_SUPPLIER = 3, for everybody, on the rails that
  recommend. The proposal:

      ABSOLUTE CEILING, per supplier, per rail — tier MAY raise it:
          FREE 3      PRO 4      MAX 5      PARTNER 5

      PROPORTIONAL CEILING — NO TIER MAY PASS IT:
          no supplier may hold more than 40% of any rail

  THE SECOND CEILING IS THE REAL PROTECTION and the first is the
  pricing lever. On a 12-card rail, 40% is 4 cards — so a MAX
  supplier's absolute 5 is bound down to 4, and a rail can never be
  fewer than three distinct suppliers. On a short rail the
  proportional ceiling bites harder, which is correct: a 5-card rail
  with 2 cards from one supplier is already lopsided.

  AND IT APPLIES TO EARNED RAILS TOO — Mandla's reading in V2_16, and
  it is right. If the ceiling only bound where money is allowed, tier
  would have entered an EARNED rail through the back door: raising a
  MAX supplier's ceiling on "stock for your shop" is buying organic
  placement, whatever we call it.

      WITH ONE EXCEPTION, AND IT IS NOT A LOOPHOLE: "Buy it again" has
      NO per-supplier cap at any tier. It is the shop's own confirmed
      purchase history. If they buy fifteen things from MacMan, that is
      a fact about their trading, not a wholesaler crowding a feed —
      and capping it would hide a shop's own stock from itself. The
      domination risk is a risk about RECOMMENDATION. This rail only
      remembers.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Q4  EXPLORE WHOLESALERS — and the catch, which is real
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Mandla asked whether the backend can even tell what a wholesaler is,
  and warned that a supplier naming themselves "Wholesalers" must not
  break it. The research says he was right to ask. See Part 2 — it is
  the largest single finding in this document.

  THE SURFACE ITSELF: a SCREEN behind a chip, not a tab and not a rail.

      NOT A TAB       a fifth tab is the most expensive real estate in
                      the app and this is not a daily destination.
      NOT A RAIL      a rail inside "For You" competes with the EARNED
                      rails for the same attention, which is exactly
                      the blurring V2_16 forbids.
      A SCREEN        reached deliberately. Somebody who opens "Explore
                      wholesalers" has ASKED to browse suppliers, which
                      makes promoted placement there honest: they came
                      to look at suppliers, and we are showing them
                      suppliers.

  That is also why it is the best home for paid placement in the whole
  app — the reader's intent and the advertiser's intent point the same
  way.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Q5  IS "COLLECT NEAR YOU" SELLABLE? — NO, and the code decides it
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Not a judgement call in the end. One line of evidence settles it:

      sections.py:657 — collect_near_you is the ONLY rail that passes
      `distances` to its cards. Every card shows a number.

  A TIE-BREAK IS ONLY DEFENSIBLE WHEN IT IS NOT FALSIFIABLE BY THE
  READER. Here the reader is holding the contradiction: they would see
  2.0 km sitting above 1.2 km and conclude either that the app is
  broken or that the order was bought. The second conclusion is the
  correct one, which is what makes it unrecoverable.

  SO: the organic cards in "Collect near you" stay in TRUE DISTANCE
  ORDER, at every tier, forever. If promotion belongs there at all it
  is a LABELLED INSERT — a card that says what it is and does not
  pretend to be part of the distance ordering.

  WHICH RAIL IS TIE-BREAKABLE, THEN: "Suppliers you haven't ordered
  from". It passes no distances and shows no ranking number, so its
  order is not a claim the reader can check — which is the same test,
  applied honestly, giving the opposite answer.

  THE GENERAL RULE THIS PRODUCES, worth more than either answer:

      A RAIL MAY BE TIE-BROKEN ONLY IF IT DOES NOT DISPLAY THE FACT IT
      IS ORDERED BY.


===========================================================================
PART 2 — WHAT IS A WHOLESALER? (THE CATCH)
===========================================================================

THE FINDING:

    supplier_profiles.category   String(120), nullable, UNVALIDATED
    every supplier in dev        category = None
    no supplier-kind enum        anywhere in the codebase

And the names in the database make the trap concrete:

    "Qwen Wholesale"          would match a name filter
    "Lepelle Wholesalers"     would match
    "Seshego Cash & Carry"    obviously a wholesaler, would NOT match
    "MacMan Businesses"       would not match

A NAME FILTER IS WRONG IN BOTH DIRECTIONS AT ONCE. It misses real
wholesalers and it admits anybody who types the word — a retailer
called "Wholesale Beauty Supplies" would be listed, and there is
nothing they did wrong.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  THE FIX: supplier_kind, an explicit enum, never inferred from text.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

      WHOLESALER      sells to businesses for resale
      DISTRIBUTOR     represents brands in a territory
      MANUFACTURER    makes what they sell
      RETAILER        sells to the public, and also to shops
      UNSPECIFIED     the default, and the honest state for everybody
                      already in the database

  SET, NOT GUESSED. The supplier chooses at onboarding; staff can
  correct it; sales-onboarded PARTNERs have it set as part of the
  agreement. UNSPECIFIED is a real answer and must never be quietly
  rewritten by a heuristic.

  WHY NOT INFER IT FROM BEHAVIOUR. There are real signals — selling by
  CASE or BOX, a minimum order, a wide catalogue — and they are
  tempting because they need no migration. They are also wrong often
  enough to matter: a spaza selling single units through TruConnect is
  not a retailer for that reason, and a new wholesaler with three
  products is still a wholesaler. Behaviour can CORROBORATE a
  declaration; it cannot replace one.

  EXPLORE WHOLESALERS THEN FILTERS ON supplier_kind = WHOLESALER, which
  is a fact somebody stated rather than a string somebody typed, and
  the screen cannot be gamed by renaming a business.

  BEFORE ANY SEEDING FOR THE APP TESTS, which is what Mandla asked:
  the column has to exist first, or the seed teaches us nothing about
  a screen that cannot work yet.

PART 3 — THE FEED AT SCALE
===========================================================================

Everything below exists because the feed currently has no ceiling and
no exit. It scrolls right forever, it fetches whatever it fetches, and
on a platform with four suppliers that looks fine. It will not.

  3.1  RAIL CAPS AND THE WAY OUT

  Every rail shows a bounded number of cards, and says so when there
  is more behind it:

      RAIL                    ON THE RAIL     ARROW LEADS TO
      Buy it again            12              new screen, up to 50
      Moving fast right now   10              new screen
      Stock for your shop     12              new screen
      Your suppliers          12 (2 rows)     My Connections (exists)
      Collect near you         5              new screen, list layout
      Suppliers not ordered   10              new screen

  THE ARROW IS NOT DECORATION. Without it the cap is just truncation,
  and a shop with sixty previously-ordered products would silently lose
  forty-eight of them. The rail becomes a preview, which is what a rail
  should always have been.

  3.2  THE SEE-ALL SCREENS

  PRODUCT SCREENS ARE A 3-WIDE GRID, scrolling down, in the shape of
  every stock-browsing app a shopkeeper already uses. Up to 50 items,
  paginated.

      Buy it again        everything they have ever ordered, newest
                          first. NO per-supplier cap (Q3).
      Moving fast         the full rising list, per-supplier capped,
                          and RECOMPUTED as the algorithm moves — the
                          screen is a view of today's velocity, not a
                          saved list.
      Stock for your shop the demand engine's full answer, capped.

  THE SUPPLIER SCREEN IS A VERTICAL LIST, not a grid: avatar, name,
  tick, distance, one action. A supplier is an entity you are choosing
  between, and a list is how you compare entities; a grid is how you
  compare goods.

  3.3  SUPPLIERS ARE MIXED, DELIBERATELY

  Within any product rail or grid, cards from different suppliers are
  INTERLEAVED rather than grouped. Grouping by supplier turns a market
  into a series of shopfronts, and the per-supplier ceiling (Q3) is
  what stops any one of them owning the top of the screen.

  Interleaving is also what makes "also from 3 suppliers" legible once
  the catalogue resolves: the reader is already used to seeing several
  suppliers in one rail.

  3.4  DIVIDERS

  A hairline between rails. Sounds cosmetic; it is not. Six rails of
  horizontally-scrolling cards with nothing between them read as one
  undifferentiated wall, and the titles stop doing their job — which
  matters most for the rails whose titles carry the REASON ("Based on
  what you buy", "Shops are buying these faster than last month").

  3.5  "YOUR SUPPLIERS" IN TWO ROWS

  Mandla's request and it is the right shape: a 2 x N grid scrolling
  horizontally fits twelve suppliers in the width that currently holds
  six, and supplier avatars are small enough that two rows still read
  cleanly. Past twelve, the arrow goes to My Connections, which already
  exists and is already the right screen for the full list.

===========================================================================
PART 5 — BUILD ORDER
===========================================================================

  1  SUPPLIER KIND AND PLAN COLUMNS + migration. Nothing else can be
     tested until a supplier can BE a wholesaler or a PRO. Seed data
     for the app tests comes after this, not before — which is
     precisely what Mandla flagged.

  2  RAIL CAPS, hasMore AND seeAllHref, with the proportional ceiling
     and interleaving. Backend only; the app keeps working unchanged
     because it already ignores fields it does not know.

  3  THE SEE-ALL ENDPOINTS, paginated.

  4  THE FRONTEND: dividers, arrows, two-row supplier rail, the
     3-wide product grid, the four screens.

  5  PLACEMENT AND THE TIE-BREAK, with its architecture test. Last,
     deliberately: it is the only step that lets money touch ranking,
     and it should land on a feed that is already bounded, already
     interleaved and already honest about what it shows.

  6  EXPLORE WHOLESALERS.

STEP 5 COMES AFTER STEP 2 FOR A REASON. The ceilings and interleaving
are what make tier-ordering safe; shipping the tie-break first would
put money into a feed with no anti-domination floor underneath it.

PART 6 — WHAT IS STILL OPEN
===========================================================================

  1  PHASE 4, IMPRESSIONS. Unchanged from V2_15 and still Mandla's
     call. It is also what would settle Q2 and Q3 with evidence rather
     than argument — the tolerance band and the ceilings are both
     guesses that impressions would falsify or confirm within weeks.

  2  THE CANONICAL CATALOGUE. Seeded from real offers, per the
     conversation of 2026-09-15. Independent of everything here, and
     the thing that makes offer_selection mean something.

  3  V2_14's FIVE FULFILMENT QUESTIONS. Nothing moves an order out of
     CONFIRMED. The feed now produces demand that the order lifecycle
     cannot finish.

  4  WHETHER PROMOTED CARDS APPEAR IN "FOR YOU" AT ALL, or only in
     Explore. V2_16 reserves the sponsored slot; this document argues
     Explore is the honest home. Shipping ONLY Explore first would
     test the revenue model without touching the feed's credibility,
     and that may be the right order.
