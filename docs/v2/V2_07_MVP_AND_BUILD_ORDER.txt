V2_07 — THE MVP, AND THE ORDER TO BUILD IT IN
===========================================================================

Depends on V2_03 through V2_06, plus V2_08 for the third-party layer.
REVISED 2026-09-09 after review: item F was corrected (it described a
cross-write between ledgers that should never happen), and item K — the

This is the one to argue with, because it is where the ideas become a
schedule and the cuts get made.


===========================================================================
PART 1 — WHAT "MVP" MEANS HERE
===========================================================================

Not "the smallest thing we can ship". This app is already large — around
170 route files and forty-plus feature folders. The question is not what
to add but WHAT MUST BE TRUE for a real business and a real supplier in a
real township to use this for a month without us standing next to them.

The test:

  A shop owner can find a supplier, place an order, receive it, record
  what they owe, and come back next week to reorder without being
  reminded by a human.

  A supplier can list what they sell, be found, receive an order, fulfil
  it, and see who has gone quiet.

Everything in the MVP column below serves that sentence. Everything else
waits, however good it is.


===========================================================================
PART 2 — THE CUT
===========================================================================

IN — required for the sentence above to be true

  A  Community feed endpoint, business side, real data
     Buy-it-again · your suppliers · category chips · new near you.
     Replaces mockCommunityFeed. The contract already exists.

  B  Category imagery + the fallback chain
     16 categories, one helper, the trust line from V2_04 Part 4.
     Unblocks every visual surface at once.

  C  Community feed endpoint, supplier side
     Reorder-due · wants-to-connect · businesses near you · shopfront
     health. The demand-gap rail can follow — it is the best idea in
     these documents and it needs data volume to be non-embarrassing.

  D  My Connections as a relationship ledger, both roles
     Real photos, last order, reorder gap, open order. Ordering by
     trading reality.

  E  Home, both roles
     The four cards. Nothing new computed except the reorder gap.

  F  Stock Orders into the Account tab (V2_06 3.1)
     A third tile beside Loan Book and Order Book. NO cross-writing
     between the three ledgers — see the non-goal in V2_06 3.1.
     Cheapest high-value work in the plan.

  G  Cash Up (V2_06 3.2)
     Three numbers, once a day.

  H  Supplier API keys screen + fulfilment mode
     The backend shipped in step 7 and has no UI. Without this the
     wholesaler segment cannot onboard at all.

  I  Discovery: announcements and opportunities only
     No YouTube yet. TruConnect announcements plus a curated,
     hand-entered opportunities list — which, given registration
     deadlines and the Spaza Shop Fund, is the most useful content we
     could ship and needs no ingestion engine.

  J  The More audit (V2_06 Part 5.1)
     Find every setting that controls nothing.

  K  THE SUPPLIER CURTAIN — TWO GATES (V2_03 Part 4)  [REVISED 09-11]
     A supplier signs up, builds their whole catalogue, and their
     offers stay invisible to businesses until BOTH gates pass:

       IDENTITY    documents, registered name, ID. The PENDING state
                   and the reminder ladder.
       CATALOGUE   the offers themselves are reviewed. New in this
                   revision, and the first draft was wrong to omit it.

     WHY BOTH. A supplier can be entirely genuine and still list
     something that must not be sold here. The 2024 poisonings that
     made registration compulsory killed at least 23 children, traced
     to terbufos — an agricultural pesticide — found in a snack, with
     inspectors finding illegal pesticides sold from spaza shops. No
     document check catches that.

     HOW IT SCALES WITHOUT A REVIEW DEPARTMENT. The matching ladder is
     already the risk classifier: AUTO_LINK means the catalogue already
     knows this product, so it auto-approves; CREATE_NEW means nobody
     has ever vetted it, and that is the review queue — which already
     exists as MatchReview rows. Measured free-tier resolution was 80%,
     so roughly one line in five needs eyes, falling as the catalogue
     grows. Plus a hard prohibited/restricted list that skips the
     ladder entirely (pesticides, medicines, alcohol, tobacco, infant
     formula, raw dairy).

     THE VISIBILITY RULE GOES IN THE REPOSITORY LAYER, not in each
     route. A rule applied per-endpoint leaks the first time somebody
     adds an endpoint, and this is the rule that must not leak.

     THE HONEST COST: this is the only item in the MVP that needs a
     PERSON rather than code. Budget for it.

OUT — good, and not now

  Price-movement rail          needs order volume to be meaningful
  Demand-gap rail              needs the 5-business threshold to clear
  YouTube ingestion            build after Discovery has readers
  Shelf list                   after reorder proves itself
  News                         probably never

  THE THIRD-PARTY LAYER — all of V2_08, none of it MVP, and none of it
  dismissed. Ranked there by value ÷ risk:

  WhatsApp notifications       first out of the gate after MVP. Cheapest
                               and highest-reach thing in any of these
                               documents (~R0.14 a message).
  VAS float                    gated on a partner conversation, not on
                               engineering — a VAS distributor is a
                               supplier whose products are digital.
  Delivery brokerage           gated on answering "who pays for a
                               failed delivery".
  Group buy / stokvel          biggest prize, needs its own document
                               and a conversation with three real
                               stokvels.
  Lending on our own book      never. No capital, no licence, and a
                               conflict of interest with our own
                               marketplace.

PART 3 — THE ORDER, AND WHY THIS ORDER
===========================================================================

  PHASE 1 — MAKE IT LOOK REAL          (B, D)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Category imagery and the fallback chain first, because every screen
  after this benefits and because it is the one thing that makes the
  existing feed stop looking like a prototype. Then My Connections with
  real photos and trading data on both sides.

  Why first: it is the smallest change with the largest visible effect,
  it has no backend dependencies beyond what exists, and it settles the
  question that has been circling for two weeks.

  PHASE 2 — MAKE IT WORK               (A, C, E)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
  The two feed endpoints and Home. This is the bulk of the backend work:
  one new domain (network/community), the composition service, the
  reorder-gap computation, and the boundary test that keeps it a leaf.

  Why second: it depends on Phase 1 for how it looks, and everything in
  Phase 3 depends on it for where things link to.

  PHASE 3 — MAKE THEM STAY             (F, G)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Connect the books, then Cash Up. The retention layer.

  Why third and not first: the moat only matters if people arrive, and
  Phases 1–2 are what makes them arrive. But it must not slip past
  here, because it is the thing that decides month two.

  PHASE 4 — OPEN THE WHOLESALE DOOR    (H, K)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
  API keys screen, fulfilment mode, and the verification curtain.
  Grouped because they are the same conversation from the supplier's
  side: what it takes to be a real, trusted, integrated supplier here.

  ARGUABLY K BELONGS IN PHASE 1. The counter-argument is that until
  there are businesses looking at shopfronts, an invisible shopfront
  and a visible one are the same thing. But the moment the marketplace
  is live to anyone outside the team, K must already be in place.

  PHASE 5 — GIVE THEM SOMETHING TO READ (I, J)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Announcements, opportunities, and the settings audit.

A NOTE ON PHASE 1 GOING FIRST. There is a real argument for Phase 3
first — the moat before the shopfront. It is rejected because a
bookkeeping tool nobody opened the app to find is a bookkeeping tool
nobody uses, and because the visual problem is the one that has been
blocking confidence in the product for two weeks. Fix the thing that is
in the way.

PART 5 — THE UNCOMFORTABLE QUESTION, ASKED PROPERLY
===========================================================================

The research contains one finding that argues against this entire plan,
and it would be dishonest to bury it in a footnote.

  "WhatsApp reaches 95 to 97% of informal retail trade."

  "The brands winning digital ordering in Africa did not build a
   marketplace, they put a catalog where the conversation was already
   happening."
                       — African Marketing Confederation; AVODA Group

Every shop owner already has WhatsApp, already knows how to use it,
already orders stock on it, and pays nothing extra in data to open it. We
are asking them to install a second thing.

THREE HONEST RESPONSES.

  1  THE APP IS NOT THE ONLY POSSIBLE FRONT DOOR, AND THE ARCHITECTURE
     ALREADY KNOWS THIS. The backend serves three clients through one
     auth system as of step 7 — bearer, cookie, API key. A WhatsApp
     Business API channel would be a fourth client, not a rewrite. The
     catalogue, the offers, the orders and the matching are all
     transport-agnostic. This is the single strongest argument for the
     domain structure the last two weeks produced.

  2  WHATSAPP CANNOT DO THE PART THAT RETAINS. A chat thread cannot be
     a loan book, a cash-up history, or a price history across three
     months. The Account tab — the moat in V2_03 Part 3 — is precisely
     the thing that needs an app. So the likely long-run shape is
     ORDER IN WHATSAPP, KEEP RECORDS IN TRUCONNECT, which is a
     defensible split rather than a defeat.

  3  IT IS NOT AN MVP DECISION. Building a WhatsApp channel now would
     mean building it against a catalogue whose feed rules are not yet
     settled. Build the rules once, then expose them through a second
     door.

  WHAT SHOULD HAPPEN: a small, honest test after MVP. Ten real shops.
  Half ordering in the app, half through a WhatsApp number a human
  answers by hand. Measure which ones are still ordering in week six.
  That is a cheaper answer than any argument, including this one.

  AND ONE THING TO DO SOONER, from the review. WhatsApp does not have
  to be an ordering channel to be worth using. As a NOTIFICATION
  channel — order confirmation, reference, supplier, expected delivery
  date — it reaches the 95–97% without asking anyone to change how they
  work, and a utility template costs roughly R0.14. That is the
  cheapest, highest-reach thing in any of these documents. It is the
  first item in V2_08's ranking and it plausibly follows immediately
  after MVP rather than waiting for the ordering question to be
  settled.

  NOTE THE DATE: utility messages inside the 24-hour service window are
  free today and become chargeable on 1 October 2026. Any cost model
  built before then is wrong after it.

PART 6 — DECISIONS: WHAT ACTUALLY BLOCKS, AND WHAT DOES NOT
===========================================================================

REVISED 2026-09-11. The first version listed seven open questions as
though all seven needed answering before anything could start. On review
that was unhelpful — most of them are reversible, and a reversible
decision does not need certainty, it needs a default and a note saying
what would change it.

So: each one now has a DEFAULT that holds unless overruled, and an
honest statement of what being wrong would cost.

6.1 TWO THAT GENUINELY BLOCK PHASE 1

  CATEGORY ART STYLE
    DEFAULT: illustrated or flat-with-icon, not photographic.
    WHY: a photograph of maize meal in a category slot edges toward
    the one thing V2_04 Part 4 forbids — art that could be mistaken
    for a claim about a specific product. Illustration cannot be
    mistaken that way, at any size, in any slot.
    COST IF WRONG: low. Sixteen images, swapped in an afternoon,
    because the fallback chain resolves them by category value.
    WHAT WOULD CHANGE IT: a supplier telling us the app looks cheap.

  WHO SOURCES THE SIXTEEN IMAGES
    DEFAULT: one coherent licensed set from a single source, so the
    sixteen look like a family rather than sixteen decisions. Source
    URL, licence name, licence URL and acquisition date recorded WITH
    each asset, not in someone's memory.
    COST IF WRONG: low for style, HIGH for licensing. A mismatched set
    is a re-buy; an unlicensed set is a letter from a rights-holder
    eighteen months from now with no record of where the file came
    from. The recording discipline is the part that is not optional.

6.2 THREE THAT SHOULD BE DECIDED BY DATA, NOT BY US, NOW

  These are tuning parameters. Picking them from an armchair is
  guessing; picking them from ten real suppliers is measurement. Ship
  the conservative default, watch, adjust.

  DEMAND-GAP THRESHOLD
    DEFAULT: 5 distinct businesses in 30 days.
    DIRECTION OF ERROR MATTERS: too LOW identifies an individual shop's
    buying, which is a privacy breach. Too HIGH just means the rail
    stays empty. So when uncertain, go higher. Start at 5, raise it
    without hesitation if an area is thin, and never lower it below 5
    without a real argument.

  PRICE MOVEMENT SCOPE
    DEFAULT: connected suppliers only for MVP.
    WHY: showing a business a cheaper price at a supplier they are NOT
    connected to is the most valuable version for the buyer and the
    most dangerous for supplier relations (V2_04 2.4). Widening later
    is a decision we can make deliberately, having told suppliers
    first. Narrowing after launch is a feature removal, which is worse.

  REORDER-DUE TIMING (does the supplier see it before the business)
    DEFAULT: the business sees their own prompt first; the supplier's
    row appears only after the window has clearly passed.
    WHY: if both fire the same morning, one of them feels watched.

6.3 TWO THAT ARE ALREADY DECIDED, AND SHOULD STOP BEING LISTED

  PHASE ORDER — Phase 1 (looks) before Phase 3 (moat). Argued in Part
  3 and settled: a bookkeeping tool nobody opened the app to find is a
  bookkeeping tool nobody uses.

  NEWS IN DISCOVERY — no. Four curated videos and two real
  opportunities beat a news river nobody maintains.

6.4 TWO THAT DEPEND ON A FACT WE DO NOT HAVE YET

  CASH UP IN MVP?
    It is the one IN item not required by the test sentence in Part 1.
    DEFAULT: keep it, cut it first if Phase 3 runs long. It is small,
    and it serves the tools-stand-alone principle — a kota seller who
    never buys stock from us still has a reason to open the app.

  WHERE THE SUPPLIER CURTAIN (K) GOES
    DEFAULT: Phase 4 as scheduled.
    THE FACT THAT DECIDES IT: the date the marketplace is first visible
    to anyone outside the team. K must be in place BEFORE that date,
    whenever it falls. If that date lands before Phase 4, K moves. This
    is a launch-timing question, not an engineering one, and it is the
    one item here where being wrong is not recoverable — an unvetted
    product reaching a real shop is not something a later commit fixes.

6.5 THE RULE BEHIND ALL OF THIS

  A decision that is cheap to reverse should be made quickly and
  revisited with evidence. A decision that is expensive to reverse —
  licensing, privacy thresholds, anything a person could be harmed by
  — gets the conservative default and waits for a real reason to move.

  Only two questions in this list are in the second category: image
  licensing, and when the curtain lands.
