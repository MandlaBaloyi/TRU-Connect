HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT

Written to be drawn on in investor conversations, but written as an
internal assessment rather than a pitch. Part 5 lists what is NOT true
yet and what should not be claimed. That section exists because being
caught overstating in front of someone who asks one hard follow-up is
worse than understating from the start -- and an investor who finds the
limits stated plainly in your own document tends to trust the rest of it.

Every number here is either measured or labelled as an estimate.


===========================================================================
PART 1 — THE INVERSION
===========================================================================

The obvious benefit of a central catalogue is price comparison: a
business sees that the same case of Coke is R89 from one supplier and
R94 from another.

That is the LEAST valuable thing it does.

It is the visible feature, the one that belongs on a landing page, and
if it were the only benefit the complexity would not be worth carrying.
The three things underneath it are where the value actually is, and none
of them are visible in the product.

Ranked by what they are worth to the business, most first:

  1. Supplier onboarding cost      -- existential, today
  2. Making a basket legible       -- unlocks lending
  3. Price data nobody else holds  -- a by-product of the first two
  4. A compounding moat            -- gets harder to copy over time
  5. Price comparison for buyers   -- the visible one


===========================================================================
PART 2 — THE FOUR THAT MATTER
===========================================================================


2.1 SUPPLIER ONBOARDING IS THE GROWTH CONSTRAINT

The binding constraint on TRU Connect is not demand from businesses. It
is how long it takes to get a supplier listed with a real catalogue.

Without a central catalogue, a supplier arriving with 3,000 products is
a data-entry project -- either theirs or ours, and neither party will do
it. That is not friction, it is a refusal, and it caps the platform at
whatever size the smallest suppliers can be typed in by hand.

With it, a supplier uploads the spreadsheet they already have and is
trading. Everything else in this document is downstream of that: none of
it happens if suppliers cannot join.

THIS IS THE ONE TO LEAD WITH. It is concrete, it is measurable, and it
is the thing an operator will recognise immediately.


2.2 A LEGIBLE BASKET IS WHAT MAKES LENDING POSSIBLE

Lending to informal businesses is already on the roadmap, and the
catalogue is the piece that makes it underwritable.

The problem: you cannot assess a spaza shop from a bank statement,
because most do not have a meaningful one. Cash trade leaves no record a
lender can read.

What TRU Connect has instead: "orders R14,000 a month, consistently,
from three suppliers, pays on time." That is a credit file that does not
otherwise exist anywhere.

THE CATALOGUE IS WHAT TURNS THAT NUMBER INTO A SIGNAL. Without it,
R14,000 is opaque -- money spent on unspecified things. With it, the
basket is readable: maize meal, cooking oil, bread. Staples, stable
demand, price-inelastic, low risk. Change the same basket to
discretionary goods and the risk profile is completely different.

An opaque number is data. A legible basket is an underwriting decision.
That is the whole difference, and it is worth being precise about it
because it is the least obvious link in this document.


2.3 PRICE DATA NOBODY ELSE HOLDS

Once several suppliers quote the same catalogue products, TRU Connect
knows the real wholesale price curve for South African informal retail
-- by product, by region, over time.

Who does NOT have this:

  Stats SA          measures consumer prices, not informal wholesale
  The banks         see payments, not line items
  The FMCG brands   see their own sell-in to distributors, NOT what a
                    spaza actually pays two steps down the chain

That last one is the interesting one. A brand knows what it charged its
distributor. It does not know what the shop paid, and it would like to.

This is genuinely sellable, and it costs nothing extra to produce -- it
is a by-product of doing 2.1 and 2.2 properly. It should be treated as
upside rather than a plan, since it depends on reaching enough density
in a region for the numbers to mean anything.


2.4 A MOAT THAT COMPOUNDS

Most marketplaces have weak network effects. This one has a real one,
and it is worth being specific about the mechanism rather than asserting
"network effects".

Every confirmed product match is stored as an alias: this exact text
means this exact product. The next supplier who writes it that way is
matched instantly and free, forever.

So each supplier who joins makes onboarding CHEAPER AND FASTER for the
next one. A competitor starting today starts at zero and pays full price
for every row, while TRU Connect's cost per supplier keeps falling.

The alias table, the brand dictionary and the confirmed match history
are owned outright. They cannot be repriced, withdrawn, or bought by a
competitor.


===========================================================================
PART 3 — RENTED VERSUS OWNED
===========================================================================

The matching system is built as a ladder. Each rung that resolves a row
means the next never runs:

  tier 0  Barcode         exact identifier            FREE
  tier 1  Learned alias   confirmed before            FREE, and compounds
  tier 2  Normalised      same words, any order       FREE
  tier 3  Rules + fuzzy   brand dictionary,           FREE
                          pack-size rules, trigram
  tier 4  AI              the ambiguous tail          PAID, per row

TIERS 0 TO 3 ARE BUILT AND WORKING. Tier 4 is not, and nothing depends
on it ever being switched on.

The strategic point: THE AI IS RENTED, THE REST IS OWNED. Tier 4 costs
the same per row forever and is subject to somebody else's pricing. The
alias table, brand dictionary and confirmed matches are assets that grow
on their own and cost nothing to keep.

Tier 4 is scaffolding to build the owned asset faster. If it were
switched off permanently, matching would keep working -- just with a
longer human review queue.

Estimated cost to onboard a 4,000-product supplier with tier 4 enabled:
roughly R110, one time, falling as tiers 0 to 3 absorb more. With
barcodes present in the file, materially less. Price updates re-run only
what changed.


===========================================================================
PART 4 — THE LONGER-TERM VERSION
===========================================================================

Tier 3 currently scores candidates with four hand-picked weights:
similarity, plus adjustments for brand agreement and pack-size
agreement. Those numbers are an educated guess.

Meanwhile every proposal is recorded with its features and, once
reviewed, marked accepted or rejected. That is labelled training data,
accumulating from day one at no cost.

So the path to a proprietary model is not "build an ML platform". It is:
once there are a few thousand reviewed rows, fit a small classifier on
those same features and replace four constants with learned ones. One
function changes; the tiers, the gate and the queue stay exactly as they
are.

THE HONEST DEPENDENCY: this needs a REVIEWER. The admin control centre
does not exist yet, so labels currently accumulate unreviewed. The
bottleneck is a person, not the technology, and that should be said
plainly rather than presented as imminent.


===========================================================================
PART 5 — WHAT IS NOT TRUE YET
===========================================================================

Read this section before any investor conversation.

  THE CATALOGUE IS EMPTY. The schema exists, the matcher works, and the
  tables have no rows in them. Nothing is matched today.

  TIER 4 IS NOT BUILT. Extraction and adjudication by model are
  designed, budgeted and unbuilt.

  NOBODY CAN REVIEW. The queue records proposals; no interface exists
  for a person to accept or reject one.

  THE CSV DOES NOT YET CARRY BARCODES. The matcher accepts a barcode;
  the import parser does not read one yet, so tier 0 is unreachable from
  a real upload.

  NO SUPPLIER HAS BEEN ONBOARDED THIS WAY. Every cost figure in Part 3
  is an estimate from token pricing, not a measurement.

  THE PRICE DATA IS WORTH NOTHING YET. It requires density -- several
  suppliers quoting the same products in the same region -- and there is
  none.

  LENDING IS NOT BUILT, and the underwriting case in 2.2 is an argument,
  not a validated model.

WHAT IS SAFE TO CLAIM: the architecture is built, tested and in
production code; matching works today on four free tiers; the design
deliberately makes the paid tier optional; and the asset that compounds
is owned rather than rented. All of that is verifiable by reading the
repository.


===========================================================================
PART 6 — THE RISK THAT MATTERS
===========================================================================

All of the above depends on the catalogue being ACCURATE.

A catalogue with wrong matches is worse than no catalogue. The price
comparison becomes a lie, the lending signal becomes noise, and the
price data becomes unsellable -- and unlike a missing feature, a wrong
match is invisible until someone catches it, by which time it has been
believed.

This is why the system has a confidence gate that refuses to decide in
the middle band, why a wrong match is treated as strictly worse than no
match, and why the review queue is expected to be long rather than
empty. The caution is not conservatism; it is protecting the only asset
here that matters.
