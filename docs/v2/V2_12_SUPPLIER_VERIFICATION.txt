V2_12 — SUPPLIER VERIFICATION: IDENTITY, DOCUMENTS, AND CUSTODY
===========================================================================

Written 2026-09-11. A PLAN, not an implementation. Nothing in here is
built; src/domains/verification/ and src/domains/trust/ are still empty
scaffolding — seven __init__.py files and no code.

WHAT THIS INHERITS, and does not re-argue:

  V2_03 Part 4   decided the STRATEGY. Verification is a supplier
                 problem, not a business one. It is TWO gates —
                 identity and catalogue. The architecture is a CURTAIN,
                 not a wall: a supplier builds everything and sells
                 nothing until verified. Read that first; this document
                 assumes it.

  V2_11          decided the CATALOGUE gate. The matching ladder is
                 already a risk classifier: AUTO_LINK is low risk,
                 CREATE_NEW is the content-review queue. That gate is
                 partly built (MatchReview, flask catalogue reviews).

  This document  is the IDENTITY gate, and specifically the part V2_03
                 waved at in one line: "document upload and review
                 (Cloudinary is already the media provider)". That line
                 hides the single most sensitive thing this platform
                 will ever hold, and it needs its own design.

THE ONE-SENTENCE VERSION. We will hold South African ID documents and
company registration certificates belonging to real people, and the
plan has to be good enough that a leak is survivable — which mostly
means holding as little as possible, for as short a time as possible,
behind a door that is shut by default.


===========================================================================
PART 1 — HOW LARGE PLATFORMS ACTUALLY DO THIS
===========================================================================

1.1 THE INDUSTRY NAME FOR IT

  Customer-facing identity checks are KYC (Know Your Customer). The
  seller-side equivalent is KYB — Know Your BUSINESS — and a marketplace
  onboarding a merchant runs both: KYB on the entity, KYC on the humans
  behind it.

  A single KYB session in commercial tooling covers: registry lookup,
  UBO identification, owner KYC, entity AML screening, and document
  checks. The documents most commonly demanded are certificate of
  incorporation, business licence, bank statements, utility bills, and
  proof of identity for the owners.
                       — Didit; Stripe; Resistant.ai; Identomat

  UBO — Ultimate Beneficial Owner — means the actual humans who own or
  control the entity, conventionally anyone holding 25% or more.

1.2 THE PATTERN WORTH COPYING: RISK-BASED TIERING

  The most useful finding in the research is that mature platforms do
  NOT verify everyone to the same depth.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Requirements scale with EXPOSURE — transaction volume, transaction
  value, category risk — not with a single rule applied to everybody.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                       — Didit, "Marketplace KYB"; Stripe onboarding guide

  This matters enormously for TruConnect, because our supplier
  population is not uniform. A registered wholesaler moving R2m a month
  and a one-person bakery supplying four spazas are both "suppliers",
  and demanding a tax clearance certificate from the second is how you
  lose them on day one. V2_03's own thesis — the student selling perfume
  is a real TruConnect business — applies on the supply side too.

1.3 WHAT TAKEALOT ASKS, AS THE LOCAL BENCHMARK

  Worth knowing precisely, because it is the bar South African sellers
  have already met elsewhere and will compare us to:

    CIPC registration document, tax clearance, bank confirmation
    letter, proof of address (all less than 3 months old), plus a valid
    SA ID or passport for the individual and for each director.
    Application takes 2–5 business days.
                       — SME South Africa; VerifyNow; Takealot seller guides

  AND THE DETAIL THAT MATTERS MOST: a business does NOT need to be
  CIPC-registered to sell on Takealot. VAT registration is recommended
  but only becomes mandatory above R1m turnover — which is SARS law,
  not Takealot policy.

  So even the strictest local marketplace does not require company
  registration. That is a deliberate commercial choice, and we should
  make the same one for the same reason: requiring it would exclude
  most of the informal supply chain this platform exists to serve.

1.4 REGISTRY LOOKUP IS CHEAP AND WE SHOULD USE IT

  CIPC — the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission — is the
  registrar for every South African company, and its data is reachable
  by API through several commercial providers (Didit, Datanamix,
  Standard Bank SearchWorks, and scraper services).
                       — CIPC / Wikipedia; Didit; Datanamix; Standard Bank

  A registration-number lookup returns company name, status, directors
  and registered address. That single call does something a human
  reading a PDF cannot: it checks the certificate against the REGISTRY
  rather than against itself. A forged certificate looks perfect; a
  forged certificate whose number belongs to a deregistered plumbing
  company does not.

  DEFERRED, NOT SKIPPED. This is a paid third-party dependency and a
  GeocodingProvider-shaped abstraction (V2_08's third-party layer). It
  is Phase 3 below. But the DATA MODEL should record where a fact came
  from — supplier-asserted vs registry-confirmed — from day one, or
  retrofitting provenance later means re-reviewing every supplier.


===========================================================================
PART 2 — WHAT WE ARE ACTUALLY VERIFYING
===========================================================================

Verification is not one question. Naming the questions separately is
what stops the design collapsing into "collect documents, feel safe".

  Q1  DOES THIS BUSINESS EXIST?
      A registered entity, or an identifiable trading person.
      Evidence: CIPC certificate, or an ID plus a trading name.

  Q2  IS THE PERSON IN FRONT OF US CONNECTED TO IT?
      The account holder is a director, owner, or authorised agent.
      Evidence: ID matched against the directors on the registry.

  Q3  CAN MONEY REACH THEM SAFELY?
      A bank account in the entity's name, not a stranger's.
      Evidence: bank confirmation letter.

  Q4  ARE THEY WHERE THEY SAY THEY ARE?
      Evidence: proof of address, or the geocoded pin already collected.

  Q5  WHAT ARE THEY SELLING?
      THE CATALOGUE GATE. Not this document — V2_11 and V2_03 §4.2.
      Listed here only so the boundary is explicit: passing Q1–Q4 says
      nothing whatsoever about Q5, and the terbufos case in V2_03 §4.2
      is what happens when a platform confuses the two.

WHICH QUESTIONS APPLY TO WHOM. Q1 and Q2 are the floor — everyone.
Q3 becomes load-bearing the moment digital payments carry real money
(V2_03 §3.3: only digital transactions are PROVEN). Q4 is the weakest
signal and the most annoying to supply; treat it as corroboration, not
a gate.


===========================================================================
PART 3 — THE TIERS, AND WHAT EACH ONE COSTS THE SUPPLIER
===========================================================================

Three tiers, mapping onto the SupplierVerificationStatus column that
already exists, plus what each unlocks. The principle from 1.2: ask for
the minimum that makes the next thing safe.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  TIER 0 — UNVERIFIED                          (the column's default)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Asks for      nothing beyond sign-up.
  Can do        build a profile, import a full catalogue, set terms,
                invite connections, use the API.
  Cannot do     be seen. No offer is visible to any business.

  This is the CURTAIN from V2_03 §4.5. It is the whole reason we can be
  strict without being hostile: the work is never blocked, only the
  selling.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  TIER 1 — IDENTITY VERIFIED                   (answers Q1 + Q2)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Asks for      ONE of:
                  (a) CIPC registration number + a director's SA ID
                  (b) SA ID + a declared trading name, for an
                      unregistered trader
  Can do        sell. Offers become visible (subject to the catalogue
                gate per offer). Badge shown.

  PATH (b) IS NOT A LOOPHOLE, IT IS THE MARKET. Per 1.3, even Takealot
  does not require CIPC registration. An unregistered trader who gives
  us a real, checkable ID is more accountable than an anonymous one,
  and refusing them would empty the platform.

  The two paths produce DIFFERENT badges. See Part 4.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  TIER 2 — FINANCIALLY VERIFIED                (adds Q3)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Asks for      bank confirmation letter in the entity's name
  Can do        receive digital payment; be eligible for anything that
                depends on a proven ledger (V2_03 §3.3).
  Trigger       reaching a payment threshold, NOT sign-up.

  DELIBERATELY DEFERRED UNTIL THE MONEY IS REAL. Asking a supplier for
  bank documents before they have made a single sale is the highest-
  friction question at the lowest-trust moment.


===========================================================================
PART 4 — THE BADGE MUST NOT LIE
===========================================================================

A verification badge is a claim TruConnect makes to a business who is
about to send money. It has to mean something specific.

  PROBLEM WITH A SINGLE TICK. If path (a) and path (b) of Tier 1 both
  render as one generic "Verified", the badge means "we checked
  something" — which is exactly the kind of reassurance that is worse
  than none, because a business reads it as "TruConnect vouches for
  this company".

  SO THE BADGE STATES WHAT WAS CHECKED:

    "Registered business"      CIPC number confirmed against the
                               registry. Company name shown as the
                               registry spells it.
    "Identity verified"        a real person is accountable for this
                               account; the business is not registered.
    "Payments verified"        bank account confirmed in the entity's
                               name.

  These stack. A supplier can hold all three.

  AND THE PROVENANCE RULE: never show a fact as confirmed when it was
  only asserted. If the CIPC lookup has not run (Phase 3), the badge
  says "Registration number supplied" — not "Registered business". This
  is why Part 1.4 insists the model records provenance from day one.


===========================================================================
PART 5 — CUSTODY: THE PART THAT MATTERS MOST
===========================================================================

This is the section the whole document exists for.

5.1 A FINDING FROM THE CODEBASE, NOT A HYPOTHETICAL

  The existing media path CANNOT safely carry these documents, and the
  reason is specific rather than vague:

    src/shared/media/providers/cloudinary_provider.py
      generate_signed_upload() passes no `type` parameter.

  Cloudinary's default delivery type is `upload`, which is PUBLIC — the
  asset is readable by anyone who has the URL, with no authentication
  at all. Cloudinary offers `private` and `authenticated` types where
  the original is reachable only through a signed, time-limited URL.
                       — Cloudinary, "Media Access Control and Authentication"

  It also passes allowed_formats = ALLOWED_IMAGE_FORMATS (no PDF) and
  an incoming transformation that resizes to a max dimension — which
  would degrade a document to the point of illegibility.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  IF VERIFICATION DOCUMENTS WERE POSTED THROUGH TODAY'S MEDIA PATH,
  SOUTH AFRICAN ID DOCUMENTS WOULD BE WORLD-READABLE BY URL.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Not a criticism of that code — it was built for product photographs,
  which are meant to be public, and it does that correctly. It is a
  statement that verification needs a SECOND path, and that reusing the
  first one by habit is the failure mode to guard against.

5.2 HASH OR ENCRYPT? THEY ANSWER DIFFERENT QUESTIONS

  The question was raised as "encrypted in the db or hashed". They are
  not alternatives, and choosing wrongly breaks the feature:

    HASHING is one-way. You can never read it back. Correct for data
    you only ever need to COMPARE.

    ENCRYPTION is two-way. Correct for data a human must later READ.

  A reviewer has to LOOK at an ID document. Hashing it would make it
  permanently unreadable — the equivalent of shredding the evidence and
  keeping the receipt. So:

    THE DOCUMENT ITSELF        encrypted at rest, or held by the
                               provider under authenticated delivery.
                               Never hashed.
    THE ID NUMBER              hashed (salted, or HMAC with a server
                               key). We never need to display it; we
                               need to ask "have we seen this person
                               before?" — which a hash answers.
    THE COMPANY NUMBER         stored in clear. It is public registry
                               data; CIPC will tell anyone who asks.
    THE DECISION               stored in clear. Who approved, when, on
                               what grounds.

  WHY HASH THE ID NUMBER AT ALL, rather than encrypt it. Because it
  removes a temptation. An encrypted field can be decrypted by any
  future feature that asks nicely; a hash cannot. The only legitimate
  uses are dedup and re-check, and both work on a hash.

5.3 THE STRONGEST CONTROL IS NOT HOLDING IT

  POPIA's minimisation principle: collect only what is necessary, keep
  it only as long as necessary, define a retention period for every
  category. An SA ID number is personal information; biometric data is
  SPECIAL personal information with a higher bar again.
                       — POPIA §26; VerifyNow; Termly; idchecker.co.za

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  THE DOCUMENT IS EVIDENCE FOR A DECISION. ONCE THE DECISION IS MADE,
  THE EVIDENCE IS A LIABILITY, NOT AN ASSET.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  So the lifecycle is deliberately short:

    upload → review → decide → EXTRACT the few fields that matter →
    DELETE the document → keep the decision forever

  What survives deletion: the decision, who made it, when, the document
  TYPE that was seen, its hash (proof of what was reviewed, without the
  contents), and the extracted fields. That is enough to defend the
  decision in an audit and not enough to leak somebody's identity
  document.

  PROPOSED RETENTION: destroy on decision + 30 days (a window for
  appeal per V2_03 §4.4), or immediately on rejection-and-resubmit.
  Rejected suppliers must not accumulate a shadow archive of failed
  attempts.

5.4 THE CONCRETE STORAGE DESIGN

  DEFAULT: authenticated delivery + short-lived signed URLs.

    · a new MediaProvider capability — delivery_type — with
      verification uploads requesting `authenticated`
    · the reviewer's view fetches a signed URL valid for MINUTES,
      minted per view, never stored and never put in a template
    · X-Robots-Tag: noindex on the asset, per Cloudinary's guidance for
      confidential assets
    · no transformation on the incoming file; PDF and image formats
      allowed; a hard size cap
    · malware scanning ON (the Perception Point hook already exists in
      the provider) — an uploaded "certificate" is an executable often
      enough to matter

  AND A LIMIT WORTH STATING PLAINLY: with authenticated delivery the
  bytes still live on a third party's infrastructure, and Cloudinary
  can read them. That is acceptable for a document we delete in 30
  days, and it is why 5.3 matters more than 5.4. If we ever hold
  documents long-term, the answer is application-level encryption
  before upload — the provider then stores ciphertext it cannot read —
  and that is a Phase 4 decision, not a launch blocker.

  NEVER, UNDER ANY CIRCUMSTANCES:
    · a verification document delivered from a public URL
    · a document URL in an email, a notification, or a log line
    · an ID number in an application log
    · a document reachable by guessing an incrementing id
    · a reviewer downloading documents to a laptop

5.5 THE ACCESS RULE

  Reading a verification document is a PRIVILEGED ACT and must be
  gated by the capability seam already built:

    supplier:verify        already declared in
                           src/cli/_shared/capabilities.py

  Today that resolves to "is an active admin". When staff roles split,
  it resolves to a grant — and no call site changes.

  EVERY READ IS LOGGED. Not the decision — the READ. "Who looked at
  this person's ID, and when" is the question that gets asked after a
  leak, and a system that cannot answer it has to assume the worst
  about everyone. The audit domain already exists for this.


===========================================================================
PART 6 — THE DOMAIN ARCHITECTURE
===========================================================================

6.1 WHERE THE CODE GOES

  src/domains/verification/   — scaffolded and empty; this fills it.

  Verification is NOT a supplier-domain feature, and putting it there
  would be the easy mistake. It is a platform function performed ON a
  supplier BY staff, with its own lifecycle, its own retention rules,
  and its own access control. The supplier domain reads one field from
  it (the status) and knows nothing else.

    verification/
      models/         VerificationCase, VerificationDocument,
                      VerificationDecision
      repositories/   the ONLY code that touches document rows
      services/       case lifecycle, decisions, retention sweep
      schemas/        supplier-facing (status) and staff-facing (case)
      api/            supplier: submit, check status
                      staff:    queue, view, decide
      schedulers/     retention sweep JobSpec (deletes expired docs)
      providers/      registry lookup (CIPC) behind an interface

6.2 THE THREE TABLES

  VERIFICATION_CASE — one per supplier per attempt
    supplier_id, tier requested, status, submitted_at, decided_at,
    decided_by_user_id, decided_by_source, reason

    Mirrors MatchReview deliberately. The catalogue review queue
    already proved this shape, and a reviewer working two queues with
    the same vocabulary makes fewer mistakes than one learning two.

  VERIFICATION_DOCUMENT — one per uploaded file
    case_id, document_type, provider_public_id, sha256, uploaded_at,
    deleted_at, scan_status

    sha256 OUTLIVES THE FILE. After deletion the row remains, proving
    which exact bytes were reviewed without holding them.

  VERIFICATION_FACT — one per extracted, checkable claim
    case_id, fact_type (company_number / id_number_hash /
    bank_account_name), value, provenance (ASSERTED / DOCUMENT /
    REGISTRY), confirmed_at

    PROVENANCE IS A COLUMN, not a comment. Part 4's badge rule is
    unenforceable without it.

6.3 THE VISIBILITY RULE BELONGS IN THE REPOSITORY

  V2_03 §4.5 already committed to this and it is worth restating,
  because it is the rule most likely to be quietly broken later:

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  "The offer-visibility rule, enforced in the REPOSITORY layer, not in
  each route. A visibility rule applied per-endpoint is a rule that
  leaks the first time somebody adds an endpoint."
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  The same discipline the architecture tests already enforce for
  shared/finance applies here: an offer query that does not filter on
  supplier verification should be impossible to write by accident, not
  merely discouraged in review.

  RECOMMENDED: an architecture test that AST-parses for supplier-offer
  queries outside the sanctioned repository, in the same commit as the
  rule itself.

PART 7 — WHAT THE SUPPLIER EXPERIENCES
===========================================================================

From V2_03 §4.4, restated as requirements rather than principles:

  · Listing is never blocked.
  · An offer held for review says SO, with a plain reason and an
    expected time.
  · A rejection names the policy it breached and can be appealed.
  · The reminder is persistent and not cruel: the message is "your
    shop is ready and nobody can see it yet", never "you are not
    verified".

ADD, from this document:

  · TELL THEM WHAT HAPPENS TO THE DOCUMENT. At upload: what we will
    look at, how long we keep it, that it is deleted after the
    decision. POPIA requires openness; it is also simply the difference
    between a supplier who uploads an ID and one who abandons onboarding.
  · SHOW THE TIER LADDER. What they have, what the next one unlocks,
    what it costs them to reach it. A tier system that is invisible is
    experienced as an arbitrary refusal.
  · NEVER ASK TWICE. If CIPC confirms the company name, do not also
    demand a proof of address that says the same thing.


===========================================================================
PART 8 — BUILD ORDER
===========================================================================

  PHASE 1 — THE CURTAIN, WITHOUT ANY DOCUMENTS AT ALL
    The visibility rule in the repository layer, the architecture test,
    the supplier-facing status screen and reminder ladder. Verification
    is granted by hand — `flask verification approve` — by an admin who
    looked at something out-of-band.

    WHY FIRST: it is the piece that makes the platform safe, it holds
    no personal data whatsoever, and it is the prerequisite for every
    other phase. A curtain with a manual switch is worth more than a
    document pipeline with no curtain.

  PHASE 2 — DOCUMENT INTAKE AND REVIEW
    MediaProvider gains delivery_type. Verification uploads go to
    authenticated delivery. The three tables, the staff queue, the
    retention sweep, read-auditing, the supplier-facing submit flow.
    Reviewing happens through `flask verification ...`, mirroring the
    catalogue commands.

  PHASE 3 — REGISTRY CONFIRMATION
    CIPC lookup behind a provider interface. Promotes facts from
    ASSERTED to REGISTRY. Turns "Registration number supplied" into
    "Registered business".

  PHASE 4 — SCALE AND TIGHTEN
    Tier 2 / bank verification when payments are real. AI extraction to
    reduce transcription (never to decide — V2_03 §4.3: an AI saying
    "this is fine" is dangerous, an AI saying "look at this" is free).
    Application-level encryption if retention ever lengthens.

THE HONEST WARNING, INHERITED FROM V2_03 §4.4 AND STILL TRUE: this is
the biggest ongoing OPERATIONAL cost in the plan. Every other feature
runs itself. This one needs a person, indefinitely.


===========================================================================
PART 9 — DECISIONS NEEDED BEFORE PHASE 2
===========================================================================

Phase 1 can start on what is already agreed. These block Phase 2:

  1  TIER 1 PATH (b) — do we accept an unregistered trader with only
     an ID? Recommended YES (Part 1.3, Part 3). It is the difference
     between serving this market and describing it.

  2  RETENTION WINDOW — 30 days after decision proposed. Shorter is
     safer; longer is friendlier to appeals.

  3  WHO REVIEWS — you alone for now, via supplier:verify. When does
     that stop being viable, and what is the trigger to hire?

  4  REJECTION AND RE-SUBMISSION — how many attempts before an account
     is flagged rather than merely refused?

  5  THE DOCUMENT SET — the minimum list for Tier 1 path (a) and path
     (b). Part 3 proposes it; it should be confirmed rather than
     assumed, because every item added costs onboarding completion.

  6  CIPC PROVIDER — commercial API (Didit / Datanamix / Standard
     Bank) or deferred entirely? Phase 3, but the data model needs to
     know it is coming.
