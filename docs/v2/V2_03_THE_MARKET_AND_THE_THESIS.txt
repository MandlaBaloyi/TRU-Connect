V2_03 — THE MARKET, THE THESIS, AND WHAT EACH TAB IS FOR
===========================================================================

Written 2026-09-08. REVISED 2026-09-09 after review — the revision changed
four substantive things, and they are marked [REVISED] where they appear:
the definition of who this is for, the Order Book / stock orders
distinction (which the first draft got factually wrong), what "money"
means on Home, and where verification is actually enforced.

This document exists because the next phase is not a feature list. It is a
set of bets, and a bet you cannot say out loud is a bet you cannot check
later. Everything here is either sourced from research (cited inline) or
derived from the codebase as it stands today.

Read this one first. V2_04 through V2_08 are the designs; this is the
reasoning they inherit.


===========================================================================
PART 1 — WHO THIS IS ACTUALLY FOR                              [REVISED]
===========================================================================

The first draft of this document talked about spaza shops almost
exclusively. That was too narrow, and the narrowness would have leaked
into the product.

  A student who sells perfume to other students, and buys that perfume
  from a supplier on TruConnect, is a TruConnect business. Nothing about
  them is a spaza shop and nothing should stop them ordering.

  A person selling kota who writes their customers' orders in a paper
  book is a TruConnect business the day they use Order Book — even if
  they never place a single stock order with us.

So: THE INFORMAL ECONOMY, NOT THE SPAZA SECTOR. Home-based sellers,
street traders, salons and barbers, food vendors, tuck shops, students
reselling, market traders, small caterers, anyone running a trade without
a formal storefront. Spaza shops are the largest and best-documented
segment, which is why the research below is about them, but they are a
segment and not the definition.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  PRINCIPLE: TOOLS MUST STAND ALONE.
  A business that uses ONLY Order Book, and never buys anything through
  TruConnect, is a complete and valid user. Not a half-user, not a
  funnel stage, not someone to be nudged toward the marketplace.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

That principle has real consequences, so it is worth being explicit:

  · No tool may require a supplier connection to work.
  · No tool may be gated behind placing an order.
  · Onboarding must not assume the person wants to buy stock from us.
  · A tool being someone's only reason to open the app is a SUCCESS,
    not a conversion problem.

It is also, commercially, the stronger position. A marketplace with
150,000 possible shops is a market. A set of tools for everyone trading
informally in South Africa is a much larger one, and it is the half
nobody else is building — the competitors in Part 2 all died trying to
own distribution.

THE SPAZA NUMBERS, which still matter as the anchor segment:

  Over 150,000 spaza shops in South Africa.
  ~R120 billion a year to the economy.
  30–40% of national food expenditure.
  Only ~1% accept electronic payments.
                       — Kazang / Ventureburn, 2025; BFA Global

  Stokvels: ~800,000 groups, ~11 million members, >R50 billion held.
                       — Ipsos market study, 2024

A third of the country's food spend moves through shops with no stock
records and no card machine. The absence of records is not a detail — it
is the whole opportunity and the whole difficulty at once.


===========================================================================
PART 2 — WHAT KILLED THE COMPETITION, AND WHY IT MATTERS TO EVERY
         DECISION BELOW
===========================================================================

Between 2022 and 2024, the African B2B e-commerce cohort — Wasoko,
Sokowatch, Alerzo, MarketForce, Twiga — went from investor darlings to
shutdowns, mergers and mass layoffs. The post-mortems agree on the causes
(Rest of World, 2023):

  1. ASSET-HEAVY. They built their own warehouses and delivery fleets
     because the logistics did not exist. "The cost of maintaining these
     assets will always eat into its margins."

  2. DISCOUNTS AS ACQUISITION. Up to 30% below market to win merchants.
     Unsustainable the moment funding tightened.

  3. NO STICKINESS. Retailers cycled between platforms chasing the better
     offer. A race to the bottom with nothing holding anyone in place.

  4. UNIT ECONOMICS NOBODY HAD CHECKED.

  What survived did so by going ASSET-LIGHT and adding FINTECH —
  working capital, BNPL, point-of-sale — for margin and retention.

TruConnect is already on the right side of three of those four, by
architecture rather than by luck:

  We hold no stock.          There is no warehouse to pay for.
  We run no trucks.          Delivery is the supplier's own arrangement,
                             or a partner's (V2_08), never our fleet.
  We set no prices.          A supplier prices their own offer. We
                             cannot discount our way to growth even if
                             we wanted to.

That leaves stickiness, and Part 3 is about it.

ONE ADDITION FROM THE REVIEW. "Asset-light" does not mean "CRUD screens
only". The survivors added fintech and point-of-sale — services they
brokered rather than owned. TruConnect can broker too: delivery for a
supplier who has none, airtime float for a business that resells it. We
arrange; a licensed partner performs. That is still asset-light, and it
is where the margin is. V2_08 is the whole design.

PART 3 — STICKINESS, AND THE VERIFIABLE LEDGER               [REVISED]
===========================================================================

The most useful sentence in the research:

  "What brings a shopkeeper back is seeing their own prices, the schemes
   they qualify for, what they owe, and where the last order is — not a
   mandate."
                       — African Marketing Confederation / AVODA

3.1 THE MOAT IS THE MERCHANT'S OWN RECORDS

  Community is where a business finds a supplier. Valuable, and
  completely substitutable — the next app shows them the same suppliers
  tomorrow.

  The tools hold something no competitor can show them, and re-entering
  it into a rival app costs evenings. That is what stops the cycling
  that killed Alerzo.

    Community brings them in. The tools are why they stay.

3.2 THE CORRECTION: THREE LEDGERS, NOT TWO           [REVISED — WAS WRONG]

  The first draft treated "Order Book" and "what they ordered from
  suppliers" as the same thing. They are not, and merging them would
  have produced a genuinely broken feature.

    LOAN BOOK      Money owed TO the business by its own customers —
                   the neighbour who took bread on credit.
                   business/loan_book. Self-reported.

    ORDER BOOK     Orders the business's OWN CUSTOMERS place with it —
                   the kota seller's order list. Its types file is
                   explicit: "A customer of THIS business."
                   Self-reported. Nothing to do with suppliers.

    STOCK ORDERS   What the business ordered FROM A SUPPLIER through
                   TruConnect. business/orders/ + checkout.stock_orders.
                   PLATFORM-OBSERVED, not self-reported.

  All three are already built. The gap is that STOCK ORDERS is reachable
  only from the cart and an overflow menu — it was never wired into the
  Account tab alongside the other two, so the business has two tools in
  their toolbox and a third one hidden in a drawer. That wiring is a
  Phase-3 item in V2_07 and it is small.

  THE DISTINCTION MATTERS BEYOND TIDINESS, because of 3.3.

3.3 ONLY DIGITAL PAYMENTS PRODUCE A VERIFIABLE LEDGER

  This is the sharpest idea to come out of the review and it reframes
  the financing question entirely.

  Loan Book and Order Book are the merchant's own record-keeping. We
  cannot verify them. A shopkeeper may record every sale, or three sales
  a week, or use Order Book for something we never anticipated. That is
  fine — it is their book — but it can never be evidence.

  A STOCK ORDER PAID DIGITALLY IS DIFFERENT. The platform observed it:
  a real business ordered real goods from a real supplier and the money
  actually moved. Nobody's honesty is being taken on trust.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Cash transactions are RECORDED. Digital transactions are PROVEN.
  Only the proven ones can ever back a financing conversation.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Which produces the actual, honest version of the "financial footprint"
  argument — and it runs in both directions:

    BUSINESS SIDE   A business with nine months of repeat, digitally
                    paid stock orders has demonstrated real, recurring
                    trade. That is exactly the record a lender cannot
                    get today, because financial institutions do not
                    lend to informal retailers who "mostly trade in
                    cash and have no financial footprint to determine
                    their creditworthiness" (BFA Global). We do not
                    lend. We can be the reason a partner will.

    SUPPLIER SIDE   A supplier with a rising volume of digitally paid
                    orders through the platform has a receivables
                    history. Same argument, larger numbers, and it is
                    the more fundable side.

  WE ARE NOT A LENDER AND SHOULD NOT BECOME ONE. No capital, no credit
  licence, and lending against your own marketplace's merchants is a
  conflict before it is a business. What we can be is the party holding
  a verified record that a licensed partner underwrites against. See
  V2_08.

  AND IT SETTLES A PRODUCT ARGUMENT. This is the real reason to make
  digital payment attractive rather than merely available — not because
  we earn on the transaction, but because it is the only transaction
  that can ever be worth something to the merchant later. Worth saying
  to them plainly, too: "paying digitally builds a record you can use."
  That is true, and it is a better reason than a discount.

  WHAT IT DOES NOT LICENCE. We do not degrade the cash path, hide it, or
  nag. Cash is how this economy works and a platform that punishes it
  has misunderstood its users.

PART 4 — VERIFICATION IS A SUPPLIER PROBLEM, NOT A BUSINESS ONE [REVISED]
===========================================================================

The first draft implied compliance pressure applies to both sides. It
does not, and the correction is an architectural requirement rather than
a wording fix. A second review (2026-09-11) added 4.2-4.4: verification
is TWO gates, identity and catalogue, and the first draft only had one.

4.1 THE ASYMMETRY

  A BUSINESS SIGNS UP AND TRADES. No documents, no ID, no permit
  demanded by us. The student selling perfume does not have a municipal
  licence and does not need one to place an order. Regulatory
  registration is a real obligation THEY have to THEIR municipality, and
  Discovery can help them meet it (V2_05) — but it is never a gate we
  impose.

  A SUPPLIER IS VERIFIED, AND STRICTLY. Documents, registered name, ID.
  A supplier is who a business hands money to, so this is where trust
  has to be earned. It also feeds the TruConnect verification badge that
  already exists as SupplierVerificationStatus.

4.2 TWO GATES, NOT ONE                          [ADDED 2026-09-11]

  The first version of this section had one gate: documents. Review
  caught that it is not enough, and the gap is serious.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  IDENTITY   Who are you?      Documents, registered name, ID.
  CATALOGUE  What are you      Review of the OFFERS themselves.
             selling?
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  A supplier can be entirely genuine — real company, real documents,
  verified identity — and still list something that must not be sold
  through this platform. Identity verification says nothing about
  contents. Treating it as though it did is the mistake.

  WHY THIS IS NOT THEORETICAL IN THIS MARKET. The 2024 poisonings that
  made spaza registration compulsory killed at least 23 children. The
  Naledi deaths were traced to terbufos — a WHO Class 1a agricultural
  pesticide, illegal for domestic use — found in a snack. Inspectors
  investigating found illegal pesticides being sold from spaza shops,
  and food stored alongside pesticides and detergents. The Minister of
  Agriculture banned terbufos outright in May 2026.
                       — Daily Maverick; Mail & Guardian; SAnews; NICD

  A platform that connects suppliers to shops, in this country, at this
  moment, with no view of what is being listed, is not a platform with
  a missing feature. Every document check in the world would have let
  that through.

4.3 HOW TO REVIEW A CATALOGUE WITHOUT A REVIEW DEPARTMENT

  The obvious objection: a wholesaler with 3,000 lines cannot wait for
  a human to read each one, and we do not have anyone to read them.

  THE MATCHING LADDER IS ALREADY A RISK CLASSIFIER, and this is the
  part worth noticing. It was built to answer "which catalogue product
  is this?" and it answers a second question for free:

    AUTO_LINK    resolved to an EXISTING catalogue product with high
                 confidence. Somebody has seen this product before and
                 it is already in the shared catalogue.
                 -> low risk. Auto-approve.

    REVIEW       ambiguous.
                 -> already queues for a human. Unchanged.

    CREATE_NEW   matched nothing. By definition nobody has ever vetted
                 this product on this platform.
                 -> THIS IS THE CONTENT REVIEW QUEUE. It already
                    exists, as MatchReview rows.

  So the volume that needs human eyes is not "every offer" — it is
  "every offer that introduces something new to the catalogue". On the
  measured two-supplier trial the free tiers resolved 80%, which means
  roughly one line in five, falling as the catalogue grows. The first
  supplier in a category is expensive to review; the fiftieth is nearly
  free. That is the catalogue paying for itself a second time.

  PLUS A HARD LIST THAT SKIPS THE LADDER ENTIRELY. Some things are
  never auto-approved regardless of how confidently they match:

    PROHIBITED — may not be listed at all
      agricultural pesticides and poisons, veterinary medicines,
      prescription and OTC medicines (SAHPRA), tobacco and vapes,
      alcohol (a liquor licence is a separate regime), firearms and
      ammunition, counterfeit goods, anything requiring a cold chain we
      cannot verify.

    RESTRICTED — allowed only with a named permit on file
      infant formula, raw/unpasteurised dairy, fresh meat and poultry,
      prepared foods.

  WHO DOES THE REVIEWING, EARLY ON. The developer, assisted by AI, with
  a human decision required whenever the documents or the offer look
  suspicious. That is the right shape, and one asymmetry makes it safe:

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  AN AI SAYING "THIS IS FINE" IS DANGEROUS.
  AN AI SAYING "LOOK AT THIS" IS FREE.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  So bias every automated step toward FLAGGING, never toward CLEARING.
  Three things automation does well here:

    TRIAGE      sort the queue so the riskiest item is first. Costs
                nothing when wrong.
    EXTRACTION  read a CIPC certificate and pull out the company number
                so a person VERIFIES rather than transcribes. The
                document is still seen.
    FLAGGING    "this offer's name suggests a restricted category". A
                false positive costs ten seconds; a false negative is
                the terbufos case.

  The auto-approve path is not AI at all -- AUTO_LINK means the ladder
  resolved the offer to a product already vetted, deterministically.
  That is the cheap majority, and it is rules.

  AND THE ECONOMICS ARE THE OPPOSITE WAY ROUND FROM MATCHING, which is
  why this is affordable when the matching AI tier is not:

    verification   per SUPPLIER, once, a handful of documents
    matching       per ROW, forever, thousands per file

  Same model, same price per call, completely different bill. That is
  the whole reason the matching AI tier sits behind a spend guard and
  document assistance does not need to.

  THE LIST IS NOT A KEYWORD FILTER. A keyword filter is trivially
  evaded ("terbufos" spelled wrong) and constantly wrong ("baby powder"
  is talc, not formula). The list decides what a REVIEWER looks at and
  what a supplier ATTESTS to; matching decides what reaches a reviewer.
  Automation narrows the queue; it does not make the decision.

4.4 WHAT THE SUPPLIER SEES, AND WHAT WE OWE THEM

  · Listing is never blocked. Build the whole catalogue.
  · Offers are invisible until BOTH gates pass — identity verified AND
    the offer approved.
  · An offer held for review says so, with a plain reason and an
    expected time. "Pending review" with no explanation and no date is
    how a supplier concludes the platform is broken.
  · A rejection names the policy it breached and can be appealed.
  · Prohibited-category attempts are recorded. A supplier repeatedly
    trying to list poisons is not a catalogue problem, it is an account
    problem.

  AND THE HONEST WARNING: this is the single biggest operational cost
  in the whole plan. Every other item is code that runs itself. This
  one needs a PERSON, at least until the catalogue is dense enough that
  the CREATE_NEW rate collapses. Pretending otherwise is how platforms
  end up with review queues nobody reads.

4.5 THE ARCHITECTURE: NOT A WALL, A CURTAIN

  The requirement is precise, and it is a better design than a hard
  gate:

    A supplier signs up, completes their profile, and lists their
    entire catalogue WITHOUT verification. Nothing blocks them.

    Their OFFERS ARE NOT VISIBLE to any business until verification is
    complete.

  Everything is buildable, nothing is sellable. The supplier does the
  slow work (uploading a price file, setting terms) while the documents
  are in process, and the day they are verified their shopfront simply
  turns on — rather than facing an empty catalogue on day one of being
  allowed to trade.

  It is also honest in a way a hard gate is not: we never took their
  time on the promise of business we were not going to deliver.

  WHAT ALREADY EXISTS:
    SupplierVerificationStatus  UNVERIFIED / PENDING / VERIFIED, a real
                                column on SupplierProfile.
    src/domains/verification/   scaffolded, empty — this is what it is
                                for.
    settings/supplier-visibility.tsx, storefront.tsx — the screens exist.

  WHAT MUST BE BUILT:
    · document upload and review (Cloudinary is already the media
      provider; the review queue needs the control centre)
    · the offer-visibility rule, enforced in the REPOSITORY layer, not
      in each route. A visibility rule applied per-endpoint is a rule
      that leaks the first time somebody adds an endpoint.
    · the reminder ladder: in-app banner on every supplier screen, an
      email template, and a dismissible-but-returning prompt, until the
      condition is met.

  ON THE REMINDER LADDER — it must be persistent and it must not be
  cruel. The state to communicate is "your shop is ready and nobody can
  see it yet", which is motivating. "You are not verified" is a scolding
  and reads as rejection to someone who has just done an hour of work.

4.6 THE TIMING, WHICH IS STILL GOOD

  Through 2025–2026 foreign-owned spazas closed in the hundreds across
  KwaZulu-Natal, Gauteng and the Western Cape, with new local owners
  taking their place. Bread went from ~R16 to ~R30 in some communities
  as inexperienced operators mispriced.
                       — TechCabal, 30 July 2026

  Meanwhile registration with local municipalities became compulsory for
  food-handling outlets after the 2024 poisonings (deadline extended to
  28 February 2025), and the Spaza Shop Fund additionally requires CIPC,
  SARS and UIF registration.
                       — SAnews; spazashopfund.co.za

  So there is a cohort of new owners who have never run a shop, who owe
  paperwork they have never filed, and who are pricing badly enough that
  their customers noticed. That is a product specification for Discovery
  (V2_05) and for the compliance tool in Account (V2_06) — offered as
  HELP, never as a gate. It has an expiry date: this cohort learns the
  trade with or without us over the next two years.

PART 5 — WHAT EACH TAB IS FOR                                  [REVISED]
===========================================================================

Each tab gets one sentence; anything that does not fit belongs elsewhere.
The first draft's Home lines said "where is my money" for both roles.
That was wrong and is corrected here.

BUSINESS ROLE

  Home        What needs me today, and what should I be reordering.
  Community   Buying: find suppliers, compare, order, and keep the
              relationships I already have.
  Discovery   Learning: how to run this trade better, and what is
              available to me from outside TruConnect.
  Account     The tools I run my business with — whether or not I ever
              buy anything through TruConnect.
  More        Me, my business, my settings.

  WHY NOT "WHERE IS MY MONEY". We do not track this business's money and
  we should not pretend to. There is no till integration, no bank feed,
  no card machine. The only money-shaped number we hold is the Loan
  Book total, and that is SELF-REPORTED — the shopkeeper may record
  every credit sale or a third of them, and may well use the tool for
  something we never imagined. Presenting it as "your money" would dress
  a partial, user-maintained list as a financial position.

  So Home is: what needs a decision today, where the open stock orders
  are, and what the reorder algorithm thinks is due. The Loan Book total
  may appear, labelled as what it is — what your book says you are owed.

SUPPLIER ROLE

  Home        What needs me today, and what did I sell.
  Community   Selling: who buys from me, who could, and what they want
              that I do not stock.
  Discovery   Learning: trade conditions, opportunities, TruConnect news.
  Account     The tools I run my supply business with — catalogue,
              inventory, orders, integrations.
  More        Me, my business, my settings.

  SAME CORRECTION, DIFFERENT SHAPE. We hold no funds and settle nothing.
  What we can state exactly is order activity: orders received, orders
  awaiting delivery, deliveries due today, and the value of orders
  placed — with digitally paid ones distinguished from cash ones,
  because per Part 3.3 only the first are confirmed.

  THE DELIVERY-DATE FLOW, which came out of the review and is a real
  design requirement:

    Payment method CASH        The supplier taps Approve, and a prompt
                               immediately asks for the expected
                               delivery date. There is no payment event
                               to wait for, so the commitment is made at
                               approval.

    Payment method DIGITAL     Approval and payment are separate
                               moments. The expected delivery date is
                               captured AFTER payment is confirmed,
                               because promising a date against money
                               that has not arrived is how a supplier
                               ends up apologising.

  Both then fire automatic in-app messages to the business, and — see
  V2_08 — a WhatsApp utility template, which is the channel this market
  actually reads.

THE TWO COMMUNITY LINES ARE DELIBERATELY NOT MIRRORS. The business
shops; the supplier does not. V2_04 Part 3 is the design.

PART 6 — THE PRINCIPLES THIS PLAN IS BOUND BY
===========================================================================

  1. ASSET-LIGHT, ALWAYS — but brokering is allowed. We never own
     inventory or vehicles. We may ARRANGE a licensed partner's service
     (delivery, VAS, credit) and take a margin. Owning it is what killed
     the cohort in Part 2; arranging it is what the survivors did.

  2. TOOLS STAND ALONE. Every tool works for someone who never places
     an order. (Part 1.)

  3. THE MERCHANT'S DATA IS THE MERCHANT'S. The Loan Book is their
     customer list. We compute on it FOR them; we never show it to a
     supplier and never sell it.

  4. CASH IS RECORDED, DIGITAL IS PROVEN. Both are first-class. Only
     the second can back a financing conversation, and we say so
     plainly rather than manipulating anyone toward it. (Part 3.3.)

  5. VERIFICATION GATES SUPPLIERS, NEVER BUSINESSES. (Part 4.)

  6. NOTHING ASPIRATIONAL ON SCREEN. No star ratings, no delivery-time
     estimates, no counts we cannot stand behind. Silence is the honest
     answer.

  7. THE CATALOGUE IS THE ENGINE. Price comparison, buy-again, demand
     signals and category imagery are all possible only because two
     suppliers' messy line items resolve to one catalogue product.

  8. LOW DATA, WEAK SIGNAL, CHEAP PHONE. Successful apps here are
     "deliberately light and work on weak connections, holding actions
     on the device until signal returns."

PART 7 — THE OPPORTUNITY LAYER                                 [REVISED]
===========================================================================

The first draft filed these under "not building". The review corrected
that: they are opportunities with real revenue and real architecture, and
three of them fit the existing model better than expected. They now have
their own document — V2_08 — and are summarised here only.

  VAS FLOAT (airtime, data, electricity, vouchers)
    Not us becoming a vending provider. The insight from review is
    better: the merchant who resells airtime has to BUY it somewhere,
    and a VAS distributor is simply a supplier whose products are
    digital. Commissions run 3–5% on airtime, 1.5–3% on electricity.
    Fits the marketplace we already have.

  DELIVERY BROKERAGE
    A supplier with no vehicle currently sets offers_delivery=false and
    loses every business that cannot collect. SA has courier APIs
    (Bob Go, Droppa, Pargo, The Courier Guy) built for exactly this.
    We arrange; they drive.

  WHATSAPP AS THE COMMUNICATION LAYER
    Not an ordering channel first — a NOTIFICATION channel. Order
    confirmations and delivery dates as utility templates cost roughly
    R0.14 per message. This reaches the 95–97% of informal trade already
    on WhatsApp without asking anyone to change how they work.

  STOKVEL / GROUP BUYING
    ~800,000 groups, ~11 million members. And the research found the
    model that avoids our biggest objection: some stokvels "save
    directly with the wholesaler, who then records the contributions and
    makes stock available for collection." The supplier holds the money,
    not us.

  DEFERRED PROPERLY: lending on our own balance sheet (no capital, no
  licence, and a conflict of interest); reviews and ratings (rejected
  outright — nothing here can be gamed the way a review score invites).
