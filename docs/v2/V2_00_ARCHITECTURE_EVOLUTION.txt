TRU CONNECT — V2 ARCHITECTURE EVOLUTION
The Supplier Marketplace Architecture

Version: 2.0.0
Status: Architectural Specification
Authority: Subordinate to 00A_TRUEPOINT_PLATFORM_CONSTITUTION
Supersedes: the supplier-role assumptions in 01_TRU_CONNECT_PRODUCT_SPECIFICATION

PURPOSE

This document defines the second architecture of TruConnect.

V1 is complete, working, and tagged. It is not deprecated and it is not a
mistake — it is a correct implementation of an assumption that turned out to be
wrong. This document states that assumption, states why it was wrong, and
defines what replaces it.

Everything here was reached by the same method every other decision in this
codebase was reached by: a problem was found in the current architecture, and
the change is the solution to that problem. Nothing in this document is here
because it is fashionable, because a larger company does it, or because it
would be nice to have. Where a change is speculative, it is labelled
speculative.


===========================================================================
PART 1 — WHY V2 EXISTS
===========================================================================


1.1 THE ASSUMPTION V1 WAS BUILT ON

V1 modelled TruConnect as a two-sided network of PEERS.

A business is a user. A supplier is a user. Both sign up in the app, both have
a profile, both have a Community tab, both search, both invite, both accept or
decline connection requests. The supplier was, structurally, a business that
happens to sell.

Every V1 design decision follows correctly from that assumption:

  - Connection is mutual and must be approved by both sides
  - The supplier sits in the app and taps Accept on incoming orders
  - Products belong to the supplier — there is no shared notion of a product
  - Products are created one at a time in a form, or bulk-imported once
  - The supplier's Community tab mirrors the business's Community tab

The assumption was never written down as an assumption. It was inherited from
the shape of the first screens and then everything grew on top of it.


1.2 WHY IT IS WRONG

A supplier is not a peer of a business. A supplier is SUPPLY-SIDE
INFRASTRUCTURE.

The evidence, in the order it surfaced:

  (a) A real supplier does not sit in an app tapping Accept. A wholesaler
      taking 200 orders a day has no one whose job is to approve them
      individually. Requiring approval makes the platform unusable at exactly
      the scale where it starts to matter.

  (b) A real supplier already has their stock somewhere else — a spreadsheet,
      an accounting package, an ERP. Asking them to retype 3,000 products into
      a phone form is not a friction problem, it is a refusal.

  (c) Informal businesses in South Africa buy from MANY suppliers, and those
      suppliers are scattered and heterogeneous. A model that only fits one
      kind of supplier cannot serve the actual buying behaviour.

  (d) Products belonging to suppliers means the same physical product exists
      as N unrelated rows across N suppliers. Nothing can be compared, and the
      one question a business actually asks — "who sells this cheaper" —
      is unanswerable by construction.

  (e) Accept/decline was never a supplier feature. It is a consequence of CASH.
      A supplier accepts because they are agreeing to release stock against a
      promise to pay. Once money has already moved, there is nothing left to
      accept. V1 encoded a payment-era control as a permanent part of the
      order lifecycle.

Point (e) is the clearest example of the general failure: V1 took a property of
one situation and made it structural.


1.3 WHAT V2 ASSERTS INSTEAD

  A supplier is an entity that can reliably fulfil a repeat stock order for an
  informal or small business.

Four capabilities define one, and none of them is "has the app installed":

  1. RANGE          — carries enough of what a business restocks
  2. FULFILMENT     — can actually get it there, or have it collected
  3. ACCOUNTABILITY — can be held to a price and a delivery
  4. RELEVANCE      — already serves informal businesses, or wants to

TruConnect's job is to accept supply from any of them, in whatever form they
can give it, and present it identically to the buyer.


1.4 THE PROBLEM TRU CONNECT SOLVES, RESTATED

V1 framed the problem as the business's: hard to find suppliers.

V2 frames it as BOTH sides', and the supplier's side is the one that pays:

  - Orders arrive by WhatsApp, phone call, and SMS, in no format, at all hours
  - No record of what was ordered, agreed, or delivered
  - Sales reps are expensive and cover a fraction of the territory
  - No visibility of who is buying, what is selling, or what is running out

The bigger the supplier, the more that chaos costs them. That is the argument
for eventually going upmarket — but see Part 6 for why we do not start there.


===========================================================================
PART 2 — THE CORE ARCHITECTURAL CHANGES
===========================================================================


2.1 PRODUCT AND SUPPLIER OFFER ARE SEPARATE THINGS

THE CHANGE

  V1:  Product  ──belongs to──▶  Supplier
       (name, category, price, sku, unit, stock)

  V2:  Product (catalogue, shared, platform-owned)
         │  name, brand, category, pack size, barcode, image
         │
         └──◀ Supplier Offer (per supplier)
              price, compare-at price, selling unit, sku, stock, archived

WHY

Without this, "who sells Coca-Cola 2L x 6 cheapest" cannot be asked. Two
suppliers' rows for the same physical product have no relationship to each
other. Every comparison, every substitution, every "buy it again from someone
else" is impossible — not hard, impossible.

This is the Amazon ASIN model and the reason it exists is exactly this.

WHAT MAKES IT SHIPPABLE

The Offer's link to a catalogue Product is NULLABLE.

An unmatched Offer behaves exactly like a V1 product. Nothing breaks on the day
the split lands, and the catalogue can be empty. Matching enriches over time.
This is what turns a rewrite into a migration.

PACK SIZE — DECIDED 2026-09-08

Pack size lives on the OFFER. The catalogue Product is the CONSUMER UNIT.

  catalogue.products
    name        Coca-Cola Original
    brand       Coca-Cola
    unit_qty    2
    unit_meas   L
    gtin        5449000054227        consumer-unit barcode

  supplier_offers
    product_id      ──────────▶ exactly one Product
    selling_unit    CASE
    pack_quantity   6
    case_gtin       15449000054224   optional, for scanning and matching
    price_cents     8999

WHY

Comparison is the entire point of the split, and putting pack size on the
Product breaks it in the common case. If "Coke 2L x 6" and "Coke 2L x 12" are
separate Products, a supplier selling 6-packs and one selling 12-packs never
compare — even though the business simply wants Coke 2L cheaply. With pack size
on the Offer, every supplier selling that product maps to ONE Product
regardless of how they pack it, and price-per-unit compares directly.

This also makes the existing `unitEconomics` per-unit price the comparison key,
which is what a restocking buyer actually reads.

THE COST, STATED HONESTLY

Case barcodes (GTIN-14) no longer map 1:1 to Products. They live on the Offer
as an optional second identifier. Matching accepts either: a scanned case
barcode resolves through the Offer, a consumer barcode through the Product.
This is a small amount of special-casing in one place, traded for comparison
working everywhere.


2.2 SUPPLIERS HAVE CAPABILITIES, NOT TIERS

THE CHANGE

There is no SupplierTier enum that drives behaviour. The supplier row carries
independent capability columns. A "tier" is a PRESET that sets them, and no
code branches on tier.

  Axis              Values
  ────────────────  ──────────────────────────────
  ingestion_mode    MANUAL | CSV | API
  accepts_direct_payment    (exists in V1)
  accepts_digital_payment   (exists in V1)
  requires_connection       default FALSE
  auto_accept_orders        derived from payment method

WHY

Because we already made this mistake once and fixed it. V1 carried
`cash_transaction_cap_cents` and `proof_of_payment_transaction_cap_cents` as
two separate caps. On inspection the differentiation was not real, and they
collapsed into a single `direct_transaction_cap_cents`. A tier enum driving
five behaviours is that same bug at five times the size.

The axes correlate but are not identical. A hustler who wants digital-only is
reasonable. A large wholesaler who wants approval for NEW customers only is
reasonable. A single enum makes both unrepresentable, and the first supplier
who does not fit the box arrives in week two.

PRECEDENT IN THE CODEBASE

`accepts_direct_payment` and `accepts_digital_payment` already exist as
independent booleans on supplier_profile. V2 continues a pattern V1 started
without naming.


2.3 THE CONNECTION GATE IS REMOVED

THE CHANGE

Connection stops being a PERMISSION and becomes two separate things:

  - A RELATIONSHIP RECORD, derived from trade. A business is connected to a
    supplier because they have ordered, not because a request was approved.

  - A SUPPLIER-SIDE OPTION (`requires_connection`, default off) for the small
    supplier who genuinely wants to know who is buying.

WHY

  (a) Order accept/decline is a strictly better control than connection
      approval. It is per-transaction, and it carries far more information —
      the supplier sees what is being ordered, how much, and where to, instead
      of approving an abstract future relationship.

  (b) An integrated supplier structurally cannot use it. There is no one
      sitting there to click accept on a connection request.

  (c) It blocks the discovery model entirely. Browsing is already open in V1 —
      `list_supplier_products` and the storefront services both state that only
      ORDERING is gated. The connection requirement is the last thing standing
      between the catalogue and a real discovery feed.

WHAT DIES -- CORRECTED 2026-09-08, AFTER BUILDING IT

The original draft of this section listed the request/approve/decline
flow, the disconnect flow and the contact gate as things that die. That
was wrong, and checking the mobile app is what showed it: the app
actively calls those endpoints. Deleting them would break a shipped
client for a change whose entire purpose is to make the platform more
usable.

What actually died is the word MANDATORY. Nothing was deleted.

  - connection is no longer required to order (requires_connection,
    default FALSE); request, approve, decline and disconnect all still
    work for suppliers who opt in
  - "businesses connected" as a public track-record metric still needs
    replacing -- it is meaningless when most suppliers never use
    connection at all

THE CONTACT AND ADDRESS GATES STAYED, and that is a consent decision
rather than an oversight. A supplier who set share_contact_details=true
was agreeing that CONNECTED businesses could see their number. Quietly
reinterpreting that as "anyone can" changes what they consented to
without asking. It needs a supplier-facing re-consent, not a code
change.

The one gap this leaves is covered: a business ordering for COLLECTION
from a supplier it never connected to still sees the collection address,
because the cart returns it. That exposure is narrow by construction --
it takes a real cart with that supplier, not browsing.

WHAT REPLACES THE CONTACT GATE

Nothing new. `share_contact_details` already exists on supplier_profile as a
supplier-controlled boolean, alongside `photo_visibility` and the other
visibility switches built in V1. The supplier already owns this decision; the
connection gate was a second, redundant lock on the same door.

TRACK RECORD AFTER THE CHANGE

"Businesses connected" is no longer meaningful. The three public numbers become
orders accepted, orders fulfilled, and businesses served — the last derived
from distinct order counterparties rather than from approvals.


2.4 PAYMENT METHOD SELECTS THE ORDER PATH

THE CHANGE

  DIRECT payment (cash, EFT, whatever moves outside the platform)
    → the order is a REQUEST
    → accept / decline is real and necessary
    → spend caps apply

  DIGITAL payment (money moves through the platform)
    → the order is a PURCHASE
    → it goes straight to fulfilment
    → "decline" becomes REFUND + CANCEL, a different action with a real
      financial consequence

WHY

Accept/decline exists because the supplier is releasing stock against an
unsecured promise. That is a property of cash, not of suppliers. When the money
has already moved, an accept step is friction with no purpose — and it is
precisely the friction that makes the platform unusable for a supplier at
volume.

WHY THIS UNIFIES THE ORDER ENGINE

One engine, two paths, selected by a column that already exists
(`stock_orders.payment_method`). A small cash supplier and a large integrated
supplier use the same code and neither knows the other path exists. This is
what makes "support every supplier" tractable rather than a matrix of special
cases.

COMPLIANCE

Larger suppliers must be digital-only. The REASON is not their size — it is
transaction value and volume. Cash at R500,000/month is a money-laundering
exposure; cash at R8,000/month is a spaza owner paying a bakkie driver.

So the rule is threshold-driven, and V1 already built the thresholds:

  Business direct caps    FREE  R1,000/txn   R5,000/month
                          PRO   R2,000/txn   R10,000/month

  Supplier direct caps    FREE  R10,000/month
                          PRO   R25,000/month
                          MAX   R50,000/month
                          warn at 75% and 90%

A supplier growing past the direct ceiling converts to digital-only as a
consequence of the caps, not as a consequence of a tier check. The mechanism
exists; V2 makes the top band a hard stop instead of warn-only.


2.5 INGESTION IS AN ADAPTER; THE OFFER IS CANONICAL

THE CHANGE

  Phone form  ─┐
  CSV / XLSX  ─┼──▶ staging rows ──▶ match to Product ──▶ Supplier Offer
  API / ERP   ─┘                                              │
                                                              ▼
                          storefront · feed · ordering · per-unit price
                            (know nothing about how the Offer arrived)

WHY — THE AIRBNB LESSON

Airbnb did not build a "hotel product" when it moved upmarket. It made a
Listing creatable either by a human filling a form or by a property management
system pushing over an API, and made everything downstream unable to tell the
difference. Search, booking, payouts, reviews: identical.

That is the property V2 needs, stated precisely:

  A Supplier Offer is identical regardless of how it was created.

This is what makes "when a real supplier arrives with their own system, we can
integrate them" true rather than aspirational. The API adapter becomes additive
work — roughly a week GIVEN the staging pipeline — instead of a rebuild.

PRECEDENT IN THE CODEBASE

This is the fourth instance of a pattern V1 already proved three times:
MediaProvider (Cloudinary), PaymentProvider (PayFast/manual), GeocodingProvider
(Mapbox). One interface, several concretes, one composition point, and no
service imports a concrete provider by name.


2.6 BULK IMPORT BECOMES A STAGED, UPSERTING PIPELINE

THE LIVE BUG

V1's importer is CREATE-ONLY. `import_worker.py` calls
`product_service.create_product` per row. A supplier who imports twice gets
duplicates.

This is not a future-architecture concern. A supplier above hustler scale sends
a PRICE FILE EVERY WEEK. The one-time import is the exception; the recurring
update is the relationship. The importer as built structurally cannot serve a
repeat supplier, and that is a lifecycle limit, not a size limit.

THE SECOND PROBLEM

V1 requires a fixed template: name, category, price, unit. A supplier with
3,000 rows already has a spreadsheet with THEIR column names — Item Code,
Description, Pack, Excl Price, SOH. Telling them to rename columns loses the
supplier at the door.

THE PIPELINE

  Stage              What happens                              V1 status
  ─────────────────  ────────────────────────────────────────  ──────────
  1 Upload           file stored, ImportJob created            EXISTS
  2 Profile          detect headers, PROPOSE A COLUMN MAP,     NEW
                     supplier confirms
  3 Parse+validate   row → ImportRow staging table             parser EXISTS
  4 Match            row → catalogue Product, with confidence  NEW
  5 Preview          "2,847 ready · 112 review · 41 errors"    error rows EXIST
  6 Commit           UPSERT Offers by (supplier, supplier_sku) NEW
  7 Report           per-row outcome, downloadable             EXISTS

WHAT SURVIVES UNTOUCHED

The parsers, the zip-bomb and spreadsheet-bomb defences, the row validator that
mirrors the frontend field-for-field, abuse tracking, rate limits, and the
no-deploy kill switch. This is good work and V2 keeps all of it. What changes
is that the worker stages instead of creating.

THE DELETION SEMANTICS PROBLEM

A row that vanishes from this week's file means either "delisted" or "just not
included". Guessing wrong silently deletes a supplier's catalogue. This must be
an explicit per-supplier setting (FULL_CATALOGUE vs PARTIAL_UPDATE), never
inferred.

THE PAYOFF

An API push from an ERP lands in the SAME staging table at stage 3, skipping
1 and 2. Stages 3-7 are shared. Same validation, same matching, same preview,
same upsert.


2.7 THE CATALOGUE AGENT

WHAT IT IS

Claude, called over HTTPS from the Flask backend, behind a provider abstraction
exactly like Cloudinary and PayFast. There is no separate product to adopt and
no new infrastructure. `src/shared/catalogue_ai/providers/` with an ABC, one
concrete implementation, one composition point, and a fake for tests.

TWO JOBS, DIFFERENT FAILURE COSTS, DIFFERENT DESIGNS

  Job A — NORMALISE AND PROPOSE (high volume, cheap)
    Extract structured fields from a raw supplier row.
    Model: Claude Haiku 4.5. This is extraction, not judgement.
    Batch API (50% cost) — a CSV import is not latency-sensitive.
    Prompt caching on the taxonomy prefix — identical across all rows.
    strict:true tool schema — a schema violation here is silent catalogue
    corruption, so the output must always parse.

  Job B — ADJUDICATE (low volume, expensive)
    "Is this supplier's row the same Product as this catalogue entry?"
    Model: Claude Opus 5, adaptive thinking, high effort.
    Vision where images exist on both sides — as CORROBORATION only, never
    the primary key. Two pack sizes of the same drink look identical.
    Output is a recommendation with a confidence and a reason. Never an action.

CANDIDATE SEARCH IS DETERMINISTIC, NOT MODEL-DRIVEN

Barcode exact → brand + normalised name trigram → embedding. It must be
reproducible and explainable to a supplier who disputes a match.

THE CONFIDENCE GATE — NON-NEGOTIABLE

  Exact barcode        auto-link, no model call
  High confidence      auto-link, logged, reversible
  Middle band          HUMAN REVIEW QUEUE
  Low confidence       create new Product, flag possible duplicate

A wrong match is worse than no match. No match means an Offer sits alone. A
wrong match means TruConnect tells a business "R89 here, R74 there" about two
different things — the product breaking its core promise. The agent never
decides autonomously in the middle band, and every auto-link is reversible with
an audit row.

HIGHEST-LEVERAGE FIRST

Column-header mapping (stage 2) is ONE CALL PER FILE, not per row. It costs a
fraction of a cent and it is the difference between a supplier onboarding
themselves and a supplier giving up. Build it before per-row extraction —
better return, a tenth of the complexity.

MEASURED COST (per 4,000-product supplier onboarding)

  Extraction   Haiku 4.5, cached, batched          ~$2.40
  Adjudication Opus 5, ~10% of rows                ~$9.00
  TOTAL                                            ~$11  (~R200)

One-time per supplier. Price updates re-run only changed rows. R200 to onboard
a supplier's entire catalogue is a customer-acquisition cost, and it is the
number that makes supply-side onboarding cheap enough for this architecture to
be viable at all.

THE CATALOGUE-FIXING AGENT

The same machinery pointed backwards, on a schedule (V1's master scheduler
already exists for exactly this shape of job): near-duplicate Products, Offers
whose price-per-unit is a wild outlier for their Product (usually a pack-size
extraction error), Products with no Offers.


2.8 IMAGES ARE A CASCADE, NOT A FIELD

THE CHANGE

  offer.image_url        supplier uploaded
    ↓ null
  product.image_url      catalogue, barcode-enriched
    ↓ null
  brand.mark_url         ~200 marks cover most of SA FMCG
    ↓ null
  category.image_url     16 images. Always present. Never blank.

WHY

A tile never renders empty. A supplier who opens an account and uploads nothing
but names and prices still has a storefront that looks deliberate. Every image
that arrives later is an UPGRADE, not a fix — which means image sourcing stops
being a blocker and becomes background work.

THE RULE

  An image is an enrichment, never a gate.

A Product exists the moment it has a name, brand, pack size and barcode. If the
catalogue cannot launch imageless, image sourcing blocks the whole
architecture — and it must not.

WHY THIS IS ENOUGH

B2B restock is not consumer e-commerce. Consumer shopping is browse-driven, so
the photograph does the selling. Restocking is search-driven: the buyer already
knows what they want and is checking price per unit, pack size, and stock. The
image's job is RECOGNITION AND SCANNING SPEED, not persuasion.

A brand mark over a category-tinted background, with name, pack size, and
price-per-unit, is denser and faster to scan than a photograph — and needs zero
product photography.

WHERE IMAGES ACTUALLY COME FROM

  Tier              Source                            Count      When
  ────────────────  ────────────────────────────────  ─────────  ──────────
  Category          Licensed (Unsplash/Pexels) or     16         NOW
                    commissioned and owned
  Brand mark        Brand press kits + supplier       ~200       Early
  Product photo     Supplier upload, rights           unbounded  Ongoing
                    WARRANTED IN TERMS
  Barcode           Open Food Facts (CC-BY-SA)        free wins  Background
  Bulk              Anchor supplier integration       thousands  The multiplier

WHAT WE DO NOT DO

Bulk-scrape the internet. Not primarily for legal reasons — because it produces
a visually incoherent catalogue. Scraped images arrive at different sizes,
crops, backgrounds, and watermarks, and a rail of them looks broken. Watermarks
on stock imagery ARE the paywall, not an obstacle to route around. The legal
exposure is real but it is the second problem, not the first.

HOW THE COMPARABLES DO IT

Uber Eats: merchant uploads, plus a PAID photography programme, plus master
assets from franchise brands, plus — critically — a large fraction of items
with NO PHOTO AT ALL. They did not solve the image problem; they designed for
it not being solved. Sixty60 uses Shoprite's in-house studio. Takealot is
seller-uploaded plus own-stock studio. Everyone with good images either paid a
photographer or got it from the brand. There is no trick.

Supplier-uploaded with a rights warranty is what every marketplace does —
Takealot, Amazon and Shopify do not own their product images either.


2.9 THE WEB IS THE PLATFORM SURFACE; THE APP IS THE DAILY SURFACE

THE CHANGE

`frontend/web/` is not a supplier tool. It is the whole TruConnect web
presence, and it was always intended to be:

  - Public landing page and marketing
  - "For Suppliers" — sign up, sign in, and the supplier portal
  - The TruConnect admin control centre, hidden from the public
    (private network or equivalent)
  - Future: a lender surface

THE SPLIT IS BY TASK, NOT BY SUPPLIER SIZE

An earlier draft of this section said the app remains "for the smallest
suppliers". That was wrong — it tiered by supplier, which is exactly the
mistake 2.2 exists to prevent. The real division is what the work IS:

  WEB — SETUP AND DESK WORK, done once or periodically
    Catalogue management at volume, import and column mapping, the weekly
    price file, API credentials, analytics, account configuration.
    Requires a keyboard, a wide screen, and a file picker. No amount of
    mobile design makes bulk catalogue management good on a phone.

  APP — DAILY OPERATIONS, done continuously
    Accepting and declining cash orders, connection decisions where the
    supplier has turned that on, notifications, checking an order on the
    move.

A supplier of ANY size uses both. A hustler with 30 products still sets up on
the web if they prefer a keyboard; a large wholesaler still wants order
notifications on a phone. Size correlates with which surface dominates, but it
does not determine access.

WHY THE APP KEEPS A SUPPLIER ROLE AT ALL

Because suppliers are heterogeneous, and the daily actions are genuinely
mobile: a cash order needs a decision now, wherever the person is. Moving all
supplier work to the web would make the platform unusable exactly where it is
most time-sensitive.

BUSINESSES HAVE NO WEB SURFACE

Deliberately, and it is not an oversight. An informal business has their phone
on them all day; that IS their computer. Their problem does not require a desk.
Building a business web app would be building for a need that does not exist.

WHAT STAYS THE SAME IN THE APP

Sign up and sign in are unchanged. Home, Account (business tools live here),
Community, Discovery, and the More tab all stay. Notifications stay. Stock
orders stay, with modification per 2.4.

BUILD ORDER FOR THE WEB

Landing page first. It is pure frontend, has no backend coupling, and is
cheap to change later — so it does not need to be right on the first pass.
Then the "For Suppliers" route from the footer into sign up / sign in, reusing
the existing auth domain. Then the portal dashboard, which is where the real
work lives.

Note the coupling: the landing page and auth pages touch NOTHING in the
product model, so they can be built before the Part 8 rename. The catalogue
and import screens cannot — they must wait for it.


2.10 AUTHENTICATION SPLITS THREE WAYS

THE CHANGE

  Who                        What it is                Mechanism
  ─────────────────────────  ────────────────────────  ──────────────────────
  Supplier on web portal     human, first-party,       httpOnly Secure
                             browser                   SameSite COOKIE
  Mobile app                 human, first-party,       JWT bearer
                             native                    (UNCHANGED)
  Supplier's ERP             machine, no human         API key, or OAuth
                                                       client_credentials

WHAT WE ARE NOT BUILDING

A full OAuth2 / OIDC authorization-code + PKCE identity provider.

That flow exists for when a THIRD PARTY logs users in via YOUR identity
provider. The supplier portal is first-party — our subdomain, our backend, our
users table. Running the full dance against ourselves adds an IdP we would have
to build and secure, and buys nothing.

PKCE specifically exists for PUBLIC clients that cannot keep a secret — mobile
apps and browser SPAs. An ERP server CAN keep a secret, so the integration path
is client_credentials or scoped API keys (what Stripe, Shopify and Takealot
actually hand out), never authorization-code + PKCE.

If a genuine third-party developer ecosystem ever appears, run Keycloak or Ory.
Hand-rolling an OIDC provider in Flask is a months-long security-critical
project whose failure mode is a breach, not a bug.

WHAT ACTUALLY CHANGES IN THE AUTH DOMAIN

  - Token transport: a browser must not hold a JWT in localStorage (XSS reads
    it). The same backend issues credentials two ways depending on client.
  - CSRF protection: required the moment cookies are used. Bearer tokens do not
    need it. This is new code.
  - Session lifetime: a supplier keeps a portal tab open all day; mobile
    refresh cadence does not fit.
  - Role routing: web login must reject `business` and redirect to the app.
  - API keys: a new table — hashed at rest, scoped, revocable, last-used
    tracked. This is the ERP door and it does not exist.

UNCHANGED: sign-up, OTP, verification, lockout, rate limiting.


2.11 COMMUNITY IS REDEFINED PER ROLE

The two roles diverge completely. One tab name, NO shared components below the
tab bar. Trying to share them is what produced the generic-admin-list feel in
V1.

BUSINESS COMMUNITY — my trading network, and finding more of it

Not a social network. Three things only: see who I buy from, discover who else
I could buy from, get back to buying.

  Section              Real source
  ───────────────────  ─────────────────────────────────────
  Category chips       16 categories + category descriptions
  Your suppliers       distinct suppliers from order history
  Buy it again         line items from delivered orders
  On sale now          cross-supplier variant of list_on_sale
  New on TruConnect   recently verified supplier profiles
  Near you             supplier area — BLOCKED, field may not exist

Two rules carried from the storefront work, both learned the hard way:
  - Rank in SQL, not Python. Python sorting breaks the moment it paginates.
  - A thin rail is a broken rail. Under 3 items, the section does not render.
  - Unknown section types render nothing, so the server can add a section
    without an app release.

SUPPLIER COMMUNITY — who buys from me

CRM-lite. My customers sorted by recency or value, one customer's order history
with this supplier, and search + invite unchanged.

NO "FOR YOU" FEED. A supplier has nothing to discover here. Leave the space
empty rather than inventing content — the same "silence is the honest answer"
rule the track record already follows.


2.12 THE BUSINESS SIDE EXTENDS TO SMALL BUSINESS

Structurally the smallest change in V2, stated plainly so it does not consume
planning time.

A small business is an informal business with a bank account, possibly VAT
registration, and higher volume. What changes:

  - Spend caps — the tier mechanism already exists. New tier, same code.
  - Verification — a registered business can prove more, so it can be trusted
    with more.
  - Invoice / PO — the one genuinely new feature, and only if asked for.

The buyer UX does not fork. There is no "small business mode".


===========================================================================
PART 3 — WHAT V2 EXPLICITLY DOES NOT BUILD YET
===========================================================================

Recorded so that these are decisions rather than omissions.

  PRODUCT-FIRST SEARCH AND PRICE COMPARISON UI
  The Product/Offer split earns its keep on the SUPPLY side — onboarding a
  supplier's 3,000-row file against a shared catalogue — long before it shows
  up in the buyer's UI. Building comparison UI now means building it on a
  catalogue without enough overlapping offers to compare. Search stays
  supplier-name-first, because a business searches for suppliers they already
  know: they have bonds, discounts, and history.

  THE API / ERP ADAPTER
  Built when a real supplier asks, not before. It is roughly a week of work
  GIVEN the staging pipeline, and zero weeks of guessing what their ERP looks
  like. The property in 2.5 is what buys the right to defer it.

  A PHOTOGRAPHY PROGRAMME
  The Uber Eats move. Later, and only for anchor suppliers, as a sales
  sweetener.

  LENDING
  Already in the roadmap. Unchanged by V2, but note that the Offer/Product
  split and real order history are what make underwriting possible later.

PART 4 — WHAT V1 GOT RIGHT AND V2 KEEPS
===========================================================================

Listed because a version boundary invites unnecessary rewriting, and most of
this codebase does not need to change.

  - The domain structure itself. api / services / repositories / models /
    schemas per domain, with enforced isolation tested by AST parsing.
  - The provider abstraction pattern. Proven three times; V2 adds a fourth.
  - The master scheduler. Jobs live in domains; the scheduler defines none.
  - The AppError hierarchy and the response envelope.
  - Tri-state booleans — null is not false.
  - The storefront services. They already say browsing is open and only
    ordering is gated; they need almost nothing.
  - The bulk import defences — zip bombs, spreadsheet bombs, abuse tracking,
    the kill switch.
  - Long narrative comments recording past bugs and rejected alternatives.
    This is why V2 could be planned at all: the reasoning was still readable.
  - The payment rails, spend caps, and DUE-vs-SUCCEEDED distinction. V2 builds
    directly on them.
  - The community feed contract and the six storefront sections. Server-driven,
    already shipped, and the correct foundation.

PART 5 — MARKET SEQUENCE
===========================================================================

  BUYERS:    informal business  →  small business
  SUPPLIERS: individual/hustler →  small  →  medium  →  large

WHY SMALL SUPPLIERS FIRST — this is a marketing decision, not a technical one

Medium and large suppliers are strict about data and will not join at an early
stage. Small suppliers will. And at this stage WE NEED THEM MORE THAN THEY NEED
US — they arrive with few products, tolerate rough edges, and are reachable.

They also happen to be the ones for whom cash and direct payment are
appropriate, which is why 2.4 keeps that path first-class rather than treating
it as legacy.

THE TENSION, STATED HONESTLY

V2 builds the hardest architecture — Offer/Product split, staging, upsert,
API-ready ingestion — for suppliers who will not arrive for a year, while
shipping for suppliers who need none of it.

That is deliberate, and it is the Airbnb sequence exactly: build the Listing
abstraction while you still only have air mattresses, because retrofitting it
across 100,000 listings is impossible.

The discipline is: BUILD THE ABSTRACTION, NOT THE FEATURES.

PART 6 — OPEN DECISIONS
===========================================================================

These are not yet decided. They are recorded here so they are decided
deliberately rather than by accident in a pull request.

  1. PACK SIZE PLACEMENT — RESOLVED 2026-09-08.
     On the Offer; the Product is the consumer unit. See 2.1. Step 2 of the
     migration is unblocked.

  2. INVENTORY OWNERSHIP for integrated suppliers.
     Does stock live in our table, or is it a mirror of their ERP?
     Proposal: same table, plus `stock_source` (MANUAL | IMPORT | API) and
     `synced_at`. Stock stays a number internally; in/out is what is exposed;
     be tolerant on integration.

  3. DOES THE SUPPLIER APP KEEP DISCOVERY?
     Discovery is independent of the core logic — news, opportunities, videos,
     an ingestion engine. Probably yes, but unexamined.

  4. "NEAR YOU" FEED SECTION
     Requires a supplier area/region field that may not exist. Verify before
     planning the section.

  5. FULFILMENT / SETTLEMENT STATE
     "Orders fulfilled" is a public track-record number with no data source
     today. Needs a real fulfilment state on the order.

  6. DELETION SEMANTICS DEFAULT for price files (2.6). FULL_CATALOGUE or
     PARTIAL_UPDATE as the default for a new supplier?

PART 7 — THE METHOD
===========================================================================

Recorded because it is the reason this document is trustworthy, and because it
is the standard the V2 work must be held to.

Nothing in TruConnect was wired because it was good. Every piece exists
because a problem was found in the current architecture and this was the
solution.

V2 follows the same rule. Each change in Part 2 names the problem first:

  2.1  products cannot be compared across suppliers
  2.2  a tier enum makes real suppliers unrepresentable
  2.3  connection approval blocks discovery and cannot scale
  2.4  accept/decline is a cash-era control encoded as permanent
  2.5  ingestion shape leaks into everything downstream
  2.6  the importer is create-only and breaks on the second import
  2.7  matching by hand does not scale; matching wrongly is worse than not
  2.8  the catalogue cannot launch if images gate it
  2.9  bulk catalogue work is desk work
  2.10 a browser must not hold a bearer token
  2.11 one Community for two roles served neither
  2.12 the buyer model was drawn too narrowly

Where a change has no problem statement, it does not belong in V2.
