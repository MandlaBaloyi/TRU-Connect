===========================================================================
PART 1 — WHY THIS TAB DECIDES WHETHER TRUCONNECT SURVIVES
===========================================================================

The African B2B cohort died partly because "small retailers cycled between
platforms seeking better offers, creating a race-to-the-bottom risk with
no sticky customer relationships" (Rest of World, 2023).

Nothing in a marketplace prevents that. A better price is one download
away, and a supplier list is not a possession.

What prevents it is a merchant having something HERE that exists nowhere
else and would cost them evenings to recreate: months of who owes them
money, what they sold, what they ordered and what they paid. That is the
Account tab.

  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Community is why they install it.
  Account is why they still have it installed in a year.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The research names the same thing from the merchant's side. What brings
them back is "their own prices, the schemes they qualify for, what they
owe, and where the last order is". Three of those four are records, not
listings.

And there is a second-order effect, though it needs stating more
carefully than the first draft did. Financial institutions do not lend to
informal retailers because they "mostly trade in cash and have no
financial footprint to determine their creditworthiness" (BFA Global).

BUT ONLY ONE OF THE THREE LEDGERS IS EVIDENCE. Loan Book and Order Book
are the merchant's own record-keeping — we cannot verify a line of
either, and we should never present them to a third party as though we
could. Digitally paid STOCK ORDERS are different: the platform observed
them and the money actually moved. See V2_03 Part 3.3, which is the full
argument.

So the honest version is: we are not a lender and should not become one,
and the record that could one day make a merchant lendable is built from
their digital order history — not from their books. The books are why
they stay. The digital orders are what a partner could underwrite.

PART 2 — WHAT IS ALREADY BUILT
===========================================================================

  THREE LEDGERS, AND THE FIRST DRAFT CONFUSED TWO OF THEM. Corrected
  here; see V2_03 Part 3.2.

  LOAN BOOK      business/loan-book/ — customers, entries, payments,
                 history, analytics, trash.
                 "Who owes ME money." The neighbour who took bread on
                 credit. SELF-REPORTED.

  ORDER BOOK     business/order-book/ — customers, orders, balances,
                 history, performance, auto-clear.
                 "What MY OWN CUSTOMERS ordered from me." The kota
                 seller's order list. Its types file says it outright:
                 "A customer of THIS business." SELF-REPORTED, and it
                 has nothing to do with suppliers.

  STOCK ORDERS   business/orders/ + checkout.stock_orders.
                 "What I ordered FROM A SUPPLIER through TruConnect."
                 PLATFORM-OBSERVED, and when paid digitally, PROVEN —
                 which is what makes it the only one of the three that
                 can ever back a financing conversation (V2_03 3.3).

  All three exist and work. The two self-reported ones are tiles in the
  Account tab; the platform-observed one is reachable only from the cart
  and an overflow menu, so the business has two tools in a toolbox and a
  third hidden in a drawer.

  SUPPLIER SIDE  products, inventory, stock orders, bulk import,
                 storefront. Also real.

The Account tab is therefore not a green field. It is a tab with three
good tools, one of which is not in it.


===========================================================================
PART 3 — THE BUSINESS TOOLS, PROPOSED
===========================================================================

Ordered by (value to the merchant) ÷ (cost to build). Each is judged
against a single question: DOES A SHOPKEEPER LOSE SOMETHING REAL IF THEY
DELETE THE APP?

3.1 PUT STOCK ORDERS IN THE ACCOUNT TAB            ← do this first
    ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    Not a new tool. A built one that is not where anyone would look.

      · Stock Orders becomes a third tile beside Loan Book and Order
        Book. Same screens, one route.
      · Open stock orders appear on Home (V2_05).
      · The Loan Book total appears on Home, LABELLED AS WHAT IT IS —
        what your book says you are owed, not "your money".

    AND AN EXPLICIT NON-GOAL, because the first draft proposed it and
    it was wrong: a stock order must NOT write itself into the Order
    Book. Order Book records what this business's OWN CUSTOMERS ordered
    from IT. A crate of cooldrink bought from a supplier is not a
    customer order, and auto-filing it there would corrupt the one
    ledger the merchant maintains by hand. Three ledgers, three
    purposes, no cross-writing.

    Cheapest work in this document and the highest return, because it
    puts all three ledgers in one place. Nothing new to learn, nothing
    new to build, one route to add.

3.2 CASH UP                                         ← the biggest gap
    ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    The research is blunt: most spaza owners "do not maintain formal
    stock records and buy stock as and when needed", and "cash-only
    trading and manual stock records are the two biggest constraints.
    If you can't see your margins, you can't negotiate or borrow."

    So: one screen, once a day, three numbers.

        money in the till at open
        takings today
        money paid out today (stock, transport, wages)

    That is it. No categories, no accounting, no double entry. It takes
    under a minute at closing time and it produces, over eight weeks,
    the first picture of the business the owner has ever had — and the
    beginning of the financial footprint from Part 1.

    THE DESIGN RISK IS ASKING FOR TOO MUCH. Every abandoned bookkeeping
    app in this market asked for one more field. Three numbers. If it
    cannot be done standing up, holding a phone, in a shop, it will not
    be done at all.

3.3 WHAT I PAY                                      ← catalogue-powered
    ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    A price history of what THIS business actually paid, per catalogue
    product, across suppliers and over time. "You paid R64, R66, R71 for
    2kg maize meal over three months."

    Their own data, so no supplier-relations problem at all (contrast
    V2_04 Part 2.4). And it is the single most direct answer to "their
    own prices" from the retention research.

    Only possible because matching resolves different suppliers' line
    items to one catalogue product. Without the catalogue this is three
    unrelated strings.

3.4 COMPLIANCE FOLDER                               ← timely, and dated
    ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    Municipal registration is now compulsory; the Spaza Shop Fund
    additionally requires CIPC, SARS and UIF. Health inspections cover
    storage, expiry dates and fridge temperatures.

    A simple, honest tool: a checklist of what this business needs,
    somewhere to keep photographs of the certificates, and expiry
    reminders.

    WHAT IT MUST NOT DO IS GIVE LEGAL ADVICE OR IMPLY WE FILE ANYTHING.
    Requirements differ by municipality and change. It links to the
    official source and it reminds. That is a genuinely useful thing to
    be, and pretending to be more would be the kind of claim that ends
    badly.

3.5 SHELF LIST (light stock)                        ← after MVP
    ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    Not inventory management. A list of what this shop sells, with a
    low-stock flag the owner sets by eye. It feeds "time to reorder"
    and pre-fills baskets.

    Full inventory has been tried on this market repeatedly and is
    abandoned repeatedly, because counting stock is work and the payoff
    is invisible. A shelf list with a reorder button has a payoff the
    same afternoon.

3.6 BROKERED SERVICES — NOT EVERY TOOL IS CRUD      ← see V2_08
    ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    A correction to this document's original framing, from review: the
    Account tab must not be limited to screens that create, read,
    update and delete our own rows.

    Two examples that belong here and are designed in V2_08:

      BUSINESS   Buy airtime, data, electricity and voucher stock —
                 the float a merchant resells over the counter. A VAS
                 distributor is simply a supplier whose products are
                 digital, so most of the machinery already exists.
                 Commissions run 3–5% on airtime, 1.5–3% on electricity.
                 It is also the tool that brings someone into the app
                 daily rather than weekly.

      SUPPLIER   Book a third-party courier for a delivery they cannot
                 make themselves, instead of setting offers_delivery to
                 false and losing every buyer who cannot collect.

    THE RULE THAT KEEPS THIS SAFE: we arrange, a licensed partner
    performs. We never hold the float, never own the vehicle, never
    carry the credit. That is the distinction between the survivors of
    the 2022–24 B2B collapse and the casualties.

3.7 GROUP BUY / STOKVEL                             ← after MVP, real
    ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    ~800,000 stokvels, ~11 million members, >R50bn, already buying
    groceries in bulk 20–30% below retail. Several shops combining to
    clear a wholesaler's minimum is the same mechanic they already run
    informally, and it directly attacks the documented problem that
    spazas "lack the relationships that enable larger shops to enjoy
    bulk discounts".

    It is also genuinely hard: who pays, who collects, what happens when
    one member drops out, who bears the loss. Design it properly or not
    at all — a group-buy feature that leaves someone out of pocket
    destroys more trust than it creates. Not MVP. Worth a document of
    its own when its turn comes.

PART 5 — THE MORE TAB
===========================================================================

More is settings and identity: My Account, My Business, Account Security,
Settings. It is already the most complete area of the app — roughly forty
screens across both roles, and genuinely good.

TWO THINGS TO DO, BOTH SMALL.

  1  AUDIT FOR SCREENS THAT PROMISE SOMETHING UNBUILT. Forty settings
     screens written ahead of the features they configure will contain
     toggles that control nothing. A setting that does not work is a
     small, repeated lie, and this is the tab where a careful person
     goes looking. One pass, list them, hide or wire each.

  2  NOTIFICATION SETTINGS MUST COVER THE NEW SURFACES. There are
     already notifications-community, notifications-discovery,
     notifications-orders, notifications-announcements screens. Reorder
     reminders, price alerts and demand-gap prompts are new categories
     of interruption and each needs its own switch — a merchant who
     cannot turn off a daily prompt turns off the app.

NOTHING ELSE. More does not need features; it needs to be true.


===========================================================================
PART 6 — WHAT WE OWE THE MERCHANT, WRITTEN DOWN
===========================================================================

The Loan Book contains the names of a shopkeeper's neighbours and what
each of them owes. It is the most sensitive data in this product by a
wide margin, more sensitive than payment details.

  IT IS NEVER SHOWN TO A SUPPLIER. Not aggregated, not anonymised, not
  "as a creditworthiness signal". Not ever.

  IT IS NEVER SOLD, and no analytics product is built on it.

  IT IS EXPORTABLE BY THE MERCHANT, on request, in a format they can
  keep. If the moat is that their records live here, then the honest
  version of that bargain is that they can walk out with them. A moat
  built on lock-out rather than on being useful is one bad month from
  collapsing anyway.

  IT IS DELETED WITH THE ACCOUNT, properly — noting that the FK audit
  done on 2026-09-08 found supplier and business rows are referenced by
  NO ACTION foreign keys across ten tables, so "delete my account" does
  not currently work cleanly. That is a real gap with a legal edge
  (POPIA) and it should be scheduled, not discovered.
